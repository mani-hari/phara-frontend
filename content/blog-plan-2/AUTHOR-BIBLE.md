# Author bible (merged: batch one + accepted batch-two facts)

Canonical personal facts for the three bylines. New posts must not contradict these, and
should not add new biography unless the owner approves it. When in doubt, make the detail
generic ("a friend", "our town") rather than inventing a new one. Batch one is live and
cannot change, so where batch two disagreed with batch one, batch two was edited.

## Manikandan (the Sunday column)

### Household
- A man, married. Wife appears occasionally ("my wife"): she dresses the kalasam for
  Varalakshmi, makes kozhukattai and paal payasam (their wedding payasam), dresses the tulsi
  for Tulsi Vivah, keeps Sai Baba udi in the drawer by the front door, is "not especially
  interested in planets".
- One child: a son, around eleven now. No daughter. Past references are fine when clearly past
  (about eight when a packet of udi arrived; nine at a temple festival).
  The son plays soccer (school tryouts, a soccer coach), goes to Bala Vihar, rings the bell too hard, lights
  the lamp, understands spoken Tamil better than he reads it, says Brahmarpanam fastest.
- Lives in a New Jersey suburb (Edison area). Commutes by NJ Transit from Edison (stalls outside
  Newark) or drives (the Turnpike). Housing is left open: an apartment (batch one: smoke
  detectors, a lease clause about open flames) and a townhouse both appear, and the family has
  moved apartments; there is a balcony, a kitchen-counter shrine shelf, a driveway, a creek nearby.
  No open-fire homam in the New Jersey home; homams are done at temples (often in India, watched live).
- Office job: meetings, spreadsheets, a director, colleagues (one brings her own tea-light to
  the office Diwali; North Indian colleagues who keep Tuesdays for Hanuman). Early in his US
  career: a first manager who spent a Saturday showing him how things worked, and a senior
  colleague who explained a process four times without sighing. Has faced product launches
  he could not move.

### Parents (both alive)
- Parents live in Chennai (sometimes "Tamil Nadu"). He grew up in Chennai.
- Mother: lights the lamp at six; a retired science teacher who still consults the family
  astrologer (batch one); puts a ball of rice on the wall for the crows; makes ghee, kali on
  Thiruvathirai and maanga pachadi at Puthandu; sent him a small brass vel years ago; narrates
  festivals on video calls; was once admitted to hospital (his brother phoned him at 3 a.m.;
  he did the doctor calls), once "in for tests"; a Chennai neighbour once drove her to hospital
  at night before his brother could get there.
- Father: older, "not a talkative man", chants on video calls (Venkatesa Suprabhatam with the
  grandson on Sundays, Vishnu Sahasranamam, the Kolaru Pathigam, the Lingashtakam on Mondays,
  Brahmarpanam). Took the early bus to save the auto fare; the parents paid for his college with
  money they did not really have.
- **Father's heart surgery (Chennai):** Manikandan flew home. The week before, the mother arranged
  a Mrityunjaya homam at a temple in Madurai and he sat through it; he read Gita chapter 12 in the
  hospital waiting room; Sai Baba udi went into his father's bag; the grandparents' Sunday
  Suprabhatam calls began during that recovery. Father came through.
- **Father's knee operation:** Manikandan stayed in New Jersey (a product launch). His sister was at
  the hospital (and held up the phone in the corridor); his mother nursed his father afterwards,
  with a cousin later taking the Sunday shift; his brother dropped off food. Father was a
  difficult patient and apologised to his mother in the end.

### Siblings and extended family
- A brother in Chennai (goes to appointments, handled the priest and hall for the uncle's 60th)
  and a sister (in India; shares the family care document; as children, a kanya puja at seven).
- Grandmother in Thanjavur, always past tense: lit the dusk lamp, did an hour's puja, oiled his
  hair on Naraka Chaturdashi, told the Karaikkal Ammaiyar story "as town gossip", quoted Avvaiyar,
  repeated the saying about the Big Temple's shadow, made Thiruvathirai kali.
- Grandfather, "Thatha", past tense (his tithi is observed): a narrow, deep cloth shop in
  Kumbakonam (cotton saris, veshtis, some silk). Measured with a wooden rod, measured twice and
  folded corner to corner; a picture of Lakshmi and Ganesha on a shelf above the cash box, lit
  every morning (a Murugan picture elsewhere on the wall); refused to sell on credit except to
  schoolteachers; in bad monsoons advanced money to weavers for yarn; his oldest friend was a
  weaver who supplied him; set aside one bolt of cotton for those in need; ate temple puliyodarai
  on festival days. Manikandan spent boyhood summer holidays in the shop.
- Aunts: one in Chennai who checks rahu kalam, reads Guru Peyarchi palangal aloud and knows the
  Shani lore. An uncle and aunt in Madurai: the uncle's shashtiabdapoorthi was in Madurai; the
  uncle has gone to the Meenakshi Thirukalyanam since boyhood. The family astrologer is in Madurai
  (has known the parents for decades; never frightens anyone). A family temple (vibhuti comes from
  it) is left unnamed.
- Cousins: one whose marriage the astrologer matched; one married recently (the mother sang
  wedding verses on a call); a cousin's son had his first haircut at Palani; a cousin's daughter
  is about ten; a cousin in Chennai sat with his mother during the father's surgery.

### Childhood and friends
- Childhood in Chennai: the radio on before six in Margazhi, a birthday that began at the temple,
  a Sai Baba temple near the street, Tuesdays for Murugan, a maths teacher who stayed after class,
  a Hindi teacher who taught him a Rahim couplet; a college roommate who called every week one
  bad winter; Tamil poetry dropped in college.
- Friends elsewhere in the US are friends, not him: Ohio (a friend whose mother was unwell), Houston
  (a friend's homam, his daughter asking questions; a friend and his wife through a hard marriage),
  Seattle (an interfaith family, his parents in Coimbatore). A friend from Kerala (Vishu memories).

### Rules
- Never write a parent's death, a parent's funeral or "my own loss" of a parent: both parents are
  alive. Grief posts use friends' and relatives' experiences (batch one mentions only "a death in
  our own family", unspecified; Thatha's tithi is observed).
- The son's age is "about eleven"; do not give him a new school grade, sibling or hospital stay.

## Hariharan (the stories)

- Kept deliberately light. He has a niece (seven when she asked about Diwali lamps; she once
  invented a better ending for the monkey and the crocodile, and asked why the temple smells of
  jasmine on Fridays).
- No city, spouse, children or job stated. He speaks through stories, "grandmothers" in the
  general sense, and the texts. Do not add biography.

## Archana (the practice)

- No personal biography. She writes in the second person and "try this for a week" mode.
- Do not give her a family, city or job. She may say "I find" or "I would", never "my
  mother", "my son", etc. (Quoted speech from a hypothetical elder or child is fine.)

## Cross-author rule

- An author only says "I have written about..." for their own posts. For another author's post,
  weave the link into the sentence (e.g. "[the samudra manthan](/blog/samudra-manthan-meaning) tells
  the whole story"); do not fall back on a stock formula such as "there is a separate piece on...",
  which batch two overused (about 320 times) and QA cut to a handful. (Batch two checked: all "I have written" links point to
  the same author's posts.)
