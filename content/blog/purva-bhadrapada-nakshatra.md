The god of this star has one foot and, in some images, the face of a goat, and he stands, the Vedas say, holding up the sky. The story sounds strange until you notice that it is about standing firm on very little. Purva Bhadrapada nakshatra, Poorattathi in Tamil, is the twenty-fifth of the twenty-seven birth stars, and it carries some of the most unsettling symbols in the whole zodiac. Looked at directly, they are less frightening than they sound.

## Purva Bhadrapada nakshatra: the one-footed god, Jupiter and a sword

Grandmothers kept track of the twenty-seven nakshatras, the star groups the Moon moves through each month, the way we keep track of weekdays. Purva Bhadrapada nakshatra is presided over by Aja Ekapada, "the unborn one-footed one", a fiery figure closely linked with Rudra. Its adhipati, the ruling planet, is Guru, Jupiter, the planet of teachers and wisdom.

Its symbols are the front legs of a funeral cot, a sword, and a man with two faces. That is quite a list. Grandmothers did not dwell on the cot; they talked about the sword and the scholar. Tradition describes Poorattathi-born people as intense, idealistic, eloquent and capable of real sacrifice, people who hold strong convictions and sometimes pay for them. Stars, in this tradition, describe a temperament; what you do with it is yours.

## Why does the sky rest on one foot?

In the Vedas, Aja Ekapada appears as a pillar between earth and heaven, the single support that keeps the two apart. Later traditions fold him into Shiva, and some temples show a one-legged form of the god with other deities emerging from his sides. The image has always resisted easy explanation. That is part of its power.

The story says a god stands on one foot and holds up the sky. The story is really about what you are willing to stand for alone. Most of us need a crowd before we take a position. We check who agrees, we look for a second foot. Aja Ekapada has no second foot. He holds the sky anyway.

Then there is the man with two faces. It is tempting to read that as hypocrisy, but the tradition reads it as a split between the worldly and the spiritual, one face turned towards the market and one towards the forest. Poorattathi, the star says, lives with both. The funeral cot belongs here too. Its front legs point to endings, and endings are where conviction is tested. It is easy to be idealistic at the beginning.

Put the three together and you get a picture of a certain kind of person: someone who stands on principle, sees both sides and knows that every stand has a cost. That is not a comfortable star. It is an honest one.

## Ranganathapuram and Thursday silence

Nakshatra temple lists differ for Poorattathi. One widely shared list names the Thiruvaneswarar temple at Ranganathapuram in Tamil Nadu, which has long drawn devotees born under Purva Bhadrapada nakshatra, while other lists name other shrines.

Because Guru rules the star, Thursday prayers to Jupiter and to Dakshinamurthy, the form of Shiva who teaches in silence, sit naturally with Poorattathi. A Thursday lamp is enough to begin; [Thursday worship of Guru](/blog/guru-graha-thursday-worship) gives the fuller form. When Poorattathi comes round each month, a lamp and a few minutes of silence are enough. Silence suits a star whose god teaches without speaking.

The Poorattathi that falls in your birth month is your star birthday. Many South Indian families keep it more carefully than the calendar birthday, and there is a fuller guide to [the star birthday and the ayushya homam](/blog/ayushya-homam-star-birthday). For Poorattathi, keep it simply; the almanac will tell you the day.

## Standing on one foot

Purva Bhadrapada spans two signs: its first three quarters lie in [Kumbha rasi, the water-bearer's sign](/blog/kumbha-rasi), and its last quarter in Meena, the fish, a sign ruled by Guru, the star's own planet. The star that follows, [Uttara Bhadrapada](/blog/uttara-bhadrapada-nakshatra), carries the back legs of the same cot and a much calmer god. The pair belong together, fire and deep water.

On your Poorattathi day, pick one conviction and act on it, without needing anyone to agree. Do not announce it. Do not argue for it. Just do it. That is how a one-footed god holds up the sky.
