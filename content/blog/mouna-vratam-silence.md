Mouna vratam is a vow of silence, and the first thing you notice when you keep one is not peace. It is how often you were about to speak. A comment rises, a joke, an explanation, a small correction, and you have to let it go unsaid. Within an hour, most people realise how much of their speech was automatic. So start smaller than you think you should. Try four hours before you try a day. The four hours will teach you plenty.

## What is mouna vratam in the tradition?

Mouna means silence. A vratam is a vow, a chosen discipline kept for a set time. Together, mouna vratam is a vow of silence taken on purpose, not silence that simply happens.

In the tradition it is often kept on particular days. Some people observe it on Mondays, the day associated with Shiva. Some keep it on Ekadashi, the eleventh day of each lunar fortnight, alongside a fast. And one day is named for it: Mouni Amavasya, the new moon of the month of Magha, which usually falls in the depth of winter, when many people keep silence and bathe in sacred rivers. The date shifts each year, so check the current year's panchangam if you want to observe it.

The idea behind the vow of silence in Hindu practice is old. Many sages were known for long silences, and the word muni, sage, is often traced to the same root as mouna. Some teachers were said to teach more through silence than through words. The tradition also holds that silence is itself a form of prayer: when you stop filling the air, there is room for something else.

## How do you plan a day of silence at home?

Silence in a household takes a little planning. Here is what tends to work.

Tell the household in advance. Nothing is more confusing for a family than a person who suddenly stops answering. Explain what you are doing and for how long.

Pick a morning. Mornings are naturally quieter, and fewer people need you. A weekend morning is ideal for a first try.

Keep phones and messages off too. Silence that continues on a keyboard is not really silence. Texting, emailing and scrolling are all speech in another form. Put the phone in a drawer.

Keep tasks simple. Cooking, cleaning, walking, gardening, reading, all work well. Avoid anything that will force you to negotiate.

Allow gestures for safety. The vow is not meant to put anyone at risk. If a child needs help, if something goes wrong, speak. Emergencies always override the vow. The tradition was never meant to be rigid in a crisis.

## What do people notice in silence?

The first thing, as mentioned, is the urge. The urge to comment on what someone is doing. To explain yourself. To fill a pause. You will be surprised by how strong it is.

Then something odd happens. The voice in the head gets louder. With nowhere to go, the inner commentary turns up. Many people find this uncomfortable and want to stop. If you stay with it, it usually quietens, often in the second or third hour.

Listening changes. You hear more of what others say, because you are not preparing your reply. Some people notice sounds they had tuned out for years: birds, the fridge, their own breathing.

And afterwards, many people find they speak more carefully. Words feel heavier, more deliberate. That may be the most practical benefit of silence, spiritual or otherwise: you notice what is worth saying.

This connects to [why 108 is sacred](/blog/why-108-is-sacred), since a round of japa is itself a kind of silence filled with one word. [Trataka, lamp gazing](/blog/trataka-lamp-gazing), is another practice that pairs well with a silent morning. Many people keep their silence in the hour before dawn, and [waking in brahma muhurta](/blog/brahma-muhurta-waking-early) makes that hour easier to reach. And [Arjuna's despair in the Gita](/blog/arjuna-vishada-yoga-meaning) is a reminder that some of the most important moments come after words run out.

## Try this for a week

Keep one hour of silence each evening. Choose the same hour if you can. No speaking, no messages, no screens. Then, at the weekend, extend it to half a day.

Throughout the week, keep a quiet tally in your head: which words did you actually miss? Most people find the list is short. The rest, it turns out, were habit.
