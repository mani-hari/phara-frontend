There is a Tamil saying, "Magathil pirandhal jagathai aalvaan": born in Magam, you will rule the world. Parents of Magam babies hear it often, usually from a smiling relative. Magha nakshatra is indeed a royal star, with a throne for its symbol. But the throne, when you look at it closely, belongs to the dead.

## Magha nakshatra: the Pitrs' throne

Most of the twenty-seven nakshatras, the Moon's birth stars, have a god presiding over them. Magha has the Pitrs, the ancestors, the generations who came before us and who, in the tradition, are still owed remembrance. Its ruling planet is Ketu, the shadow planet associated with the past, with detachment and with what is left behind. Magha opens Simha, the sign of the lion; [Simha rasi](/blog/simha-rasi) describes the sign.

The symbol is a throne, or in some lists a palanquin. Both are seats of honour, and both are carried or inherited. Nobody builds their own throne. It is handed down.

Matchmakers expect a Magam-born person to be dignified, loyal to the lineage, generous, a little proud and fond of ceremony, someone who takes family seriously. It fits plenty of people born under other stars too, which is a good reason not to read it as a forecast.

## Why does a royal star belong to the ancestors?

There is a tale told around Mahalaya Paksha, the fortnight of the ancestors, that is not found in the Mahabharata itself but has grown up around its greatest giver. Karna, famous for never refusing anyone who asked, dies at Kurukshetra and goes to the heavens. There he is served gold and jewels instead of food. Everything he touches is precious and inedible.

He asks why. He is told: in life you gave gold freely, but you never offered food or water to your ancestors. You did not know who they were, raised as you were by a charioteer's family, and so you never fed them. Karna is sent back to earth for a fortnight to make offerings of food and water in their name, and that fortnight, the story says, is Mahalaya Paksha.

The story says greatness is inherited, or earned by generosity. The story is really about what we owe to those behind us. Karna gave to everyone in front of him and forgot the line he stood at the end of. A throne without ancestors is just a chair.

This is why the ancestral rites fit Magha so naturally. Tarpanam, the offering of water mixed with sesame to the ancestors, and tila homam, a fire ritual with sesame for the same purpose, are the traditional ways of setting the table for them. Both are described in [the guide to tarpanam at home](/blog/tarpanam-at-home) and in [Amavasya and the ancestors](/blog/amavasya-and-ancestors).

## Temple, star day and Masi Magam

Nakshatra temple lists differ for Magha. One widely shared list names the Mahalingeswarar temple at Thavasimedai, with the village spelt several ways, while other lists name other shrines, so check locally before travelling. Magam-born devotees who cannot travel ask for an archana, the chanting of Shiva's names over flowers, at whatever temple is near.

The star's great day is Masi Magam, when the full moon falls on Magam in the Tamil month of Masi, in late winter. On that day temple deities across Tamil Nadu are carried in procession to the sea or to the temple tank for a ritual bath, and devotees bathe with them. Kumbakonam's Mahamaham tank draws huge crowds on the rare years when the conjunction is especially auspicious.

On the monthly janma nakshatra, the day each month when the Moon comes back to Magam, a lamp for the ancestors is enough. The star birthday, Magam's return in your birth month, is a fitting day to remember elders, living and gone. The star before is [Ashlesha, the serpent's star](/blog/ashlesha-nakshatra), and the one after is [Purva Phalguni, the star of rest](/blog/purva-phalguni-nakshatra).

## A throne is only a chair

On your Magam day, say the names of three people who came before you, aloud. A grandparent, a great-grandparent, anyone whose name you know. If you do not know three names, ask someone who does, and write them down. That small act is the whole of Magha. A throne is only a chair until someone remembers who sat in it.
