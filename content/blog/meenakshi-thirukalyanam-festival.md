My uncle in Madurai says the whole city gets dressed for one wedding every year. On the morning of the Meenakshi Thirukalyanam, he says, you can tell the married women on the bus by the fresh yellow thread at their necks, renewed that morning while the goddess herself was being married a few streets away. I have never been there on the day. I have watched it on a phone propped against the sugar tin in our New Jersey kitchen, with my mother narrating from Chennai.

## What is Meenakshi Thirukalyanam?

Meenakshi Thirukalyanam is the celestial wedding of the goddess Meenakshi and Sundareswarar, Shiva as the beautiful lord, the centrepiece of the Chithirai festival at the [Madurai Meenakshi temple](/blog/madurai-meenakshi-temple). It falls in the Tamil month of Chithirai, usually April or May, as part of a festival that runs for well over a week of processions, chariots and crowds.

The Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam, the collection of Shiva's sixty-four sacred plays in Madurai, tells of a Pandya princess born from a sacrificial fire, raised as heir, crowned queen, who conquered the world and marched on Kailash, where one look at Shiva told her she had met her match. He came to Madurai and married her there. [Meenakshi, the queen who ruled](/blog/meenakshi-the-queen-who-ruled) tells the story properly, and asks what it means that the goddess of this city was a sovereign first and a bride second. My uncle's answer is shorter: in Madurai, he says, the wife's name always comes first on the temple.

## Why does Alagar arrive late?

Every wedding needs a brother, and Meenakshi's is Vishnu, as Kallazhagar of the Alagar Kovil temple in the hills outside Madurai. The festival tells how he set out, riding a golden horse, to give his sister away. But the journey was long, and when he reached the banks of the Vaigai river, word came that the wedding was already over. Hurt, or perhaps only gracious, he entered the river, gave his blessings from there, and turned back home without crossing into the city.

Every year the scene is re-enacted. Kallazhagar's image, on its golden horse, enters the Vaigai before a crowd that fills the riverbanks from end to end. My uncle has been going since he was a boy. He says nobody minds that Alagar is late. They come to see him arrive anyway.

I think about that more than I expected to. We miss so many celebrations: weddings in Chennai, a cousin's housewarming, my father's birthday, all of it happening while we are asleep eight thousand miles away. Alagar did not make it in time either. He came anyway, stood in the river, blessed them, and went home. Showing up late still counts as showing up.

## Keeping the wedding at home

We keep the Thirukalyanam in a small way. On the morning of the wedding, many married women renew the *thali* thread, or tie a fresh *manjal kayiru*, a turmeric-yellow thread, as a renewal of their own marriage vows. My wife does this, quietly, before breakfast. We light a lamp before a picture of Meenakshi, offer a few flowers, and if the timing works, watch the temple's live broadcast with my parents on a video call. My mother explains every step as if we have never seen it. We make something sweet for the family.

The season is full. Madurai's hills also hold Thiruparankundram, where Murugan married Deivanai, one of his [six sacred abodes](/blog/six-abodes-of-murugan), and his own birthday, [Vaikasi Visakam](/blog/vaikasi-visakam-murugan), follows a month later. Around the same time come [Akshaya Tritiya, the day of what never runs out](/blog/akshaya-tritiya-significance), and in the North, [Hanuman Jayanti](/blog/hanuman-jayanti-significance). It is a busy stretch for the gods.

## Go, even late

Manikkavasagar, the Tamil saint, sang in the Tiruvasagam of Shiva as the lord who came down to Madurai again and again for the sake of his devotees. The god of the whole universe kept showing up in one small city for people who needed him.

One small thing for this week: go to someone's celebration, even if you are late, even if you can only stay for ten minutes. A birthday, a graduation, a colleague's farewell lunch, a video call you almost skipped. Stand in the river, like Alagar, and give your blessing. They will remember that you came.
