In Chennai, my mother has already cooked the maanga pachadi by the time my alarm goes off in New Jersey. Tamil New Year Puthandu arrives at home before it arrives here; by the time I am brushing my teeth, the family WhatsApp group is full of kolams and photographs of fruit trays. Keeping it in America means keeping it a little late, a little small, and usually on a weekday. It still counts.

## When is Tamil New Year Puthandu, and why doesn't it move much?

Puthandu, which simply means new year, falls on the first day of the Tamil month of Chithirai, the day the sun enters Mesha, the sign of Aries. That makes it a solar new year. Ugadi, which many of our Telugu and Kannada friends keep a few weeks earlier, follows the moon and moves around; Puthandu follows the sun and so stays close to the same spot on the calendar, usually in mid-April. Kerala's Vishu falls on the same day for the same reason.

Even so, the exact day can shift by one depending on when the sun crosses into Mesha, so check the current year's panchangam, the traditional almanac. If the columns look like a spreadsheet from another century, [reading a panchangam](/blog/how-to-read-a-panchangam) helps. One practical note for America: the new year begins by Indian Standard Time, which is nine and a half or ten and a half hours ahead of the East Coast depending on daylight saving. Family in India will be finishing lunch when you wake up.

## The morning at home, even on a Tuesday

Puthandu at home starts the night before. My mother always set a tray in the puja room before she went to sleep: fruits, especially mango, banana and jackfruit when she could find it, a small mirror, a piece of gold jewellery, flowers, betel leaves and areca nut, and a few coins. In the morning, the first thing you are meant to see on waking is that tray. It is the same idea as the [Vishu kani, the first sight of the new year](/blog/vishu-kani-meaning), which my Malayali friends arrange with even more care.

Our version in New Jersey is shorter. The tray goes on the kitchen counter, because that is where our small puja shelf is. My wife draws a small kolam at the front door with rice flour, which the wind usually takes by noon. We light a lamp. And if there is time, we make the mango pachadi: raw mango, jaggery, a little neem flower if the Indian store has it, tempered with mustard seeds. Sweet, sour and bitter together, like its Telugu cousin, the [Ugadi pachadi with its six tastes](/blog/ugadi-pachadi-six-tastes). Vadai happens only if Puthandu falls on a weekend.

When it falls on a Tuesday, as it often seems to, the whole thing takes twenty minutes before the school bus. That is fine. The tradition never said it had to be long.

## Calling India, finding a temple, telling the kids

The time difference turns out to be a gift. India's evening is our morning, so the video call with my parents happens while their lamp is still lit and ours has just been lit. My father sometimes reads out a few lines of the new year's panchangam forecast, the rains and the harvests, and I nod as if I will remember.

Many US temples hold the panchangam reading on the nearest weekend rather than the day itself, which is kinder to working families. Call ahead. Explaining it at work is simple too; [explaining Hindu festivals to coworkers](/blog/explaining-hindu-festivals-to-coworkers) has lines you can borrow, as I have.

And for the kids, a two-line version. When my son was younger I said: "The sun starts a new lap around the sky today, so we start a new year. We look at good things first so the year starts well." He is about eleven now and asks harder questions, but that answer still holds. Around the same weeks comes [Ram Navami, a noon puja you can keep at home](/blog/ram-navami-puja-at-home), and he likes that one better because of the panakam.

## Begin something small

The Tirukkural opens with the first letter of the Tamil alphabet: *agara mudhala ezhuththellaam*, as the letter A stands first among all letters, the Divine stands first in the world. It is a book that starts, quite literally, at the beginning. On Puthandu morning, begin something small. It can be as small as lighting the lamp before you open the laptop. The new year does not need a grand opening. It only needs a first letter.
