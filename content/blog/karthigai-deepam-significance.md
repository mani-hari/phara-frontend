Brahma and Vishnu once argued over which of them was greater, and in the middle of the argument a pillar of fire appeared between them, so tall and so deep that neither could find its end. Karthigai Deepam is the night we remember that pillar. The Karthigai Deepam significance is simple to state: it honours Shiva as endless light, and it falls on the full moon of the Tamil month of Karthigai, when the Krittika star is in the sky, usually in November or December. What the story means is less simple, and more interesting.

## Karthigai Deepam significance: the column of fire

The story is told in the Shiva Purana. Brahma, the creator, and Vishnu, the preserver, fell into a quarrel about rank. Before it could turn into a war, a column of fire rose up between them, without beginning or end. A voice said that whoever found either end of it would be the greater.

Brahma became a swan and flew upward. Vishnu became a boar and dug downward. They searched for what the story calls a very long time. Vishnu, somewhere deep in the earth, finally stopped and admitted he could not find the bottom. Brahma, flying upward, met a ketaki flower drifting down, and persuaded it to say he had reached the top.

The pillar split open, and Shiva stepped out. He honoured Vishnu for his honesty and rebuked Brahma for the lie. The tradition says that is why Brahma has so few temples, and why the ketaki flower is never offered to Shiva. The fiery column is called the Jyotirlinga, the linga of light, and the Lingodbhava image of Shiva emerging from it is carved on the back wall of many Tamil temples.

## Why is the flame lit on a hill?

In Tiruvannamalai, the tradition says that pillar of fire cooled into a mountain: Arunachala, the red hill, which is worshipped as Shiva himself rather than as his home. On the evening of Karthigai Deepam, a huge beacon called the Maha Deepam is lit on the summit at dusk, fed with ghee and a thick cloth wick, and it can be seen for miles. Before dawn that same day, five lamps are lit inside the temple and merged into one, the Bharani Deepam. If you watch it from far away, [the Tiruvannamalai Deepam from afar](/blog/tiruvannamalai-deepam-from-afar) is about exactly that.

The night belongs to Murugan too. The Skanda Purana says Shiva's son was born as six babies in a lotus pond, raised by the six Krittika maidens, the stars of the Pleiades, until Parvati gathered them into one child with six faces. The Krittika star, Karthigai in Tamil, gives the month and the festival its name. Many families light lamps for Murugan on this night as well.

## What is the story really about?

The story says two gods went looking for the edges of Shiva. The story is really about the limits of looking.

Brahma and Vishnu are not fools. They are the two things the mind does best: going up, into ideas and plans, and going down, into roots and causes. Both are useful. Neither finds the end of something without an end. One of them admits it. The other invents a witness.

Most of us are Brahma more often than we would like. We would rather report that we reached the top than say "I do not know". The ketaki flower is a small, pretty lie, and it is always available.

The lamp answers the pillar. The pillar is infinite fire; the lamp in your doorway is a tiny, humble copy of it, a flame you can hold in your palm. It does not claim to be the whole of the light. It only claims to be light. On a night like this, the choice of oil matters to some families, and [ghee lamp or oil lamp](/blog/ghee-lamp-or-oil-lamp) explains the difference.

## One lamp, one full minute

At dusk on Karthigai Deepam, light one lamp, or many if you like; there is a guide to [celebrating Karthigai Deepam at home](/blog/karthigai-deepam-at-home) with the traditional rows of clay lamps. Then sit in front of one of them and look at it for a full minute without doing anything else.

Some quarrel is probably running in your head, about who is right, who is more important, who owes whom. Let the flame be the pillar between the two sides. You do not have to find its end. You only have to stop measuring, for one minute, and let the argument finish there.
