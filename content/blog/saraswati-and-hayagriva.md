Two demons, Madhu and Kaitabha, slip into Brahma's realm while he is lost in thought, steal the Vedas and carry them to the bottom of the ocean. Creation stalls, because Brahma cannot create without knowledge. Vishnu dives after them and comes back with the head of a horse. So who is the god of education in Hinduism? Two answers, really: Saraswati, goddess of speech, music and learning, and Hayagriva, the horse-headed form of Vishnu who brought the Vedas back. Knowledge is rescued, but read closely, this is a story about how learning keeps getting lost, and found again.

## Who is the god of education in Hinduism?

Saraswati is the name most people know. She is honoured across India as the goddess of knowledge, speech, music and the arts, and in the Rig Veda she is first of all a river, flowing and nourishing. In much of South India her great day is Saraswati Puja, near the end of Navratri, when books and instruments are placed before her and taken up again on Vijayadashami, the tenth day. If you have ever wondered why that festival stretches over nine nights, [why Navratri lasts nine nights](/blog/why-navratri-lasts-nine-nights) tells the larger story.

Hayagriva is less famous outside South India, but inside it he is everywhere. Students in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka say his shloka, a short verse of praise, before exams, and families invoke him when a child first learns to write. He is Vishnu as the restorer of knowledge: not the one who invents it, but the one who goes and gets it back when it has been taken.

Between them, they cover the two halves of learning. Saraswati is the flow of it. Hayagriva is the recovery of it.

## Saraswati's white, the veena and the swan

Saraswati symbolism is unusually precise. Every object with her means something.

- **White.** She is dressed in white and often seated on a white lotus. White is the colour that holds all colours without mixing them. It points to clarity, to attention that is not yet coloured by opinion.
- **The veena.** She holds a stringed instrument, not a scroll alone. Music cannot be learned by reading about it. It is learned by practice, every day, badly at first. Knowledge, in this picture, is a skill, not a store of facts.
- **The swan.** Her vehicle is the hamsa, a swan said in tradition to be able to separate milk from water when the two are mixed. This is discernment: the ability to take what is true from what is mixed with it, and let the rest go.

Put together, the image is not of a goddess who gives you answers. It is of a goddess who gives you the means to find them: clear attention, steady practice and the judgment to tell good from bad.

## Hayagriva and the stolen Vedas

The Mahabharata tells the Hayagriva story in its Shanti Parva, and the Puranas retell it with variations. Madhu and Kaitabha steal the Vedas; Vishnu takes the form of Hayagriva, descends into the waters, recites the Vedas in a deep, resonant voice, and while the demons follow the sound he recovers the texts and restores them to Brahma.

Why a horse? In Vedic ritual the horse is a symbol of energy and speed, and the horse's head is linked with the sound of the chant itself. A horse also does not stay still. The god of recovered knowledge is one who moves, who goes down into the deep and comes back.

The story is familiar to anyone who has studied for an exam. You learn something, you lose it, you have to go back down and fetch it again. That is not failure. That is how learning works. It is also why so many students keep a [short prayer before exams](/blog/prayer-before-exams) to Hayagriva: he is the god of the second attempt.

## A river, not a throne

Look at Saraswati again and notice where she sits. Not on a throne, but by a river, or on a lotus floating on it. Learning is not a place you arrive at. It moves. It needs tending every day, like the veena, like the flame. The [Gayatri mantra](/blog/gayatri-mantra-meaning), perhaps the oldest prayer for a clear mind, asks for the same thing: that our thinking be lit and kept moving.

So here is a suggestion. Keep one book beside the lamp where you pray, something you are actually reading. Open it for a few minutes after you light the lamp. If you are a parent in exam season, [this column on praying for a child's exam](/blog/exam-season-for-parents) is a gentle companion.
