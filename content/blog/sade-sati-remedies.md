The most traditional Shani sade sati remedies are not dramatic. A sesame-oil lamp on Saturday evening. A short mantra. A promise kept. A meal given to someone who needed it. The common thread is doing small, slow things on time, which happens to be exactly the quality Saturn is said to reward. If sade sati is a season of pressure, the remedy is not to fight the pressure. It is to become the kind of person who can carry it.

## Shani sade sati remedies you can do at home

Here are the simple remedies for sade sati at home that families have kept for generations.

**A Saturday sesame-oil lamp.** At dusk on Saturday, light a small lamp with sesame oil, a cotton wick, in front of a picture of Shani, Hanuman or simply in your prayer corner. Sit with it for five minutes. Sesame is traditionally linked with Shani in Jyotish, and sesame oil burns with a steady, low flame. The eyes settle on it. That is the point.

**A few minutes of mantra.** "Om Sham Shanaischaraya Namah" is the short Shani mantra most families use. Say it slowly, eleven or twenty-seven times, on the out-breath. Or chant the Hanuman Chalisa, which many households prefer on Saturdays; the reason is told in [Hanuman Chalisa on Saturdays](/blog/hanuman-chalisa-on-saturdays).

**Keeping promises.** Make fewer commitments and keep all of them. Saturn is associated with time and accountability, so the practice is to be where you said you would be, when you said you would.

**Eating simply.** On Saturdays, eat plain food, less of it, and eat on time. Some people avoid meat and alcohol on Saturdays during sade sati. This is not about purity. A body that is not sluggish is better at bearing weight.

## Why do service and discipline count as remedies?

In Jyotish, Saturn governs the labourer, the elderly, the servant, the overlooked. People who work with their hands. People nobody thanks. So tradition's answer to Saturn is to go toward those people, not away from them.

That is why Saturday remedies for Shani so often involve service. Feed someone: a daily-wage worker, a person on the street, the crows that many families in South India feed before their own meal. The idea of feeding as worship runs through the tradition, and [annadhanam, feeding as worship](/blog/annadhanam-feeding-as-worship), goes into it further. Help an elderly neighbour with shopping. Clean up after yourself properly, at home and at work, including the parts nobody will check.

Discipline works on the body the same way. Wake at the same hour. Sleep at the same hour. Finish what you begin before you start something new. None of this is supernatural. It simply trains the nervous system to stay steady when life is not, and a steady person meets a hard season very differently from a scattered one. If you want to understand the phase itself before choosing what to do, read [Shani sade sati explained](/blog/shani-sade-sati-explained).

## When a temple pooja or homam helps

Some people want a larger, focused act of intention, and that is where a Shani pooja or a Navagraha homam comes in. A Shani pooja is a temple ritual addressed to Saturn, with sesame, blue or black cloth and his mantras. A Navagraha homam is a fire ritual, a homam, offered to all nine planetary influences together; there is more on how fire rituals work in [what is a homam](/blog/what-is-a-homam).

Who does it suit? People at the peak of sade sati who want to mark the season deliberately. People who feel stuck and want a clear starting point. Families who want to pray together for someone going through a hard stretch. The priest performs the ritual with a sankalpam, a spoken intention, and the devotee's name and birth star.

But be clear with yourself about what it is. A pooja is a focused prayer. It accompanies your own effort; it does not replace it. The lamp, the discipline and the service still have to happen every week.

## Seven Saturdays

Try this for seven Saturdays. Same time each week, perhaps half an hour after sunset. Same lamp, same place. Five minutes of mantra. One act of service, however small, somewhere in the day. Write one line in a notebook each Saturday night: what you did, and how you feel.

At the end of seven weeks, read the lines back. Many people find that Saturn has not changed, but they have. If you also want a clearer picture of your own chart, go slowly; [reading your birth chart calmly](/blog/reading-your-birth-chart-calmly) shows how.
