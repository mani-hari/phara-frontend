Watching a live puja online on a phone at midnight can be scrolling, or it can be worship. The screen is the same either way. The difference is what your body and your attention are doing. If you prepare as if you were standing in the temple, wash, sit down, light a lamp, silence everything else, the live stream becomes a window rather than a video. And if the live hour is impossible, watching the recording later with the same care is just as sincere.

## Before the live puja online starts

Most temple rituals in India happen in the early morning, Indian Standard Time. For someone in the US that usually means late evening or the middle of the night: a six o'clock puja in Tamil Nadu is half past eight the evening before in New York, and half past five in the afternoon in California. Once American clocks fall back in November, subtract one more hour. Check the scheduled time in IST, convert it, and put it in your calendar with a fifteen-minute warning.

Use that fifteen minutes. This is the part people skip, and it matters most.

- **Wash.** A bath if you can, or at least face, hands and feet. You will feel the shift.
- **Sit on the floor.** On a mat or folded blanket, facing the screen, spine upright. The couch tells the body it is time to be entertained.
- **Keep a lamp and water beside you.** A small oil lamp or a tea light, a cup of water, a flower if you have one.
- **Phone on do-not-disturb.** If the phone is the screen, turn off every notification. Better still, cast the stream to a laptop and put the phone in another room.

Try this: before the stream begins, sit with your eyes closed for two minutes and just feel the breath at the nostrils. You are arriving. The temple is far, but your body is here, and the body is where attention lives.

## What should I do while the priest chants?

This is where many people drift. The chanting is in Sanskrit, the camera angle is shaky, and after a few minutes the mind starts checking the other tabs. Give it something to do.

**Follow the breath with the chant.** You do not need to understand the words. Let the rhythm of the recitation set the rhythm of your breathing. Many people find the mind settles when the ear has one steady thing to follow.

**Repeat the deity's name quietly.** If it is a Ganapathi homam, repeat Om Gam Ganapataye Namah under your breath. If it is for Shiva, Om Namah Shivaya. There is more on how to start in [chanting for beginners](/blog/chanting-for-beginners).

**Offer at the moments of aarti.** When the priest waves the lamp in the aarti, the offering of light, raise your own lamp and move it in slow circles before your altar. Fold your hands. When the flowers are offered, offer yours.

**Hear your name.** If it is your puja, listen for your details in the sankalpam, the spoken intention. It is a strange, good feeling to hear your own name spoken at a temple on the other side of the world.

## When the hour is impossible

Sometimes it is three in the morning, or it clashes with the school run, or you are simply exhausted. Sleep. The ritual is complete when the priest performs it with your sankalpam, whether you watch or not. There is a longer explanation of [how online puja from the USA works](/blog/online-puja-from-usa-explained) if that worries you.

Then watch the recording at a calm hour, ideally at dawn, with exactly the same preparation. Wash, sit, lamp, water, silence. The timestamp on the video does not matter; your presence does. Many people find a recording watched with full attention at six in the morning means more than a live stream half-watched at midnight.

If you booked the puja for someone else, a parent in Chennai, say, call them afterwards and watch a few minutes together. There is a separate piece on [booking a puja for your parents from abroad](/blog/booking-a-puja-for-parents) and how to include them.

## Try this for your next puja

Three rules, just once. No second screen. One lamp. Five minutes of silence after the stream ends, before you touch the phone.

The silence is the part that surprises people. Rituals end with a closing, and then comes the stillness that the whole thing was building towards. It is close to what [meditation and puja share](/blog/meditation-vs-puja). When the prasadam arrives weeks later, you will remember that stillness, and [receiving the prasadam with the same attention](/blog/what-to-do-with-prasadam) completes the practice.
