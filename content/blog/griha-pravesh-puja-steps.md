A house becomes a home the moment you start paying attention to it. The griha pravesh puja, the ritual of first entering a new home, is simply a structured way of doing that: a prayer to Ganesha, a kalasha at the door, stepping in with the right foot, lighting a lamp and boiling milk until it overflows. It takes an hour or less. Done with care, it turns moving day from boxes and tape into an arrival. Here are the steps, why each one is there, and how to do them with or without a priest.

## Griha pravesh puja steps, in order

These are the griha pravesh puja at home steps most families follow. Regional customs add more, but this core is widely shared.

1. **Pray to Ganesha.** Before anything else, stand at the entrance and offer a short prayer to Ganesha, the guardian of beginnings. Even "Om Gam Ganapataye Namah" said three times is enough. If you have ever wondered [why Ganesha is worshipped first](/blog/why-ganesha-comes-first), the story behind it is worth reading before the day.
2. **Set the kalasha at the door.** A kalasha is a pot of water with mango leaves around its mouth and a coconut on top, symbolising fullness. Place it just inside or beside the threshold.
3. **Enter with the right foot first.** Walk in slowly. Many families carry the kalasha, a lamp or an image of the family deity as they cross.
4. **Light the lamp.** Go to the corner you have chosen for worship and light a ghee or oil lamp. This is the first flame in the house.
5. **Boil milk until it overflows.** On the new stove, heat milk in a vessel and let it rise and spill over the edge. Then turn the heat down and make it into a simple sweet.
6. **Offer, then eat together.** Offer the sweet at the lamp and share it. Cook and eat a simple meal in the house the same day.

## Why does the milk have to boil over?

This is the step people remember and the one they ask about most. The meaning of boiling milk in a new house is abundance: the household should have more than enough, so much that it spills. That is the traditional explanation, and it is a good one.

There is also something practical going on. The kitchen is the heart of a home, and this is its first use. You are standing at the stove, watching, waiting for the milk to rise. You cannot rush it and you cannot look away, or it scorches. For a few minutes, everyone in the family is paying attention to one thing in one room. Try it and notice how your body feels in the house afterwards. New spaces often feel slightly foreign for weeks. Many people find the first flame and the smell of milk make the kitchen feel lived-in within minutes.

## Doing it without a priest, or with one on a call

Can you do griha pravesh without a priest? Yes. Everything above can be done by the family, and many families do exactly that. The acts carry the meaning. What a priest adds is Vedic chanting, a formal sankalpam, the spoken statement of intention naming the family and the home, and sometimes a full sequence of offerings like the [sixteen steps of puja](/blog/sixteen-steps-of-puja).

Some families ask a priest to guide them on a video call, which works well: the priest chants, the family performs. Others do the steps themselves and have a Ganapathy homam, a fire ritual for Ganesha, performed at a temple on their behalf, so the formal part is taken care of. If you are curious what that involves, here is [what happens during a homam](/blog/what-is-a-homam). And if your new home is an apartment in another country, [a housewarming puja in the USA](/blog/blessing-a-new-home-abroad) adapts all of this to smoke alarms and electric stoves.

## A one-page checklist

Print this and stick it on the fridge the night before.

- Clean the house, especially the entrance, kitchen and puja corner
- Kalasha with water, five mango leaves and a coconut
- Lamp, ghee or oil, cotton wicks, matches
- Flowers, turmeric and kumkum, the red powder used in worship, for the threshold
- Milk, a vessel for boiling it, rice and jaggery for the sweet
- A short Ganesha prayer written on a card
- Ingredients for one simple meal

One suggestion above all: eat that meal together in the house on the first day, sitting on the floor if the furniture has not arrived. That meal is the part of the ritual you will remember.
