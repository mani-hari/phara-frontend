Every few months a forwarded message arrives saying a planet is moving and everyone of your sign should be careful. Try something different this time. Instead of worrying, keep a note of how your week actually goes, and compare it with the forecast at the end. A gochara transit is simply the current movement of the planets read against your birth chart, and it is meant to be understood like weather: something to notice and prepare for, not something to panic about.

Most people who try the note-keeping find the forecast was either vague enough to fit anything or had very little to do with their week. Both are useful discoveries.

## What is a gochara transit?

Gochara means moving through, or roaming. In Jyotish, Vedic astrology, it refers to where the planets are right now, as opposed to where they were at your birth. The traditional method is to count each planet's present sign from your birth Moon sign, your rasi. That is why transits from the Moon sign are the basis of every weekly column arranged by rasi.

The planets move at very different speeds, and that changes how much attention each one gets:

- The Sun spends about a month in each sign.
- Jupiter spends about a year.
- Rahu and Ketu spend about eighteen months.
- Saturn spends about two and a half years.

So how long does Saturn stay in a sign? Long enough to become a season of life rather than a passing mood. The Moon, by contrast, changes sign every two or three days; nobody builds a year around it.

## Which transits do astrologers watch?

Mostly the slow ones. Saturn and Jupiter get the most attention because their transits last long enough to feel like chapters. When Jupiter changes sign, many South Indian families mark Guru Peyarchi, with prayers timed to [the Jupiter transit](/blog/guru-peyarchi-jupiter-transit). When Saturn enters the sign before, on or after your Moon sign, the tradition calls it sade sati, the seven and a half years, and [a calm look at sade sati remedies](/blog/sade-sati-remedies) takes much of the fear out of it.

Tradition also counts houses. Certain positions from the Moon are considered easier for a given planet and others harder. If you want to know what those houses stand for, the piece on [the twelve houses in Vedic astrology](/blog/twelve-houses-in-astrology) sets them out plainly.

Here is the part forwarded messages leave out. Traditional astrologers never read a transit alone. They read it with your dasha, the planetary period you are running, which is explained in [the dasha periods piece](/blog/dasha-periods-explained), and with the strength of the planets in your own birth chart. A transit that is said to be difficult for your sign may barely register for you, and the reverse is also true. A sign-wide forecast is a sketch drawn for millions of people at once.

## Try this for a week

When the next transit forecast lands in your phone, do not argue with it and do not obey it. Pick one practical discipline instead and keep it for seven days.

- **Sleep.** Go to bed at the same time every night. Nothing steadies the mind faster.
- **A lamp on Saturday.** Light a small sesame oil lamp at dusk on Saturday and sit with it for two minutes.
- **One kept promise.** Choose one small commitment, such as a daily walk, a call to a parent or no phone at dinner, and keep it every day without exception.

The tradition says Shani, Saturn, governs time, work and discipline. Use that. If Saturn is the planet people fear most, the most sensible response is to practise the things he is said to reward: consistency, patience and effort without drama. Many people find that a week of kept discipline changes how they feel about the transit completely. The planet did not change. Your footing did.

Jupiter works the same way from the other side. The tradition links him with teachers and wisdom, and Thursday prayers to the Guru are a traditional response to his movement; [Guru graha worship](/blog/guru-graha-thursday-worship) describes a simple Thursday practice.

## Weather, not a verdict

A transit is weather. You check the forecast, you carry an umbrella if it looks like rain, and then you go to work. You do not cancel your life because the sky is grey.

So notice the transits if you like. Prepare simply: sleep, a lamp, one kept promise. Then watch your own week with attention, and let your experience be the thing you trust. You still choose what to wear.
