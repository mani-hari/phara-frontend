Thatha used to close his cloth shop in Kumbakonam early on Aadi Perukku. There was no point staying open, he said; nobody bought saris that afternoon, because everyone in town was down at the Kaveri with a bundle of variety rice, a lamp and a length of yellow thread. As a boy I thought it was a picnic. It took me years, and an ocean, to understand it was a thank-you note.

## What is Aadi Perukku?

Aadi Perukku falls on the eighteenth day of the Tamil month of Aadi, usually in early August, and many people simply call it Aadi Pathinettu, "Aadi eighteen". *Perukku* means rising or swelling. By this point in the monsoon, the rains in the hills have filled the Kaveri, and the river that runs thin and patient for much of the year arrives in the delta in full flood.

For the Kaveri delta, around Thanjavur, Kumbakonam, Tiruchirappalli and Srirangam, this is everything. The river is the reason there are fields, rice, towns and temples at all. So on the day it rises, people go down to the water and thank it. It is also a traditional day to begin sowing, and the proverb about sowing in the month of Aadi is still quoted by farmers and grandmothers alike. The whole month has its own character, and [the significance of the Aadi month](/blog/aadi-month-significance) explains why it is kept as the Goddess's season.

## At the river: rice, thread and lamps

My grandmother would go down to the steps of the river with the other women of the street. They offered turmeric, flowers, a little jaggery and rice to the water, lit small lamps on leaves and let them float. Married women and newlyweds tied fresh yellow thread, *manjal kayiru*, around their necks or wrists, renewing a marriage in the presence of the river. Young women prayed for good husbands. Everyone prayed for good harvests.

Then came the food. Aadi Perukku is the day of *chitrannam*, the variety rices: lemon, tamarind, coconut and curd, sometimes a sweet pongal as well, packed at home and eaten on the riverbank in family groups. Every Tamil festival has its dish, whether it is this bundle of rices or the [Thiruvathirai kali](/blog/thiruvathirai-kali-recipe) of winter, and this one is made for eating outdoors, with your hands, near water.

At Srirangam, the story goes further. The Kaveri is treated as the sister of Lord Ranganatha, and on this day the temple sends her gifts, silk, turmeric, flowers and betel, as a brother sends gifts to a married sister. The river is family.

## How do you keep Aadi Perukku far from the Kaveri?

We have no Kaveri in New Jersey. We have a small creek at the edge of the park and a very large reservoir somewhere we have never seen, which sends water to our taps.

So we improvise. On the morning, my wife fills a brass bowl with water, adds a pinch of turmeric and a few flowers, and sets it on the balcony with a small lamp beside it. We make one variety rice for lunch, usually lemon rice because it is quickest. If the day is kind, we call my mother around her evening, and she walks the phone out to wherever she can hear or see water. Some years I walk down to the creek with my son and we just stand there for a minute. He finds it strange. He also remembers it.

Aadi is full of these sacred days. Soon after comes [Varalakshmi Vratham at home](/blog/varalakshmi-vratham-at-home), and then [Avani Avittam, the renewal of the sacred thread](/blog/avani-avittam-meaning). The new moon of Aadi, which falls in the same month, is one of the great days of remembrance; there is more on [Amavasya and the ancestors](/blog/amavasya-and-ancestors). Rivers and ancestors belong together in Tamil thought. Both came before us, and both keep us alive.

## Say thank you to your water

An old Tamil saying, attributed to Avvaiyar, holds that a town without a river has no beauty. I think she meant more than scenery. A town without a river forgets where its life comes from.

One small thing for this week: find out where your own water comes from. The reservoir, the river, the aquifer, the name of it. Then say thank you to it once, out loud or under your breath, as you fill a glass. It will feel silly. Thatha would have understood completely.
