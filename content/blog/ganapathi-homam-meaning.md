When we moved into our place in New Jersey, my father asked on the video call, before anything else, whether we had done the Ganapathi homam. He did not ask about the boxes, or the commute, or whether the heating worked. He asked about Ganesha. Ask him about Ganapathi homam benefits and he names only one: you do not begin a thing without first clearing the road. It took me a few years to understand that he was not asking about fire. He was asking whether we had begun on purpose.

## What happens in a Ganapathi homam?

A homam is a fire ritual, offerings made into a consecrated fire while mantras are chanted. The Ganapathi homam is the most common of them, and for many families the first. It is also, fittingly, the one done first, and the reason is a story of its own: [why Ganesha comes first](/blog/why-ganesha-comes-first) in every Hindu ritual.

The Ganapathi homam procedure goes roughly like this.

1. **Sankalpam.** The priest states the intention, the family's names, birth stars and gotra, and what the homam is for.
2. **The kalasam.** A pot of water, topped with mango leaves and a coconut, is set up to hold Ganesha's presence.
3. **The fire.** It is lit in a square kundam, the fire pit, usually of brick or copper.
4. **The mantras.** The Ganapati Moola mantra is chanted, often with the Ganapati Atharvashirsha, an Upanishad in praise of Ganesha.
5. **The offerings.** Into the fire go the ashta dravya, the eight substances: modakam, the sweet dumpling Ganesha loves, sugarcane, coconut, jaggery, sesame, puffed rice, banana and honey. Each is an ordinary kitchen thing. That is part of the point.
6. **Purnahuti.** The last offering is a whole coconut, wrapped in cloth, given to the fire entire.

In Kerala, the ashta dravya Ganapathi homam is often performed at dawn, and in many homes it is simply part of moving in, opening a business, or beginning a new year.

## What are the Ganapathi homam benefits in the tradition's own words?

The tradition speaks of obstacles easing. Ganesha is Vighneshwara, lord of obstacles, which means he places them as well as removes them. His homam is done at the start of things: a house, a job, a marriage, a child's first school day. My father would add that it is also done after a long stretch of things going wrong, to mark a fresh start.

What the tradition does not do is promise outcomes. No ritual promises that the house will be happy or the job will last. What a Ganapathi homam gives, as I have come to see it, is a beginning made with clear intent. You stop. You name what you are starting. You ask for the road to be clear. Then you walk.

If a short prayer suits you better than a fire, [the Vakratunda Mahakaya verse](/blog/vakratunda-mahakaya-meaning) is the one most of us learned as children, and it carries the same request in one line.

## Doing it from here

For those of us in the US, there are three realistic ways.

**A priest at a temple here.** Many temples in New Jersey and elsewhere will perform a Ganapathi homam at the temple, and some priests will come to your home. Fire rules in rented apartments can make the second harder; ask your landlord and your building before you plan a flame bigger than a lamp. [Blessing a new home abroad](/blog/blessing-a-new-home-abroad) covers the rest of that question.

**A homam in India, in your name.** A temple in India performs the homam with your names in the sankalpam, and you light a lamp at home at the same hour. This is what we did for our move. My mother watched the live stream in Chennai; I watched from the kitchen floor at an unreasonable hour.

**A very small home version.** A lamp, a picture of Ganesha, a few modakam or a banana, and the mantra Om Gam Ganapataye Namah said eleven times. It is not a homam. It is the same intention.

If you are weighing Ganapathi against another fire ritual, [how to choose a homam](/blog/how-to-choose-a-homam) sets them side by side, and parents of students may want [the Saraswati homam](/blog/saraswati-homam-for-students) instead.

## One small thing for this week

Before you start something new this week, a project, a phone call you have been putting off, a new routine, pause for ten seconds and say Ganesha's name once. Nothing more. The homam is only the long form of that pause, made public, with fire and family and a coconut. The pause itself you can carry anywhere.
