A friend in Houston once told me that for a whole winter he and his wife spoke only about logistics: school pick-up, groceries, bills. Nothing was wrong, exactly, and nothing was right. He found himself praying in the car without knowing what he was asking for. If you are searching for a prayer for marriage problems, here is the honest answer first: prayer will not fix your spouse, but it can steady your own mind enough to listen, and it works best alongside real help, not instead of it.

## What can prayer for marriage problems actually do?

It helps to be clear about what we are asking. Most of us, in a hard season, pray for the other person to change. That is understandable, and it is rarely useful. A better prayer is for the marriage itself, the thing the two of you share, rather than a prayer against the person across the table.

The Puranas are full of quarrels between Shiva and Parvati. She is hurt; he withdraws into meditation; she leaves, or does penance, or takes another form. What strikes me about these stories is how they end. Not with one of them winning, but with a return. They come back to Kailasa together. Ardhanarishvara, the image of Shiva and Parvati sharing one body, half and half, is the tradition's picture of what that return looks like (more in [what Ardhanarishvara means](/blog/ardhanarishvara-meaning)).

The Hindu view of marriage problems, then, is not that they should not happen. It is that a household is a practice, and practice includes the bad weeks.

## Is patience the same as keeping quiet?

The Tirukkural has a chapter on forbearance, and its first couplet, kural one hundred and fifty-one, compares the patient person to the earth, which holds up even those who dig into it. My father used to quote it at the dinner table, usually at me. I went through the chapter more carefully in [the Tirukkural on patience](/blog/tirukkural-on-patience).

But patience with someone's faults is not the same as silence about harm. There is a difference between a season of strain, the winter of logistics, the year of a new baby or a sick parent, and a situation that needs protection. Valluvar praised forbearance. He did not ask anyone to endure cruelty. If you are afraid in your own home, the tradition's teaching on patience is not addressed to you. Safety comes first, and no ritual replaces it.

## Prayer and help, together

So what does a practical path look like?

Light a lamp. Together if you can, alone if you must. A short mantra to Uma-Maheshwara, Shiva and Parvati as a couple, is traditional; some families say "Om Uma Maheshwarabhyam Namaha" a few times, quietly. The mantra for peace between husband and wife is less important than the pause it creates. If sitting together feels impossible right now, a column on [praying together as a couple](/blog/praying-together-as-a-couple) suggests a five-minute shape that asks very little. On anxious nights, a few small [rituals for anxious days](/blog/rituals-for-anxious-days) can help you sleep.

Then talk to someone. A trusted elder who will not take sides. A couples counsellor or therapist, which many Indian families still hesitate over and should not. Going to a counsellor is not a failure of faith any more than going to a doctor is. My friend in Houston went, eventually, with his wife. He says the car prayers were what got him to the first appointment.

And again, plainly: where there is abuse or fear, safety and professional help come first. Call someone you trust. Prayer can come later, and it will still be there.

How to pray when your marriage is struggling? Short and honest. "Help me listen." "Help us find our way back." Hospitals and hard marriages are not good at long prayers.

## One small thing for this week

The seventh vow at a Hindu wedding is not a promise of ease but a request for friendship, which a piece on [the seven steps of saptapadi](/blog/saptapadi-seven-steps-meaning) describes well. Friendship is built in small moments.

So here is the one thing to carry. This week, ask your spouse one question about their day, and wait for the whole answer before you speak. Do not fix anything. Just listen to the end.
