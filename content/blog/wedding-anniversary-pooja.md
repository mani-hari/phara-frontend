A wedding anniversary pooja does not have to be elaborate. In most South Indian homes it is simply an archana, a short offering of names and flowers, in both your names and stars, a lamp lit together, and the parents' blessings. Our anniversary usually falls on a work night. For years my wife and I marked it with a dinner reservation. Then one year she suggested the temple first, and dinner after, and the evening changed shape.

## What is a wedding anniversary pooja?

It is not a prescribed ritual so much as a family custom, and customs differ. What most families share is the instinct to begin the day with thanks before celebration. The usual elements are an archana at a temple in both names and birth stars, a lamp at home, a coconut broken at the shrine, and a prostration to the parents, the older generation's way of saying that the marriage belongs to more than two people.

Many South Indian families also match the day to the nakshatra, the birth star of the lunar calendar, on which the wedding took place, not only to the calendar date. That means the ritual anniversary can drift from the one on the invitation card. My aunt in Chennai still tracks both and calls on whichever comes first. Of the wedding rituals being remembered, [the seven steps of saptapadi](/blog/saptapadi-seven-steps-meaning) are the part many couples say they return to.

A wedding anniversary temple visit in the US is easy to fold into an evening. Most temples will do an archana in a few minutes; tell the priest both names and stars and he will take care of the rest.

## A simple anniversary pooja at home, together

If the temple is too far on a weeknight, the home version is enough.

Light the lamp together, both hands on the match or both on the lamp's base. Say a short sankalpam, the spoken statement of intention, naming both of you, your stars and your family, and asking for another year of patience and kindness. Offer flowers to Shiva and Parvati, the divine couple, or to your family deity. Place a sweet on the plate from your wedding menu; ours was paal payasam, and my wife still makes it the same way. Then call the parents on video. We time it for their morning in Chennai, and my son holds the phone while we bow. It is awkward and lovely every time.

The anniversary prayer for husband and wife does not need special words. A short piece on [praying together as a couple](/blog/praying-together-as-a-couple) offers a five-minute shape you can borrow. If you are newly married, the customs of [Thalai Deepavali, a couple's first Deepavali](/blog/thalai-deepavali-newlyweds), are another place where these small rituals begin.

## Can the pooja be done at a temple in India?

Yes, and many families far from home do exactly that. The old way is to ask a relative to take an archana in your names at the family temple. Parents often love being asked. You can also have a sankalpam made in both names, with a coconut offering at the shrine. [Why a coconut is broken in worship](/blog/why-break-a-coconut) explains the symbolism.

Some couples choose a Swayamvara Parvathi homam, traditionally associated with marriage. People often think of it only as a prayer for finding a partner, but the tradition asks for more than that: harmony in a marriage that already exists. The story behind it is told in [the story of Swayamvara Parvathi](/blog/swayamvara-parvathi-story).

## One thing to carry into the week

The Tirukkural's chapter on domestic life, Ilvazhkai, treats the household as a dharma of its own, not a lesser path than the forest or the monastery. Valluvar did not think marriage needed to be escaped to be holy. It only needed to be lived well.

So here is one small thing. This week, at the lamp, say one thing you are grateful for about your spouse out loud. Not in a card, not in a message. Out loud, with the flame between you. It takes ten seconds, and it is harder than it sounds.
