Mula means "root", and the goddess of Mula nakshatra is Nirriti, who presides over dissolution. People hear that and worry, especially when a baby in the family is born under Moolam, as it is called in Tamil. But ask a gardener. Nothing grows without first going down into the dark. The root star is about depth, not doom.

## Mula nakshatra: Nirriti, Ketu and a bundle of roots

Like each of the twenty-seven nakshatras, the star groups the Moon crosses every month, Mula has a presiding power and a ruling planet. Its power is Nirriti, a Vedic goddess of decay and dissolution. Its planet is Ketu, the shadow planet associated with detachment, endings and insight. The star opens Dhanu, the sign of the archer; [Dhanu rasi](/blog/dhanu-rasi) has more on the sign.

Its symbol is a bundle of roots tied together, and in some texts a lion's tail. Roots are what nobody sees and everything depends on. A lion's tail is the part that trails behind, easy to ignore until the lion turns.

A star with roots for its symbol gets people to match, or so the tradition says: searching, direct, philosophical, drawn to the root of things. They are said to want to know why, not only what. That is a temperament, not a timetable.

The star follows [Jyeshtha, the star of the eldest](/blog/jyeshtha-nakshatra), which carries a similar reputation.

## Why do people fear Moolam, and what does the root really mean?

The fear comes mostly from folk sayings. The best known says that a Moolam bride brings misfortune to her father-in-law. You will hear it in matchmaking, and some families still refuse matches because of it. These sayings are custom rather than scripture. Many astrologers say so plainly, and many families have set them aside without fuss.

Nirriti herself is worth a second look. In the Vedas she is feared, yes, but for a reason: she is the one who removes what has decayed. Every garden needs her. Compost is her work. So is the autumn that clears the branches for spring. A tradition that honoured only growth and never decay would be a tradition that did not understand growth.

There is another thread that turns the fear upside down. Tamil tradition remembers Hanuman's birth on Moolam, in the month of Margazhi, and Tamil Hanuman Jayanti is kept then. The festival is described in [the significance of Hanuman Jayanti](/blog/hanuman-jayanti-significance). Of all the gods, the one said to be born on the "feared" star is the one people pray to for courage.

The story says strength was born on Moolam. The story is really about uprooting fear. Hanuman does not avoid the dark places; he leaps into them, across the ocean, into Lanka, into the fire. A root does the same. It goes down, and that is how the tree stands up.

## Temple, star day and protection

The shrine usually named for Mula is the Singeeswarar temple at Mappedu, near Perambakkam. An archana, flowers offered while the priest recites the deity's names, can be done there or at a Shiva or Hanuman temple near you.

Because Ketu rules the star, Ganesha and Hanuman are the deities families most often turn to. Ganesha is said to steady Ketu, and Hanuman lends courage. More on this in [how Ketu is worshipped](/blog/ketu-graha-worship). On the monthly Moolam day, your janma nakshatra, the Moon's monthly visit to your birth star, a lamp and a short prayer for courage are enough.

Moolam comes back in your birth month once a year, and that star birthday is a good day for clearing out, in whatever sense you need. The next star, [Purva Ashadha, the waters' star](/blog/purva-ashadha-nakshatra), follows the roots with water, which is exactly the right order.

## Pull out one weed

On your Mula day, pull out one weed. It might be a habit you have been meaning to drop, a grudge you have been carrying, or a drawer of old papers you have not opened in years. Do the actual work of clearing it: throw away the papers, make the call, say the sentence.

Notice how much lighter the space feels afterwards. That is Nirriti's gift. The root star is not a curse. It is an invitation to go deeper.
