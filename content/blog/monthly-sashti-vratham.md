Sashti viratham is a small fast kept on the sixth lunar day of each month for Murugan. If the six-day Skanda Sashti feels like a lot, one day a month is a gentler place to start. You choose how strictly to fast, you light a lamp in the morning, you recite one hymn at dusk, and you break the fast with a simple meal. Nothing more is needed. What it gives you is a thread through the year rather than one intense week.

## What is monthly Sashti viratham?

Sashti means the sixth. In each lunar month, the sixth day after the new moon, in the waxing fortnight, is traditionally associated with Murugan, also called Skanda, Kartikeya or Subrahmanya. The great one is Skanda Sashti in the Tamil month of Aippasi, which ends with Soorasamharam, the day Murugan defeats the asura Surapadman. Families who keep that full six-day fast are well served by the [Skanda Sashti fasting guide](/blog/skanda-sashti-fasting-guide).

The monthly day is the quieter cousin. It keeps the thread going between one Skanda Sashti and the next. Many devotees in Tamil Nadu keep it as a matter of habit, the way others keep Ekadashi or a Monday fast. It is also a natural day to think of the six abodes of Murugan, the Arupadai Veedu; if you have a connection to one of them, the [Palani Murugan temple guide](/blog/palani-murugan-temple) is a good place to begin.

## How do you keep a Sashti fast?

First, choose a level you can keep. There is no single correct version.

- **Fruit and milk** through the day, with a proper meal after the evening prayer.
- **One simple meal** at midday, nothing after until the prayer.
- **No snacking**, three ordinary vegetarian meals and nothing in between.

Any of these counts. The food tradition for Sashti viratham is simple and vegetarian: many families avoid onion and garlic on the day and break the fast with rice, a plain kuzhambu or rasam, and curd.

Then the day itself:

1. **Morning.** Bathe, light a lamp before a picture of Murugan, and say his name. Even one line counts: Om Saravana Bhava, the six-syllable mantra of Murugan.
2. **Through the day.** Notice hunger when it comes. Do not fight it or feed it at once. Watch it for a minute.
3. **Dusk.** Light the lamp again. Recite the Kanda Sashti Kavasam, the armour hymn composed by Devaraya Swamigal, or chant Om Saravana Bhava for a few minutes. To understand what you are singing, read [the Kanda Sashti Kavasam's meaning](/blog/kanda-sashti-kavasam-meaning) line by line.
4. **After the prayer.** Break the fast with a simple vegetarian meal, eaten slowly.

## Why does a small monthly fast work?

A fast that comes once a year is an event. A fast that comes once a month becomes a practice. Twelve times is enough to notice a pattern: how hunger behaves at eleven in the morning, how your mood shifts around four, how the evening prayer feels when the stomach is light. You start to learn your own body, which is most of what a fast is for.

It also does not exhaust you. Six days of strict fasting can wear a person down, especially with a job and a family. One day, at a level you have chosen, is sustainable. Many people find the monthly Sashti becomes a small anchor, a day that belongs to attention rather than to appetite.

A clear word on health. The tradition has always exempted children, the elderly and the unwell. If you are pregnant, diabetic, unwell or on medication, do not fast; keep the lamp and the hymn instead, and talk to your doctor before changing how you eat. The devotion is in the attention, not in the empty stomach.

If a lunar rhythm suits you, [Masik Shivaratri](/blog/masik-shivaratri), the monthly night for Shiva near the new moon, and [a Pournami pooja at home](/blog/pournami-pooja-at-home) on the full moon fall at other points in the same month.

## Try this for three months

Open the panchangam and mark the next three Sashti days in your calendar, with a reminder the evening before. On each one, keep one simple meal, light the lamp morning and evening, and recite one hymn at dusk. Write two lines afterwards: what the hunger was like, what the prayer was like.

By the third month, read the notes back. Notice what changed, in the body, in the mood, in how easily you sat down at dusk. That noticing is the fast doing its work.
