On Friday evenings my mother in Chennai still lights the lamp at six, and in many of the homes on her street the kolam at the front door is drawn a little larger, a little more carefully, than on other days. When I was a boy I thought it was simply because the weekend was coming. It was only much later that I learned that in Jyotish, Friday belongs to Shukra, Venus, the planet of beauty. The traditional shukra graha remedies follow from that: a Lakshmi prayer, white flowers, field beans given away, and a clean, beautiful home. Of all the planetary remedies, these are the ones you can see from the street.

## Who is Shukra in Jyotish?

In Jyotish, Shukra stands for beauty, art, music, comfort, love, marriage, pleasure and the good things of life. He is traditionally associated with Friday, the colour white, field beans, which Tamil homes call mochai, and the diamond. In many South Indian homes, Friday worship goes to Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity and grace, as his linked deity.

But Shukra is more interesting than a planet of comforts. In the Puranas he is Shukracharya, the guru of the asuras, the demons, just as Brihaspati is guru of the gods. He is brilliant, loyal to his students and the keeper of the Mrita Sanjeevani, the knowledge that brings the dead back to life. The gods' teacher gets Thursday. The demons' teacher gets Friday. The tradition gives both a day of the week, and I have always liked that.

## Why did Shukracharya lose an eye?

The story is from the Vamana avatar in the Bhagavata Purana, told in full in [the Vamana three steps story](/blog/vamana-three-steps-story): King Bali, Shukracharya's student, promised three paces of land to a small Brahmin boy who was Vishnu in disguise, and would not go back on his word though his guru warned him. The part that belongs to Shukracharya comes next. As Bali lifted his water pot to pour the water that would seal the gift, Shukracharya made himself tiny and blocked the spout. Vamana cleared it with a blade of darbha grass, and the grass pierced Shukracharya's eye.

I read this story differently at different ages. As a child I thought Shukracharya was the villain. Now I see a teacher who knew exactly what was coming and tried, at real cost, to protect a student who would not be protected. Loyalty has a price. Sometimes it is an eye.

## Shukra graha remedies on Fridays

The Friday practices are simple and, fittingly, pleasant.

- **A Lakshmi prayer and lamp** on Friday evening, often with the Mahalakshmi Ashtakam, whose verses are explained in [the Mahalakshmi Ashtakam's meaning](/blog/mahalakshmi-ashtakam-meaning).
- **White flowers**, jasmine above all, offered before the lamp.
- **Field beans or rice** given to someone in need, or cooked as sundal.
- **A clean and beautiful home.** Sweeping the threshold and drawing a fresh kolam; the old reasons for this are explained in [the meaning of kolam and rangoli](/blog/kolam-and-rangoli-meaning).
- **Om Shukraya Namah**, chanted on Friday mornings.

Shukra's Navagraha temple is the Agniswarar temple at Kanjanur, near Kumbakonam. Here, unusually, there is no separate Shukra idol in the main tradition; Shiva himself is worshipped as Shukra. Devotees come on Fridays, often with white flowers, to pray for harmony at home and in marriage.

My wife, who is not especially interested in planets, keeps a very simple version of this in our New Jersey townhouse. On Friday, the counter is cleared, the lamp is wiped, and there are flowers from the grocery store in a small brass pot. Nobody announces it. The house just feels different on Friday evening. There is a practical ten-minute routine in [the Friday Lakshmi pooja piece](/blog/friday-lakshmi-pooja) if you would like a starting point.

## One corner, a little more beautiful

We usually speak of a good home in terms of love and patience. Shukra adds something we tend to forget: that beauty, too, is a kind of care. A clean threshold, a flower, a lamp that has been wiped. None of these costs much. All of them say someone is paying attention.

One small thing to carry into this week: this Friday, make one corner of your home a little more beautiful than it was, and light a lamp there. Then notice who in the house notices.
