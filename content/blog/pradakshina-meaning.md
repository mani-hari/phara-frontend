Pradakshina is the clockwise walk around a shrine that keeps the deity on your right. It looks like a formality, something you do because the person ahead of you is doing it. But walking a circle around one point is a very old way of telling the mind where the centre is. The pradakshina meaning is right there in the shape: you move, and the centre does not. Try it with attention once and the walk stops being a queue and starts being a practice.

## Pradakshina meaning: forward, and to the right

The word comes from Sanskrit. Pra means forward, and dakshina means right, and also south. So pradakshina is moving forward with something kept on your right side. In practice, that means circling clockwise, with the sanctum always on your right hand. In the old way of thinking, the right side is the side of respect. You keep what you honour on your right, the way you would walk beside an elder.

South Indian temples are built for this walk. Around the innermost shrine runs the first prakaram, a covered corridor, and around that another, and in the great temples another again, like rings in a tree. Devotees walk the inner corridor after darshan, passing the smaller shrines set into the walls, the flagstaff, the sacred tree. The architecture assumes you will circle. Why walk clockwise in the temple? Because the whole building is designed so that walking clockwise brings you back, again and again, to the centre.

## Why walk in a circle at all?

Think about what a circle does. When you walk in a straight line, you are going somewhere. When you walk in a circle around one point, you are not going anywhere. The destination is already in the middle. Your feet move, the scenery changes, but the thing that matters stays exactly where it was.

That is also a fair description of what the mind is supposed to do in worship. Most of the time, attention circles many points: work, family, the phone, tomorrow. Pradakshina gives it one point to circle. Many people find that after three slow rounds, the chatter has thinned.

The story of [Ganesha, Kartikeya and the mango](/blog/ganesha-kartikeya-mango-story) makes the same point: while his brother raced around the world on a peacock, Ganesha walked around his parents and called them his whole world. The centre you circle is the world you choose.

The largest pradakshina of all is giri pradakshina, the walk around a sacred hill. At Tiruvannamalai, devotees walk barefoot around Arunachala, the hill worshipped as Shiva himself, especially on full-moon nights. The circuit takes hours. It is the same act as circling a small shrine, stretched across a whole landscape. If you cannot go, [keeping the Tiruvannamalai Deepam from afar](/blog/tiruvannamalai-deepam-from-afar) has ways to join in at home.

## How many pradakshina should you do, and how should you walk?

The common custom is three rounds. Many families prefer an odd number: one, three, five, seven, or more for special vows. At Navagraha shrines, where the nine planetary deities stand together, there are often specific counts, and local custom varies from temple to temple. Ask the priest or watch the regulars. [Navagraha temple worship](/blog/navagraha-temple-worship-guide) covers those counts in detail.

How many rounds you walk matters less than how you walk them. Walk slowly. Keep your hands folded, or hold a flower. Do not talk. Do not check your phone between rounds. Feel your feet on the stone. Many people chant a name softly, one repetition per few steps. If you pass someone who walks slower, go around them gently; it is not a race.

## A pradakshina around yourself

Many families do atma pradakshina at home, a pradakshina around oneself. Stand before your home shrine, fold your hands, and turn slowly in place, clockwise, three times. That is all. Each morning for a week, do this before you light the lamp. Give each step your full attention: the lift, the turn, the placing of the foot. It takes less than a minute. At the end of the week, notice whether the turning has become the moment you arrive. Families circle a new car in the driveway with the same care during a [vahana pooja for a new car](/blog/vahana-pooja-new-car); the centre just happens to have wheels.
