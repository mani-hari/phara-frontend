Thursday is Guruvara, the teacher's day. In Chennai I remember Thursday evening queues outside the Sai Baba temple near our street, and small lamps glowing at the Dakshinamurthy shrine in the Shiva temple. Here in New Jersey I see the same thing in a different light: Sai temples filling with families straight from work on Thursday evenings, office lanyards still around their necks. A Thursday Sai Baba pooja at home needs very little: a clean corner, a lamp, yellow flowers, a simple sweet and a few quiet minutes. Behind all of it is one idea. Thursday belongs to teachers.

## Why is Thursday the Guru's day?

In the Indian calendar Thursday is Guruvara, the day of Brihaspati, the planet Jupiter, who in the Puranas is the guru of the gods. So Thursday became the day for teachers of every kind. The planetary side is in [Guru graha worship and Jupiter](/blog/guru-graha-thursday-worship).

In practice, families turn to different teachers on this day:

- **Dakshinamurthy**, Shiva as the silent teacher, seated under a banyan tree facing south, instructing sages without a word.
- **Sai Baba of Shirdi**, the saint who lived in a mosque, kept a fire burning and answered visitors with stories. Thursday is the main day of worship in Sai temples everywhere.
- **Guru Raghavendra**, the saint of Mantralayam, for many Kannada and Tamil families.

Some families keep Dakshinamurthy, some keep Sai Baba. In our home we keep both, and our son thinks this is completely normal.

## A Thursday Sai Baba pooja at home

Here is a simple version that fits a weekday.

1. **Clean the corner.** Wipe the shelf or table where you keep the picture of Sai Baba. A clean yellow cloth under it is traditional.
2. **Light a lamp**, ghee if you have it, oil if not. Light incense if you like.
3. **Offer yellow flowers**, marigolds or chrysanthemums, and a simple sweet: a little kesari, a piece of jaggery, or even a banana.
4. **Read a little.** Many devotees read one chapter of the Sai Satcharitra, the account of Sai Baba's life, each Thursday, working through the book over the months.
5. **Aarti.** Wave the lamp or camphor in front of the picture, singing or simply watching the flame.
6. **Prasadam.** Share the sweet with the household. If you have udi, the sacred ash from Shirdi, put a small pinch on your forehead. My wife keeps ours in the drawer by the front door; [Shirdi and the meaning of udi](/blog/shirdi-sai-baba-udi) tells where it comes from.

Some families keep the nine-Thursday vrat, a vow of nine consecutive Thursdays with a simple fast, a pooja and an act of giving on the last day. If you try it, keep the fast gentle and skip it if your health does not allow.

## Can Thursday be about your own teachers?

It can, and I think it should be. Dakshinamurthy is the teacher who teaches in silence, and the traditional offerings to him on Thursdays, yellow cloth and chickpeas, are simple enough for any home. But the day also asks a quieter question: who taught you?

The Guru Gita calls the teacher Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva. That verse used to embarrass me slightly, because my teachers were ordinary people with chalk dust on their sleeves. Now I think that is exactly the point. The divine came to me through ordinary people who took the trouble.

Fitting this into an American workweek is easier than it sounds. Thursday evenings are usually lighter than Mondays. Some of my colleagues in the Sai Baba community meet for bhajans on Thursday nights and are home by nine. Others simply light a lamp before dinner. If you cannot manage a full pooja, a [five-minute daily puja](/blog/five-minute-daily-puja) works on any evening, and [which god is worshipped on which day](/blog/which-god-on-which-day) covers the rest of the week.

## One name, said aloud

Sai Baba is often remembered for two words: shraddha and saburi, faith and patience. Those are also the two things every good teacher gave me, usually without saying so.

One small thing to carry into this week: on Thursday evening, light a lamp and say one teacher's name aloud. The saint, or the schoolteacher, or both. My son now does this with me. Last week the name he chose was his soccer coach, which I think Sai Baba would have enjoyed.
