A Pournami pooja at home can begin with the simplest step there is. At moonrise, go outside, or to a window, and look at the full moon for two minutes. Then come in and light a lamp. That is the whole beginning. Everything else, the offering, the hymn, the light meal, builds on those two minutes. Try it for three full moons before you decide whether it means anything to you.

## What makes the full moon special in the tradition?

Pournami is the full-moon day, the fifteenth day of the bright fortnight, when the moon rises at sunset and stays up all night. Almost every Hindu calendar treats it as a day of fullness and completion. Some of the best-loved observances of the year fall on it: Chitra Pournami in the Tamil month of Chithirai, and Guru Purnima, the full moon honouring teachers. Chitra Pournami has a story of its own, told in [what Chitra Pournami means](/blog/chitra-pournami-meaning).

In Tiruvannamalai, full-moon nights bring Girivalam, walking barefoot around the sacred hill of Arunachala, a circuit of about fourteen kilometres that thousands of people make after dark. Many homes also keep Pournami for a longer family observance: a Satyanarayan pooja, or a reading of the Lalitha Sahasranamam, the thousand names of the Goddess. If you already do one of these, the full moon is its natural home. The [Satyanarayan puja at home](/blog/satyanarayan-puja-at-home) fits the evening well.

But you do not need any of that to start. The moon itself is the invitation.

## Pournami pooja at home, step by step

Keep it simple enough that you will actually do it next month.

1. **Through the day, eat lightly.** A full fast is one option. Many people keep simple food instead: fruit and milk until evening, or one plain meal at midday. The point is to arrive at dusk not heavy. If you are pregnant, unwell or on medication, skip the fast and keep the rest.
2. **Before moonrise, bathe and tidy the shrine.** Wipe the lamp, change the water, clear the dead flowers. Five minutes is enough.
3. **At moonrise, step outside.** Two minutes, eyes on the moon, breathing slowly. No phone.
4. **Come in and light the lamp.** Ghee or sesame oil, a cotton wick.
5. **Offer something white or sweet.** Milk, rice kheer, or a plate of fruit. White is traditional for Chandra, the moon.
6. **Recite one hymn.** The Lalitha Sahasranamam if you know it; if not, a short Lakshmi or Shiva verse, or simply Om Namah Shivaya eleven times.
7. **Sit by a window or outdoors for a few more minutes.** Let the moonlight be part of the pooja.
8. **Break the fast** with a simple meal, eaten slowly.

A full moon pooja at home does not need a priest, a long list of items, or perfect Sanskrit. It needs you to turn up at the same hour, month after month.

## What does the body notice on a full-moon evening?

Many people report that they sleep more lightly around the full moon, or feel restless, or find the mind busier than usual. Whether or not the moon causes it, the evening is a good one to be gentle with yourself. The tradition's instinct, a lighter dinner, a quiet evening, time outdoors, happens to suit exactly that restlessness.

Notice what the light fast does too. By late afternoon you may feel hunger arrive in waves and then pass. By moonrise, attention is often sharper. A light, [sattvic diet](/blog/sattvic-diet-explained) through the day, food that leaves the mind clear rather than dull, makes the evening easier. Then eat dinner early, skip the screen after the lamp, and see how you sleep.

This is also why Pournami pairs well with the other monthly observances. If a lunar rhythm starts to suit you, the [monthly Sashti viratham for Murugan](/blog/monthly-sashti-vratham) and [Masik Shivaratri, the monthly night for Shiva](/blog/masik-shivaratri) fall at different points in the same cycle.

## Try this for three full moons

Check the panchangam for the next Pournami and mark it. On the day: two minutes outside at moonrise, one lamp, one hymn, a light meal. That is all. Do it three times.

By the third full moon, notice what has changed. Most people find that the month begins to have a shape, a point it swells towards and a point it turns from. You start to see the moon on ordinary nights and know roughly where you are in the cycle. That awareness is the real offering. The kheer is just the part you get to eat.
