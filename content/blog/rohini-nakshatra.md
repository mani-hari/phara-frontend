The Moon married twenty-seven sisters and loved only one of them. Her name was Rohini, and that favouritism, and the curse it earned, is the oldest story told about Rohini nakshatra. It is also the reason Krishna's birth star is said to be the Moon's favourite home. The story sounds like a scandal in the sky. It is really a story about attention.

## Why did the Moon love Rohini best?

Rohini's presiding god is Brahma, often called Prajapati, the creator, and its ruling planet is Chandra, the Moon itself, which already tells you whose star this is.

The Puranas tell how Daksha, a son of Brahma, gave twenty-seven daughters in marriage to Chandra. Each daughter was a nakshatra, a lunar mansion, and the Moon was meant to spend one night in each house as he crossed the sky. But he lingered with Rohini. She was the most beautiful, the most charming, and the Moon kept finding reasons to stay.

The other sisters complained to their father. Daksha warned Chandra once, then again. When nothing changed, he cursed the Moon to waste away. Chandra began to shrink, night after night. The gods intervened, Shiva among them, and the curse was softened: the Moon would waste and grow in turns, forever. That, the story says, is why the Moon waxes and wanes.

In Jyotish, the Moon is said to be exalted in Rishabha, the sign in which Rohini lies, and Rohini is counted its happiest home. [Rishabha rasi](/blog/rishabha-rasi), the sign of the bull, is the place to go next.

## Rohini nakshatra and the child born at midnight

The Bhagavata Purana places Krishna's birth on the eighth day of the waning moon, Ashtami, with the Moon in Rohini. It was midnight, in a prison cell in Mathura, during a storm. Of all the stars available to a god choosing his entry, he chose the one the Moon could not stop visiting.

This is why Krishna Janmashtami and Rohini are so closely tied in the tradition, and why some families watch for the Moon in Rohini when they fix the festival. [Keeping Krishna Janmashtami at home](/blog/krishna-janmashtami-at-home) covers the festival itself.

Rohini's symbol is a cart or chariot, something that carries goods to market and brings them home: growth, abundance, the harvest that arrives. A star with a cart for its symbol gets a reputation to match: Rohini-born people are said to be charming, steady, fond of beauty, comfort and good food, and good at making things grow. None of it is binding.

## A messenger's temple for a cowherd's star

Rohini's temple in the Tamil star lists is the Pandava Thoodha Perumal temple in Kanchipuram. Thoodha means messenger. Here Krishna stands as the envoy he became before the Kurukshetra war, when he went to the Kaurava court to ask for peace, even five villages, on behalf of the Pandavas. The cowherd who stole butter became the diplomat who tried to stop a war.

Families who keep a Rohini connection often treat the monthly star day, the janma nakshatra, as a day for Krishna: a small archana (an offering of names with flowers), a little butter and a few tulsi leaves on the altar. Because Chandra rules the star, some also keep Monday prayers for the Moon, as [worshipping Chandra](/blog/chandra-graha-worship).

Many families mark the yearly Rohini in the birth month, the star birthday, as a family occasion rather than a private one. It suits Rohini, which was always a sociable star. The star before it is [Krittika, the star of fire](/blog/krittika-nakshatra), and the one after is [Mrigashira, the seeker's star](/blog/mrigashira-nakshatra).

## Keeping Rohini fairly

Notice what the curse was for. Daksha did not punish the Moon for loving Rohini. He punished it for neglecting the others. Attention was the thing being rationed, and it was rationed badly.

So on your Rohini day, give an hour of undivided attention to someone who rarely gets it: an older relative, a quiet colleague, a child who has stopped asking. No phone on the table. The Moon learned to share its light the hard way. The star is best kept by sharing it on purpose.
