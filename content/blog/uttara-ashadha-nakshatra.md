My son, who is about eleven, finished a science project at midnight on a school night and announced, half asleep, that he had "won by not quitting." I told him there is a whole star for that. Uttara Ashadha nakshatra, Uthiradam in Tamil, is the twenty-first of the twenty-seven birth stars, and tradition calls it the star of final victory: not the win that comes in the first rush, but the one that is still standing when everyone else has gone home. He was unimpressed. He wanted toast. But I kept thinking about it on the train the next morning.

## Uttara Ashadha nakshatra: all the gods, the Sun and a tusk

A nakshatra is one of the twenty-seven star groups the Moon passes through each month, and your birth star is the one it occupied when you were born. Each has a presiding deity, a ruling planet and a symbol. For Uttara Ashadha nakshatra the deity is unusual: not one god but the Vishvedevas, the universal gods, the whole assembly invoked together in the Vedas. Its ruling planet, or adhipati, is Surya, the Sun. Its symbols are an elephant's tusk and the planks of a bed. It spans two signs: its first quarter lies in Dhanu, the archer, and the rest in [Makara rasi](/blog/makara-rasi).

Tradition describes Uthiradam-born people as principled, patient and quietly determined, slow to start and hard to stop. I read that kind of portrait the way I read a character sketch in a novel. It is a description the tradition offers, not a destiny it hands you. If you are not sure of your own star, [how to find your nakshatra](/blog/how-to-find-your-nakshatra) takes a birth time and place and does the rest, and anyone wondering why the sky gives two answers can read [Vedic vs Western astrology](/blog/vedic-vs-western-astrology).

## What does it take to win in the end?

The tusk is the clue. In the popular telling, Ganesha broke off his own tusk to keep writing the Mahabharata as Vyasa dictated, rather than let the poem pause; [the story of Ganesha's broken tusk](/blog/ganesha-broken-tusk-story) tells it properly.

What moves me is that the victory in that story is not a battle. It is a manuscript finished. Most of our victories are like that: a degree completed at night after the children sleep, a loan paid off, a difficult parent looked after for years. Nobody applauds the last page. Uthiradam is the star of the last page.

Avvaiyar's Vinayagar Agaval, the long Tamil hymn to Ganesha, is sung in many homes each morning for steadiness, for a mind that does not wander from its purpose. I learned it as a boy without understanding a word. Now I understand only a few lines, and they are enough. If you want to meet the poet herself, I have gathered a few of [Avvaiyar's wisdom sayings](/blog/avvaiyar-wisdom-sayings), most of them short enough to learn between two train stops.

## Temple, star day and Ganapathy

The Brahmapureeswarar temple at Keezha Poongudi, near Sivaganga in Tamil Nadu, is the temple the widely shared Tamil star list pairs with Uttara Ashadha. Uthiradam people make the trip when they are in India; I know a family in Houston who plan their visits home around it.

Because the Sun rules this star, Sunday prayers to Surya are a natural fit: a little water offered towards the morning sun, a line of the Gayatri, a moment facing east. Because of the tusk, Ganesha is close to Uthiradam people too. On Uthiradam day each month, a short Ganapathy archana, the offering of his names with flowers, or a coconut broken before his image, is a simple way to mark it.

The Uthiradam of your birth month is your star birthday, which many South Indian families keep more carefully than the calendar one, sometimes with a homam for long life, as described in [the star birthday and the ayushya homam](/blog/ayushya-homam-star-birthday). For Uthiradam, many families begin the day with a coconut for Ganesha, the remover of obstacles, before anything else. The panchangam, the traditional almanac, gives the day.

## One small thing for this week

My son's project got a decent grade. What stayed with me was not the grade but the moment at midnight when he could have left it undone and did not.

This week, finish one thing you started and left. A letter, a drawer, a book with a bookmark stuck at chapter four. Uthiradam's victory is not the loudest; it is the last one standing.
