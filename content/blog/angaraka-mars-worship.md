One story says that while Shiva sat in deep meditation, a single drop of sweat fell from his brow to the earth. Bhumi, the Earth goddess, took the child who rose from it and raised him as her own. He was red, fierce and restless, and he became Angaraka, the planet Mars, also called Bhauma, son of Bhumi. Fire from the god, raised by the ground: that is why Mars in the tradition is fiery and grounded at once. Most angaraka dosha remedies, the traditional practices for a troubled Mars, make sense once you see him that way. They are not ways of putting out the fire. They are ways of giving it somewhere to land.

## Who is Angaraka?

In Jyotish, Angaraka stands for courage, energy, drive, siblings, land, property and blood. He is pictured red-bodied, riding a ram, carrying weapons, a warrior among the nine grahas. He is traditionally associated with Tuesday, the colour red, toor dal and red coral. In Tamil practice his linked deity is Murugan, the young warrior god, and Tuesday, Sevvai in Tamil, is Murugan's day as well as Mars's.

A strong Mars in a chart is read as courage and the energy to act; a troubled Mars as anger, rashness, quarrels or accidents. You can hear the pattern. The same heat that makes a good soldier makes a bad neighbour. The story says Angaraka was born of Shiva's sweat. The story is really about heat that needs a place to go.

Many people know Mars mainly through marriage matching and the idea of a Mars dosha. That is a separate topic, and it has its own careful piece: [Mangal dosha explained](/blog/mangal-dosha-explained).

## The healing at Vaitheeswaran Koil

Angaraka's Navagraha temple is Vaitheeswaran Koil, near Sirkazhi in Tamil Nadu. The presiding deity is Shiva as Vaidyanatha, the divine physician, with the Goddess as Thaiyal Nayaki, who is said to carry medicinal oil.

The legend is that Angaraka suffered from a skin disease that nothing could cure. He came here, worshipped Vaidyanatha, bathed in the temple tank, and was healed. So he has his own shrine within the temple, and devotees pray there on Tuesdays and bathe in or sprinkle themselves with water from the tank, called Siddhamirtham.

Notice the shape of this story. The warrior planet, all fire and weapons, is not defeated by a bigger warrior. He is healed by a physician. The tradition could have told a story of Mars being tamed. Instead it told a story of Mars being cared for.

## Which angaraka dosha remedies does tradition suggest?

The practices are Tuesday practices, and they are simple.

- **Tuesday prayers to Murugan**, at a temple if one is near, or before a picture at home.
- **The Skanda Sashti Kavacham**, the Tamil hymn of protection to Murugan, chanted on Tuesdays. There is a line-by-line guide to [the Kanda Sashti Kavasam's meaning](/blog/kanda-sashti-kavasam-meaning).
- **Om Angarakaya Namah**, chanted nine or twenty-seven times.
- **Red flowers** offered, such as red oleander or hibiscus.
- **Toor dal or red cloth** given to someone in need.
- **A lamp in sesame oil** lit at home on Tuesday evening.

Read these as ways to channel energy rather than to fear it. Chanting a hymn of protection takes steadiness. Giving away dal is an act of care. A lamp asks you to sit still for a moment. Each one takes the fire and gives it work. There is a fuller picture of how families actually keep the day in [Tuesday worship of Murugan and Durga](/blog/tuesday-worship-murugan-durga).

## Give the soldier honest work

In the stories, Mars is not a villain. He is a soldier: loyal, protective, sometimes too quick with his weapon. Nobody fixes a soldier by shaming him. You give him a clear task and something worth protecting.

If Mars looms large in your own chart, or you simply notice a lot of heat in your days, try a Tuesday practice for a few weeks. Light the lamp. Say the hymn. Give something away. And then take one hard thing you have been avoiding and do it, since courage is his domain too.
