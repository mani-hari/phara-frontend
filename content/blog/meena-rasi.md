When my father came home from hospital after his knee operation, he asked for nothing except the window open and the evening chanting on. My brother told me this on a video call, and I could see it behind him: the old radio on the sill, the curtain moving, my father with his eyes half closed. He had spent his whole life organising things. Now he was letting the evening happen. I thought of Meena rasi, the last sign of the zodiac: two fish swimming in opposite directions, tied together, and somehow at peace.

## Meena rasi: two fish and Jupiter

Meena rasi is the twelfth and last sign. As with every rasi, what counts is the Moon's position at birth, which is why my mother always asks for a baby's birth time before anything else. It holds the last quarter of [Purva Bhadrapada, the one-footed god's star](/blog/purva-bhadrapada-nakshatra), all of [Uttara Bhadrapada, the star of deep calm](/blog/uttara-bhadrapada-nakshatra), and all of [Revati, the last star](/blog/revati-nakshatra). Its ruling planet is Guru, Jupiter, and Shukra, Venus, is said to be exalted here. We say Meenam. The Western Pisces covers roughly the same stretch of sky, shifted by the difference between the two zodiacs.

The symbol is two fish, tied together by a cord, swimming in opposite directions. As a boy I thought it looked like a fight. Now it looks to me like most marriages, most families, most lives: two directions held together by something neither fish chose.

## What does it mean to let go?

Tradition's portrait of Meena is compassionate, imaginative, spiritual and gentle, the person who feels other people's pain and forgives quickly. The shadow is escapism and over-giving, drifting away from difficulty, or pouring out so much that nothing is left. My family astrologer in Madurai once said Meena people make the best nurses and the worst accountants. I hold that the way I hold all his sayings: fondly, and as description rather than destiny.

The fish has its own story. In the Matsya Purana, a tiny fish swam into the cupped hands of King Manu as he washed in a river and begged to be saved. Manu kept it in a jar, then a pond, then a lake, then the sea, and each time it outgrew its home. At last it revealed itself as Vishnu and warned Manu of a coming flood. When the waters rose, the great fish towed Manu's boat to safety, carrying the seeds of the new world. It is the first of [the ten avatars of Vishnu](/blog/dashavatara-meaning).

The small fish that asked to be carried ended up carrying everyone. There is something in that about letting go: Manu did not try to keep the fish small.

The Bhagavad Gita puts the idea in one line, in its second chapter: "Karmanye vadhikaraste ma phaleshu kadachana." Your right is to the action alone, never to its fruits. Do the work, offer it, and let the result go where it goes. My father has been chanting that verse for as long as I can remember. I think he is only now living it.

## Temples and poojas traditionally linked through Guru and the fish

Jupiter rules Meena, so the family pilgrimage for the sign is usually to Alangudi near Kumbakonam, the Guru temple. Thursday is Guru's day, and many devotees stop before Dakshinamurthy, the silent teacher, on Thursdays. The piece on [how planetary transits are read](/blog/gochara-transits-explained) explains why Guru's movement matters so much to Meena families.

Many Meena families feel drawn to Madurai Meenakshi, the fish-eyed goddess, whose eyes, like a fish's, are said never to close as she watches over her devotees. This is a folk association rather than a rule, but it is a lovely one. Anyone who has not yet been can start with this [guide to the Madurai Meenakshi temple](/blog/madurai-meenakshi-temple).

## One small thing for this week

My father is walking again, slowly, with a stick he pretends not to need. The window is still open in the evenings.

One small thing to carry into this week: at the end of each day, say one sentence of thanks and let the rest go. The fish do not carry the river.
