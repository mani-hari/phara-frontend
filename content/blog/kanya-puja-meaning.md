Kanya puja meaning is simple to say and strange to experience: on the eighth or ninth day of Navratri, a household bows to its youngest girls as forms of the Goddess. I remember my sister at seven, sitting on a neighbour's floor with her feet in a steel plate, while an aunty we barely knew poured water over her toes and dabbed them dry with the end of her sari. I was the one boy in the row, seated at the end, puzzled and a little proud to be included. Mostly we were both waiting for the halwa. It took me many years to understand what she was doing, and why she did it with such care.

## Kanya puja meaning: the Goddess seen in a child

Navratri is nine nights of honouring the Goddess in her many forms, and the reason it lasts nine is its own story, told well in [why Navratri lasts nine nights](/blog/why-navratri-lasts-nine-nights). By Ashtami or Navami, after days of fasting and prayer, many families in North India turn from the image in the shrine to the children in the room. Girls, usually before their teens, are invited in, seated, and worshipped. Kanya means young girl. Kanya pujan on Ashtami or Navami is, in a sense, the festival stepping out of the altar and into the house.

The idea has a scriptural root. In the fifth chapter of the Devi Mahatmyam, the gods sing a hymn that returns again and again to one line: "Ya devi sarvabhuteshu", to the Goddess who dwells in all beings, we bow. The verse names her as consciousness, as hunger, as sleep, as compassion, as mother. If she is in all beings, then she is in the child who fidgets on your floor. Why girls are worshipped in Navratri comes down to that line. The ritual simply takes it at its word.

## What happens at a kanya puja?

The details vary by family, but the shape is familiar. Nine girls are the ideal, one for each form of Durga, and often a small boy is included too, seen as a form of Bhairava, the Goddess's guardian; that was my place in the row. The girls' feet are washed, sometimes in milk and water. A red thread, kalava, is tied on their wrists, a tilak placed on their foreheads, and a red chunni sometimes draped over their heads. Then comes the meal: puri, black chana and semolina halwa, served on a leaf or plate, eaten while the adults stand. At the end, each girl leaves with a small gift, a bangle, a coin, a notebook, a packet of sweets.

In Punjab and nearby regions the same custom is called kanjak, and kanjak puja meaning is no different. Children go from house to house on Ashtami morning, collecting little heaps of halwa and coins, and by noon they are sticky, exhausted and rich.

## Keeping the ritual meaningful today

Some of us look back on kanya puja with mixed feelings. It can be uncomfortable to honour girls for one morning in a world that does not always honour them the rest of the year. That discomfort is worth keeping. It is not a reason to drop the ritual, but a reason to do it with thought.

A few things help. Ask children, and their parents, whether they want to come; a child should never be positioned somewhere she does not want to be. Keep it light and warm, not solemn. Invite girls of every background, including neighbours who do not share your faith; in the US, many families host a small group from school or the temple, and the ideas in [celebrating Navratri in the USA](/blog/celebrate-navratri-in-the-usa) fit easily around it. Let the girls do something too, the way [festival rituals for kids](/blog/festival-rituals-for-kids) suggests, so they are participants and not ornaments. And let the next day matter: the [Ayudha Pooja](/blog/ayudha-pooja-meaning) that follows blesses tools and books, a natural moment to bless a girl's own schoolbag.

## One small thing to carry into the week

I think often about that aunty with her sari end. She was not making a speech about girls. She was simply acting as though the Goddess had sat down on her floor. My sister, now a mother herself, says she has never quite forgotten being treated that way.

So this is my small suggestion. Whatever you do on Ashtami, whether a full puja, a plate of halwa or nothing at all, ask one girl in your life what she wants to learn. Then help her do it. That, too, is a way of washing her feet.
