Diwali is not one night. It is a five-day arc, from Dhanteras to Bhai Dooj, and each day asks for something different from you: clean, wake early, light lamps, give thanks, reconnect. If you treat the five days of Diwali as one big party squeezed into a weekend, you will be tired by the end of it. If you treat them as five small practices, one a day, the festival lands differently in the body. It becomes a week of attention rather than a week of noise.

## The five days of Diwali at a glance

- **Dhanteras**, the thirteenth day of the waning moon: the festival begins with health and new beginnings.
- **Naraka Chaturdashi**, the fourteenth day, also called Chhoti Diwali: the pre-dawn oil bath, especially in the south.
- **Lakshmi Puja**, on Amavasya, the new-moon night: the main night of lamps.
- **Govardhan Puja**, the first day of the bright fortnight: gratitude to the land and to food.
- **Bhai Dooj**, the second day: the bond between brothers and sisters.

Regions stretch and shift these. In much of the south, Naraka Chaturdashi is Deepavali itself. Gujarat marks its new year on the fourth day. The shape, though, holds: preparation, purification, light, gratitude, family. For the stories behind the main night, see [why Diwali is celebrated](/blog/why-is-diwali-celebrated).

## What does each day ask of you?

**Dhanteras asks for health before wealth.** The word comes from dhana, wealth, and trayodashi, the thirteenth day. Many people buy gold or a steel vessel. But the older figure of the day is Dhanvantari, the physician of the gods, who the Puranas say rose from the churning ocean carrying a pot of amrita, the nectar of immortality. The tradition put the doctor before the goddess of wealth. That ordering is worth taking seriously.

**Naraka Chaturdashi asks you to wake early.** Before dawn, oil the body, bathe slowly, put on something clean. It is a reset for the nervous system after weeks of preparation. The Tamil version of this morning is described in [Naraka Chaturdashi and the Deepavali oil bath](/blog/naraka-chaturdashi-oil-bath).

**Lakshmi Puja asks you to light up the darkest night.** Lamps in every window, the house clean, the doors open. It is the one night of the lunar month without any moonlight, and the tradition answers it with a hundred small flames.

**Govardhan Puja asks for gratitude.** The Bhagavata Purana tells of Krishna lifting Govardhan hill on one finger to shelter the cowherds from Indra's storm. Many temples pile up a mountain of food, called annakut, as an offering. The practice is simple: notice what feeds you.

**Bhai Dooj asks you to reconnect.** The story says Yama, the lord of death, visited his sister Yamuna on this day, and she fed him and marked his forehead. Siblings do the same now, which is explored in [the meaning of Bhai Dooj](/blog/bhai-dooj-meaning).

## A simple practice for each day

Try this. It takes about fifteen minutes a day.

1. **Dhanteras:** clean one space thoroughly, a shelf, a drawer, the threshold. Draw a small kolam or rangoli, a pattern of rice flour or coloured powder, at the door if you like; [why we draw kolam and rangoli](/blog/kolam-and-rangoli-meaning) explains what it is for. Do one thing for your health: a walk, an early night.
2. **Naraka Chaturdashi:** wake an hour earlier than usual. Warm some sesame oil, rub it into your scalp and body, wait a few minutes, then bathe. Notice how the day feels different when it starts slowly.
3. **Lakshmi Puja:** at dusk, before switching on the lights, light your lamps one by one. Sit with them for five minutes with the phone in another room. The practice in [the dusk lamp ritual](/blog/the-dusk-lamp-ritual) works well here.
4. **Govardhan Puja:** feed someone. Cook for a neighbour, share sweets with a colleague, give food to someone who needs it.
5. **Bhai Dooj:** call a sibling, or someone who has been like one. Not a text. A call.

## The whole festival on one note

Write the five days on a sticky note and put it on your fridge: clean, wake, light, feed, call. Do one small act each day. Skip the parts that do not fit your life this season, and keep the ones that do. At the end of the five days, notice how your body feels compared with the Diwali weeks you rushed through. That difference is the festival.
