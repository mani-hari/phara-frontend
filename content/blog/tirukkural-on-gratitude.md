On a Sunday video call, I watched my father ease his knee before he sat down to talk, one hand on the armrest, lowering himself slowly, and I suddenly saw every year he had spent standing up for us. That was the day a line from the Thirukkural on gratitude stopped sounding like exaggeration: help given when nothing was owed cannot be repaid, even if you offered the earth and the heavens in exchange.

## Seynandri Arithal (Gratitude): what the chapter says

The chapter is called Seynandri Arithal, knowing the good that was done to you. It sits in the first book of the Tirukkural, the book of virtue, and it opens with the couplet I was thinking of. Kural 101:

*Seyyaamal seydha udhavikku vaiyagamum*\
*vaanagamum aatral aridhu.*

For help given without any prior help received, even the earth and the sky together would not be enough in return.

The next couplets widen the idea. Kural 102 says a kindness done at the right moment, however small, is greater than the world. Another says that help given without weighing the return is larger than the sea. So there are three kinds of help Valluvar singles out: help given unasked, help given in time, and help given without calculation. None of them is measured by its size. A glass of water at the right moment outweighs a fortune at the wrong one.

## Thirukkural on gratitude across an ocean

Living in New Jersey, gratitude has a peculiar shape. The people I owe most are mostly on the other side of the world, and the debts are the kind you cannot send by wire transfer.

My parents paid for college with money they did not really have. When I think of it now, what I remember is not the fees but my father taking the early bus to save the auto fare. There is a neighbour in Chennai who drove my mother to the hospital one night before my brother could get there, while I was eight thousand miles away, asleep. And there is my first manager here, who spent a whole Saturday showing a nervous new arrival how things actually worked, and never mentioned it again.

I cannot repay any of them. Valluvar knew that. That is exactly why he says the help cannot be repaid: the point of the chapter is not settling accounts. Gratitude, in the Kural, is remembering. It is holding the kindness in view so that it keeps shaping how you live. The old teachers who say the world is [maya, a kind of illusion](/blog/maya-meaning-illusion), are warning us about things that look solid and are not; a kindness remembered is one of the few things that turns out to be more solid than it looks.

## Why does Valluvar call ingratitude the hardest failing to undo?

The chapter ends with its strongest line. Kural 110:

*Ennandri kondraarkkum uyvundaam uyvillai*\
*seynandri kondra magarkku.*

Those who destroy every other good may still find a way out; there is no way out for one who kills gratitude.

It sounds severe, and it is easy to read as a threat. I read it more gently. Most mistakes can be repaired with an apology, a changed habit, a second try. Forgetting kindness is different, because the forgetting itself removes the thing you would need to repair it. If you do not remember that someone carried you, you will never think to carry anyone. It is less a curse than a description of how a heart closes.

Gratitude is also a kind of learning. You learn, slowly, how much was given to you; the [Tirukkural on learning](/blog/tirukkural-on-learning) would say that kind of digging never reaches the bottom. And the Kural's chapter on friendship, which I have also written about in the [Tirukkural on friendship](/blog/tirukkural-on-friendship), describes the friend as the one whose help arrives before you ask, which is exactly the help that cannot be repaid.

## Name the person you never repaid

Name one person whose help you never repaid. Then tell them. A short message is enough: "I was thinking about that night you drove Amma to the hospital." If they are no longer here, tell someone else about them, your child, your spouse, a friend at lunch. And if you want to pass the kindness forward rather than back, the tradition's oldest answer is food, and [annadhanam, feeding people as worship](/blog/annadhanam-feeding-as-worship) is where I would start. You will not settle the account. That was never the point.
