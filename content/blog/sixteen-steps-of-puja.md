What would you do if the most honoured guest imaginable knocked on your door? You would open it, invite them in, offer a seat and a glass of water. You would let them wash, perhaps give them something fresh to wear, feed them the best food in the house, and walk them to the gate when they left. That is shodashopachara puja, the sixteen-step worship: shodasha means sixteen, and upachara means an act of service. It is hospitality, performed for God, step by step.

## Shodashopachara puja: the sixteen offerings

Lists vary slightly between traditions and texts such as the Agamas, the manuals of temple worship, but here is the shodashopachara puja list most priests follow.

1. **Avahana**, invocation: inviting the deity to be present.
2. **Asana**, a seat: offering a place to sit, often a flower or a few grains of rice.
3. **Padya**, water for washing the feet.
4. **Arghya**, water for the hands.
5. **Achamana**, water to sip.
6. **Snana**, a bath: sprinkling or pouring water, milk or other liquids.
7. **Vastra**, clothing: a cloth or a piece of thread.
8. **Yajnopavita**, the sacred thread.
9. **Gandha**, sandalwood paste.
10. **Pushpa**, flowers.
11. **Dhupa**, incense.
12. **Deepa**, the lamp.
13. **Naivedya**, food.
14. **Tambula**, betel leaves and nut, the traditional after-meal offering.
15. **Nirajana**, the aarti, waving camphor or a lamp.
16. **Pradakshina and namaskara**, walking around the deity and bowing in farewell.

Read that list again as a host rather than a worshipper. It is the order in which you would care for a guest after a long journey. Door, chair, wash, water, drink, bath, clothes, perfume, flowers, a nicely scented room, light, dinner, something sweet after, then the goodbye at the gate. The meaning of the 16 steps of puja is not hidden. It is sitting right there in the sequence.

## Why treat God like a guest?

The Taittiriya Upanishad gives us the famous line atithi devo bhava: let the guest be as God to you. Indian households have lived by it for a very long time. The shodashopachara turns the saying around. If the guest is God, then God can also be a guest.

That reversal changes everything about the relationship. A king is worshipped from a distance. A guest is fed, fussed over, asked if the water is warm enough. The Ramayana tells of Shabari, an old forest-dwelling woman who waited years for Rama to pass by her hut, and when he finally came, fed him the fruits she had gathered. Later tellings add that she bit each berry first to make sure it was sweet. Nobody taught her a ritual. She simply knew how to host.

Rama accepted food a priest would have called impure. That is a story about intimacy. In a puja, you are not approaching a throne. You are bringing someone into your house.

## Do you need all sixteen?

No. The full sixteen are typically offered on festival days, at temple rituals and during special pujas. For everyday worship, tradition offers the panchopachara, the five-step puja: sandal paste, flowers, incense, lamp and food. These are the heart of the longer version, and they are considered complete. A simpler six-step sequence for beginners is in [puja at home, step by step](/blog/puja-at-home-step-by-step), and an even shorter one in the [five-minute daily puja](/blog/five-minute-daily-puja).

Tradition also allows manasa puja, mental worship, in which each upachara is offered in the imagination. If you are travelling, unwell, or without a single flower in the house, you can close your eyes and offer the seat, the water, the lamp, the food. It counts.

If you are gathering things for a fuller puja, a [list of puja items for the home](/blog/puja-items-list-for-home) is a useful checklist. And the reason the food is shared afterwards, rather than simply set aside, is its own lovely story: [why prasadam is shared](/blog/why-prasadam-is-shared).

## You are hosting

Here is a small reframe to carry into your next puja. When you light the lamp, notice that you are not performing a ritual. You are hosting. Someone has arrived. The rest of the puja follows naturally from that: you will want to offer water, a flower, something to eat. You will want to say goodbye properly.

The sixteen steps are only what good hospitality looks like when someone writes it down. If you want to begin with just one of them, begin with the lamp. [The dusk lamp ritual](/blog/the-dusk-lamp-ritual) shows how.
