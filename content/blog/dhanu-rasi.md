My son, who is about eleven, tried archery at a summer camp near Edison and came home saying the hard part was not pulling the string. The hard part, he said, was holding still before letting go. I have been thinking about that sentence ever since. It is Dhanu rasi, the archer's sign, in one line, and it came from a boy with a sunburn and a juice box.

## Dhanu rasi: the archer and Jupiter

Dhanu rasi is the ninth sign of the Jyotish zodiac. Your rasi, in the South Indian way of asking, is your Moon sign: the sign the Moon was passing through when you arrived. It holds all of Mula, all of Purva Ashadha and the first quarter of [Uttara Ashadha, the star of final victory](/blog/uttara-ashadha-nakshatra). Its ruling planet is Guru, Jupiter, the planet of teachers, wisdom and dharma. My mother calls it Dhanusu. Newspapers here would print it as Sagittarius, though the Western zodiac is measured differently and often sits a sign apart.

The symbol is an archer, half-man and half-horse, drawing the bow upward. Not at an enemy on the ground; at something above. I like that the arrow points up. The sign is about aim, and about what you choose to aim at.

## Where is the archer aiming?

Tradition's portrait of Dhanu is principled, generous and optimistic, fond of learning, travel and teaching. The shadow is bluntness, saying the true thing at the wrong moment, and restlessness, the arrow that never quite settles on a target. My aunt in Chennai, who checks the palangal, the weekly forecasts, every Sunday, says all Dhanu people are either professors or pilgrims. That is an aunt's portrait, and I take it as one: a description, not a destiny.

The old picture of the archer's focus is from the Mahabharata. Drona, the teacher of the Pandava and Kaurava princes, set a wooden bird high in a tree and asked each student what he saw. One saw the tree, the branches, the sky, his brothers. Arjuna said he saw only the bird's eye. Drona told him to shoot. The others were not less skilled. They were less still.

Dhanu also gives its name to a season. When the Sun passes through Dhanu, it is the Tamil month of Margazhi, the cold pre-dawn month when Andal's Tiruppavai is sung in Vishnu temples and homes before sunrise. I grew up waking to it on the radio in Chennai. I have written about [the meaning of the Tiruppavai](/blog/tiruppavai-meaning) and about [the month of Margazhi](/blog/margazhi-month-meaning), the solar month when the Sun passes through this sign. The whole month is a kind of holding still before the day lets go.

## Temples and poojas traditionally linked through Guru

For Jupiter, families go to Alangudi near Kumbakonam, the Guru temple of the Navagraha circuit, where Shiva is worshipped as the one who drank the poison of the churned ocean. The [guide to Thursday Guru worship](/blog/guru-graha-thursday-worship) explains the observances in detail.

Thursday is Guru's day, and in many Shiva temples devotees stop on Thursdays before Dakshinamurthy, the form of Shiva who faces south and teaches in silence, seated under a banyan tree with his students at his feet. Yellow flowers and chickpeas, kondakadalai, are the traditional offerings. For those who want to understand where Dhanu sits beside the rest of the chart, [reading your birth chart calmly](/blog/reading-your-birth-chart-calmly) is a sensible start, and the next sign, [Makara](/blog/makara-rasi), shows what the archer becomes when aim turns into long work.

## One small thing for this week

My son did not hit the target that day. He hit the edge of it twice, and he was very proud of the edge. What he remembered was the stillness, the moment with the string pulled back when the whole field went quiet.

One small thing to carry into this week: before your next big decision, hold still for as long as it takes to breathe out fully. Then let go.
