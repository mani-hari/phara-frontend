The first time I saw a Brahmotsavam chariot pulled across a New Jersey parking lot, with a strip mall on one side and a hundred people in veshtis on the other, I stopped worrying about whether tradition could travel. Brahmotsavam is the great annual festival of a Vishnu temple, a series of days of processions, music and ritual, and American temples hold it with the same care as temples in India, only compressed into weekends. If you have never been, go once. Bring the children.

## What is Brahmotsavam?

According to temple tradition, the festival was first performed by Brahma himself for Venkateswara at Tirumala, which is where the name comes from: Brahma's utsavam, Brahma's festival. The story of the Lord of the Seven Hills is told in [the Tirupati Balaji temple story](/blog/tirupati-balaji-temple-story), and Brahmotsavam there is one of the great spectacles of South India.

The festival has a shape that repeats wherever it is held. It begins with dhwajarohanam, the hoisting of a flag bearing the image of Garuda on the temple's flagstaff, an invitation to the gods to attend. Then, over the days that follow, the utsava murti, the festival image of the deity, is carried out in procession morning and evening on different vahanams, vehicles: Garuda the eagle, Hanumantha, the serpent Sesha, a lion, an elephant, the sun and moon. Each vahanam has its own day and its own story.

Near the end comes the chariot, the rathotsavam, when devotees pull the deity through the streets with thick ropes. The festival closes with avabhrutha snanam, the sacred bath of the deity, and the lowering of the flag.

## How American temples hold Brahmotsavam

Here, the festival has adapted without losing itself. Most temples compress it into a long weekend or two, so that working families can come. The processions go around the temple grounds or the parking lot instead of four car streets. The chariot is often smaller and pulled by volunteers in shifts, some of them teenagers who have been told it is a great honour and who, to their surprise, agree.

Children come in costume. Musicians play the nadaswaram and thavil, sometimes flown in, sometimes local. The canteen runs from morning until late.

Brahmotsavams in America are usually held in the warmer months, but each temple sets its own dates by its own calendar and traditions, so check with yours. The [Hindu festival calendar for families in the USA](/blog/hindu-festival-calendar-usa) is a good way to see how the year's festivals fit together.

## How can families take part in Brahmotsavam?

There are more ways in than most people realise.

- **Sponsor a day's vahanam.** Many temples invite families to sponsor a procession. Your family name is read out in the sankalpam, the statement of intention, and you walk close to the deity.
- **Help pull the chariot.** Anyone can take a rope. My son did it the year he turned nine and talked about nothing else for a week.
- **Volunteer.** The kitchen, parking and flower teams are stretched thin during festivals. There is more on this in [volunteering at a Hindu temple](/blog/volunteering-at-a-hindu-temple).
- **Simply stand along the route.** Bring the kids, a flask of tea, a folding chair for your mother if she is visiting. Watch the deity pass. Fold your hands.

What to wear is simple: clean, modest, comfortable for standing in the sun. [What to wear to a Hindu temple](/blog/what-to-wear-hindu-temple) has the details, and festival days are a good excuse for silk if you have it.

My father, on his visits, used to say that the procession is the one time the deity comes out to see the people instead of the other way round. I think about that often when the chariot turns the corner past the dumpsters and the fire lane. The Lord of the Seven Hills, visiting a parking lot in New Jersey, because that is where his people are.

## One small thing to carry into the week

Find your temple's festival calendar and look for the next Brahmotsavam or chariot festival. Pick one evening of it, just one, and go, even if it is a Tuesday. Stay for one procession. You will drive home late, a little tired, with jasmine in the car, and you will not regret it.
