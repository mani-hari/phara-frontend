Om Sahana Vavatu is a prayer said by two people at once. The Om Sahana Vavatu meaning, in short: before any learning begins, teacher and student ask to be protected together, nourished together, to work with energy together, and not to resent each other. That last request is a strikingly honest thing to put into a prayer, and it is why this chant is worth more than a quick mumble at the start of class.

## Where does Om Sahana Vavatu come from?

It is a shanti mantra, a peace chant, found in the Taittiriya Upanishad of the Krishna Yajurveda, and it is also used to open several other Upanishads, including the Katha. Traditionally teacher and student chanted it together at the start of a session of study. Many schools in India still begin the day with it, and many Bala Vihar classes and yoga teachers use it too.

The word shanti means peace, and a shanti mantra is less a request for a particular thing than a way of preparing the ground. You do not plant before you have cleared the field.

People write the opening in different ways: Om Sahana Vavatu, Om Saha Navavatu. The second is closer to how the Sanskrit splits, saha nau avatu, together us may it protect. Both are fine. The sound is the same.

## Om Sahana Vavatu meaning, line by line

Here it is in plain English.

Om saha navavatu: may it protect us both.
Saha nau bhunaktu: may it nourish us both.
Saha viryam karavavahai: may we work together with energy.
Tejasvi navadhitam astu: may our study be bright, full of light.
Ma vidvishavahai: may we not dislike each other.
Om shanti, shanti, shanti: peace, peace, peace.

The "it" at the start is left open. Many take it to mean Brahman, the underlying reality; others simply hear it as the divine, or the learning itself. You do not have to settle that before you chant.

The triple shanti at the end is traditionally explained as a request for peace from three kinds of disturbance: from within oneself, from other beings and the world around, and from forces beyond our control. I have written more about that ending in [the meaning of Om Shanti Shanti Shanti](/blog/om-shanti-shanti-shanti-meaning).

## Why pray not to resent each other?

Because learning is friction. I would go so far as to say that any real learning involves at least a little resentment. The teacher gets impatient with the student who does not practise. The student resents the teacher who corrects the same mistake for the fifth time. Pride gets in the way. Boredom gets in the way. Sometimes the student surpasses the teacher, and that stings too.

The chant names this friction at the very start, before anyone has had a chance to feel it. That is clever. Once something has been named out loud, it is easier to notice when it arrives, and easier to let go of. It is the same reason a couple might agree before a long drive not to argue about directions. The agreement will not stop the argument completely, but it changes the tone.

Try watching this in your own body. When someone corrects you, what happens in the jaw, the shoulders, the breath? The chant is a teacher-student prayer in Sanskrit, but its real instrument is attention to that small tightening.

## Try saying it with someone this week

This is the experiment. Say Om Sahana Vavatu with another person before something that involves learning or working together. A child before homework. A colleague before a hard meeting, silently if saying it aloud would be strange. A partner before you sit down to sort out the household accounts. Or silently, on your own, before a class or a lesson you are about to give.

Say it slowly. Breathe between the lines. If you are new to chanting, the [simple mantras for children](/blog/simple-mantras-for-kids) are a gentle place to begin, and this one belongs on that list. It also sits well beside a [Saraswati mantra before study](/blog/saraswati-mantra-for-students) and the older idea, told in the [story of Hayagreeva and learning](/blog/hayagreeva-homam-meaning), that knowledge is something restored, not owned.

Then notice the tone of what follows. Not whether the meeting went well or the homework was correct. Just the tone. Many people find it a shade gentler. See what you find.
