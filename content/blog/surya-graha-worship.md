The oldest Surya remedy costs nothing. Stand in the first sunlight of the morning, pour a little water from a copper vessel toward the sun, and let your eyes and your body register that the day has begun. That single act, arghya, the offering of water at sunrise, sits at the centre of most traditional surya graha remedies. The others, a Sunday prayer, the Aditya Hridayam, wheat or jaggery given away, all grow from the same root: turning to face the light, on purpose, at the same hour.

## Who is Surya among the Navagrahas?

Surya, the Sun, stands at the centre of the nine grahas, the planets of Jyotish. In the tradition he is seen as the self, vitality, the father, authority and confidence. He is associated with Sunday, the colour red, wheat and the ruby. A strong Sun in a chart is read as self-respect and clarity of purpose; a troubled Sun as a shaky sense of self, trouble with authority, or low energy.

His great temple in Tamil Nadu is Suryanar Kovil, near Kumbakonam. It is the only one of the region's nine Navagraha temples where the Sun is the presiding deity, shown with two consorts, usually named as Usha and Pratyusha (some accounts say Usha and Chhaya), while the other eight grahas have their own shrines around him. Devotees who make the Navagraha circuit traditionally begin here. At a temple, the sequence for the nine is set out in [worshipping the Navagrahas at a temple](/blog/navagraha-temple-worship-guide).

## Surya graha remedies from tradition

The practices are simple and old.

- **Arghya at sunrise.** Water poured from a copper vessel toward the rising sun, sometimes with a red flower or a pinch of kumkum in it, while chanting Om Suryaya Namah or the Gayatri.
- **The Aditya Hridayam.** A hymn in the Yuddha Kanda of the Ramayana, taught by the sage Agastya to Rama on the battlefield, when Rama was exhausted before his final fight with Ravana. It is a hymn for courage when you are depleted. The piece on [the meaning of the Aditya Hrudayam](/blog/aditya-hrudayam-meaning) goes through it verse by verse.
- **Surya Namaskar.** The sequence of twelve postures that greets the sun with the whole body. The tradition behind it is explained in [Surya Namaskar's meaning](/blog/surya-namaskar-meaning).
- **Sunday giving.** Wheat, jaggery or red cloth given to someone in need, and in many South Indian homes, sweet pongal cooked on Sundays.

None of this requires a priest. It requires being awake at sunrise.

## What does sunlight do to the body?

This is where it gets interesting. Look at what the practice actually makes you do.

It makes you wake at a fixed hour. It puts morning light into your eyes, which is one of the strongest signals the body uses to set its daily clock; many people find that regular morning light helps them fall asleep more easily at night. It makes you stand upright and face something bright, and the spine straightens almost by itself when you do. It gives the first minutes of the day a single, simple act instead of a screen.

The tradition put worship exactly where the body already needed attention. Whether you believe the Sun is a graha with influence on your chart, or simply a star that the body is built to respond to, the practice works on you the same way. You mark the beginning of the day instead of stumbling into it.

The Sun also has a quieter lesson. It is associated with the self and with self-respect. Keeping a small promise to yourself every morning, before anyone else needs you, is a very direct way to build exactly that.

## A week of sun practice

Try this for one week.

1. Find out when sunrise is where you live and set an alarm fifteen minutes before it.
2. Wash your face, and fill a small copper or steel vessel with water.
3. Go to a window, balcony or doorstep facing east. Stand for two minutes as the light comes.
4. Pour the water slowly, into a plant pot if you are indoors, and say Om Suryaya Namah once.
5. Stand still for three breaths before you go back in.

That is all. On Sunday, if you like, add the Aditya Hridayam or give something away.

At the end of the week, notice your sleep. Notice your mornings. The Sun's partner in the sky has its own day too: [Chandra, the Moon and Monday](/blog/chandra-graha-worship). Start with the Sun, though. It comes back every morning, whether you show up or not.
