The Tirukkural treats wealth as necessary, not shameful. My Thatha ran his cloth shop in Kumbakonam as if he had read the Porul section closely, and perhaps he had. The Thirukkural on wealth says, in short, that money is a powerful means to good ends, that it must be earned without harming others, and that its real purpose is to support virtue and giving. Valluvar is no ascetic about it. He simply insists that how the money comes matters as much as how much.

## What does the Porul section actually say about money?

The Kural has three books. The second, Porul, is the longest, and it covers wealth, work, statecraft and public life. Porul, in the Thirukkural, means something broader than money alone. Porul means wealth, but also substance, the stuff that keeps a household, a kingdom and a life running.

Within it is a chapter called Porul Seyalvagai, the ways of earning wealth. It opens with a blunt couplet, Kural 751: there is nothing like wealth for making people of no account into people of account. Valluvar is not naive about how the world treats the poor.

But a few couplets later, in Kural 754, comes the condition:

*Aranheenum inbamum eenum thiranarindhu*\
*theedhindri vandha porul.*

Wealth gained with skill and without wrongdoing yields both virtue and joy. The key words are theedhindri, without evil. Elsewhere, in the chapter on purity of action, Kural 659 gives the warning side: what is gained through others' tears will leave through tears, while what is gained by good means, even if lost, returns its fruit later. On earning honestly, those two couplets are the heart of Valluvar's teaching.

## Thirukkural on wealth at Thatha's counter

I have written before about Thatha's measuring rod in [Thatha's shop](/blog/honest-trade-thathas-shop). There are other things I remember from his counter that fit the Porul section.

In a bad season, when the monsoon failed and the weavers in the villages around Kumbakonam had nothing to sell, he advanced them money for yarn. Some never paid it back. He did not chase them. When I asked about it as a teenager, he said a weaver who survives the bad year will bring you cloth for twenty good ones.

When a neighbour on the street died, he pulled the shutters down for the day, even though it was a busy week before a festival. The shop across the road stayed open. Nobody in the family remembers what he lost in sales. Everybody remembers that he closed.

The Thirukkural on money is not only about coins. In the Kural's sense, wealth includes reputation, trust and the goodwill of neighbours. Thatha had a lot of that kind of wealth. Most of it was not in the cash box.

## Earning in America without losing yourself

My life looks nothing like Thatha's. A salary, the rent, a monthly transfer home to Chennai, a retirement account I check more than I should. The pull to keep score is strong here. Everyone seems to know what everyone else earns, or wants to.

Valluvar's frame helps me because it does not ask me to feel guilty about earning. It asks what the earning is for. The chapter Oppuravu Arithal, on duty to society, puts it plainly in Kural 212: all the wealth earned by hard effort is for doing good to those who deserve it. Wealth is a means to aram, virtue, and to giving. The same idea runs through [annadhanam, feeding as worship](/blog/annadhanam-feeding-as-worship), and more widely through [Hinduism and business ethics](/blog/hinduism-business-ethics).

Some of the Kural's other chapters, on patience and on speech, turn out to matter for money too. It is hard to earn cleanly with a sharp tongue, which is why [the Tirukkural on sweet speech](/blog/tirukkural-on-sweet-speech) belongs next to this one. If you are new to the book, [the Thirukkural for daily life](/blog/tirukkural-for-daily-life) is an easier way in.

## One small thing for the week

None of this is a promise that clean earning makes anyone rich. Valluvar never promises that. He promises that wealth earned without tears does not leave through tears.

One small thing for the week: before one purchase or one transfer home, ask Thatha's question. Was this earned cleanly, and will it help someone? If the answer to both is yes, spend it without guilt.
