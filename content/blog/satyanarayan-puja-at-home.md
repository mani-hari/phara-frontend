Satyanarayan puja is the most portable ritual Hindus have: one afternoon, one story, one sweet, and a room full of people who came to listen. You can do Satyanarayan puja at home as a family, with or without a priest. Honour Ganesha first, worship Vishnu in his form as Satyanarayan, read the katha, the five-part story, aloud, perform the aarti and share sheera, the semolina sweet that is its prasad. Purnima, the full moon, is the traditional day, but any day the family can gather is a good one.

## Setting up Satyanarayan puja at home

Clean the space, and yourself, first. Bathe, wear something fresh, and notice how differently the body settles when it has been washed and dressed for something. That is the beginning of the puja, not a chore before it.

Then set up the altar. Here is the traditional list, with what works in an American home:

- **A low table or chowki**, covered with a clean cloth, red or yellow if you have one. A coffee table works.
- **A picture of Vishnu or Satyanarayan.** A printed image in a frame is fine.
- **A kalash**, a pot of water topped with mango or betel leaves and a coconut. Any clean steel or brass pot will do; mango leaves are often sold at Indian grocers, and fresh bay leaves are a reasonable stand-in.
- **Banana stems or leaves** to frame the altar. Indian and Asian grocers usually carry frozen banana leaves. If you cannot find them, a few whole bananas placed around the altar keep the meaning.
- **Tulsi**, holy basil, which is dear to Vishnu. A small potted tulsi or a few leaves from an Indian store; if you keep one at home, [keeping a tulsi plant indoors](/blog/tulsi-plant-indoors) covers the cold months. Ordinary basil is not the same plant, but if that is all you have, offer it with the same care.
- **Flowers, fruit, turmeric, kumkum, rice, incense and a lamp.**
- **Sheera ingredients:** semolina, ghee, sugar, milk and a ripe banana.

If you do not yet have a regular altar, a guide to [doing puja at home step by step](/blog/puja-at-home-step-by-step) covers the basics.

## The steps, in order

1. **Ganesha puja.** A flower, a little water and a name, to clear the way. There is a good reason he always comes first, told in [why Ganesha comes first](/blog/why-ganesha-comes-first).
2. **Remembering the Navagrahas.** A brief salutation to the nine planetary deities; many families simply name them.
3. **The main puja.** Invite Satyanarayan into the kalash or the image, then offer water, flowers, tulsi, incense, lamp and food, one at a time, with a breath for each.
4. **The katha.** The story is read in five chapters. It tells of people who kept a promise and people who forgot one. What it means is unpacked in [the meaning of the Satyanarayan katha](/blog/satyanarayan-katha-meaning).
5. **Aarti.** The lamp is waved in slow circles before the image while everyone sings or claps.
6. **Prasad.** The sheera is offered, then shared with everyone present.

Try this while you cook the sheera: do nothing else. No phone, no radio. Roast the semolina in ghee slowly until it smells nutty, and stay with the smell. Many families say the prasad tastes different when it is made this way, and there may be something to it. Attention changes how you cook.

## When is the best day to do it?

Purnima, the full-moon day, is the most traditional choice, usually in the evening. Ekadashi, the eleventh lunar day, and Sankranti, when the sun enters a new sign, are also common. Many families hold it on occasions rather than dates: a housewarming, a new job, a child's birthday, the day after a long illness ends. It sits naturally beside the [steps of a griha pravesh, the housewarming puja](/blog/griha-pravesh-puja-steps).

The katha itself says the puja can be performed on any day with devotion. That is freeing. If Saturday afternoon is the only time the family can sit together without a clock in their heads, then Saturday afternoon is the right day.

## Try this: share the story

The katha is long when one person reads it and lively when five people do. Assign each chapter to a different family member, including the children, who can read the shortest one. Let them stumble over the names. Let the grandparents correct them gently. If you are inviting friends, there are suggestions in [hosting Satyanarayan puja for friends in America](/blog/hosting-satyanarayan-puja-with-friends).

Notice what happens to the room when everyone has a part. People stop watching the ritual and start doing it. That is the whole design.
