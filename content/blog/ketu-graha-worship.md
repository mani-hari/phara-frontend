The traditional ketu graha remedies, Ganesha worship, horse gram given, a lamp at dusk, make sense only once you know the story of a body without a head. At the churning of the ocean, when the nectar of immortality finally rose, Vishnu took the form of Mohini, an enchantress, to serve it to the gods and keep it from the asuras. One asura, Svarbhanu, disguised himself and slipped into the line of gods between the Sun and the Moon. He had just swallowed the nectar when the two of them recognised him and cried out. Mohini's discus took off his head at once. But the nectar had already passed his lips, so neither half could die. The head became Rahu. The body became Ketu: a planet with no eyes, no mouth, and nothing left to want.

## The headless body in the Puranas

The story is told in the Vishnu Purana and the Bhagavata Purana, among others, as part of the samudra manthan, the churning of the milky ocean, and [the meaning of the samudra manthan](/blog/samudra-manthan-meaning) is a longer story than this one needs. It explains eclipses, of course: Rahu, the head, still chases the Sun and the Moon who betrayed him and swallows them for a while, and they slip out through his severed throat.

But look at what happens to the body. Rahu gets the grudge, the hunger, the mouth that is never satisfied. Ketu gets none of that. Ketu has no head to plot with, no eyes to covet, no tongue to taste. The story says Ketu was punished. The story is really about what remains when craving has been cut away. And the tradition, remarkably, does not treat that remainder as a ruin.

## Why is Ketu linked with moksha?

In Jyotish, Ketu stands for detachment, sudden endings, intuition, past-life tendencies, spiritual insight and moksha, liberation. He is the planet that cuts things off, which is why people fear his periods. He is also the planet most associated with freedom, which is why renunciants and seekers speak of him fondly.

He is traditionally associated with smoky and mixed colours, horse gram, which Tamil homes call kollu, and the cat's eye stone. His linked deity is Ganesha. Most traditions give him no weekday of his own; Rahu and Ketu are shadow planets, chaya grahas, points in the sky rather than bodies, and the week was already full.

Why Ganesha? The tradition does not settle this in one text, but the resonance is hard to miss. Ganesha, too, lost his head, and was given another. Ketu is the body waiting for a head; Ganesha is the one who shows that a body can be made whole again, differently. And Ganesha is the remover of obstacles, which is the sort of help you want in a period of sudden endings. It is the same reason [Ganesha comes first](/blog/why-ganesha-comes-first) in every pooja.

## Ketu graha remedies from tradition

The practices are simple.

- **Ganesha worship**, a lamp and a few flowers before his picture, or a visit to a Ganesha shrine.
- **Om Ketave Namah**, the short Ketu mantra, chanted seven or one hundred and eight times.
- **Horse gram** given away or offered, sometimes cooked as sundal.
- **A lamp at dusk**, sitting with it for a few minutes without asking for anything.

Ketu's Navagraha temple is the Naganathaswamy temple at Keezhperumpallam, near Poompuhar in Tamil Nadu. There Ketu is pictured with a serpent's head and a human body, folded hands turned toward Shiva, as though the body had found a head of its own at last. Devotees come to pray during difficult Ketu periods.

Many devotees also go to Sri Kalahasti in Andhra Pradesh, where Rahu and Ketu are worshipped together; the guide to [the Sri Kalahasti temple](/blog/sri-kalahasti-temple-guide) explains what happens there. And for the pair as the tradition reads them in a chart, there is [Rahu Ketu dosha explained](/blog/rahu-ketu-dosha-explained).

## What you could live without

Ketu does not take things away out of spite. He has no spite; he has no head to hold it in. In the tradition, what he does is show you what you could live without.

That can be frightening while it happens. A job ends, a plan dissolves, a certainty drops away. But many people, looking back on such a stretch, find that what was removed was something they had been carrying for a long time without needing it.

If you are in a Ketu season, light a lamp at dusk and sit with it for a few minutes. Do not ask for anything. That is the hardest prayer, and the one that suits him best.
