The first Satyanarayan puja we hosted in our New Jersey townhouse had twenty-two guests, one borrowed brass lamp, and more sheera than anyone could finish. It was chaotic and it was lovely. If you are planning a Satyanarayan puja in America, here is what I wish I had known: choose a weekend day, plan about two hours for the puja and the story, give newcomers a short explanation and a small role, and serve the sheera prasad first and a simple vegetarian meal after. The rest sorts itself out.

## Planning Satyanarayan puja in America

**Choose the day.** Purnima, the full-moon day, is traditional, but most of us in the US hold it on the nearest Saturday or Sunday, and that is entirely in keeping with the puja. The katha itself says it can be done on any day with devotion. Afternoons work well: people can arrive after errands and leave before dinner.

**Decide on a priest, or not.** Most Hindu temples in the US offer priests for home rituals, and popular dates, long weekends and full moons, fill up early, so book a few weeks ahead. If there is no temple nearby, some priests guide by video call. Or do it yourselves. Many families do, reading from a printed vidhi, the order of worship. There is a clear guide to [Satyanarayan puja at home](/blog/satyanarayan-puja-at-home) with the items and steps.

**Plan a two-hour timeline.** Roughly:

- Guests arrive and settle, with shoes at the door.
- Ganesha puja and the main worship, with the priest or the host leading.
- The katha, in five short chapters.
- Aarti, the waving of the lamp with singing.
- Prasad, then lunch.

Add a buffer at the start. Someone will always be stuck on the freeway.

## How do you include guests who are new to it?

At our first puja, half the guests were colleagues and neighbours who had never been to one. They sat politely at the back, unsure whether to stand, sit, clap or keep still. I should have told them.

Now I send a short paragraph with the invitation: "Satyanarayan puja is a prayer of thanks to Vishnu, the preserver in Hindu tradition. We will do a short ritual, read a story aloud in five parts, and then share a sweet and lunch. Please come as you are; sit where you like. Shoes off at the door." That paragraph does more than any explanation on the day. If you want more short answers ready, there are some in [explaining Hindu festivals to coworkers](/blog/explaining-hindu-festivals-to-coworkers).

Then give people something to do. Someone can pass the flowers. Someone can ring the bell during aarti. A friend who reads well can take one chapter of the katha in English. People who have a role stop feeling like guests. The story they read is worth knowing too; I would point them to [the meaning of the Satyanarayan katha](/blog/satyanarayan-katha-meaning).

On what to wear: anything comfortable for sitting on the floor. Nobody expects silk.

## Food after the puja

The sheera prasad comes first, always. It is made from semolina roasted in ghee, then cooked with milk, sugar and banana, and offered at the altar before anyone eats it. Hand it out in small cups with a spoon. Add fruit and panchamrit, the mix of milk, curd, honey, ghee and sugar, if you have made it.

Then a simple vegetarian lunch. Rice, dal, one vegetable, a raita, a sweet. That is plenty. Avoid onion and garlic if your family follows that custom on puja days, and label dishes for anyone with allergies. The Bhagavad Gita says that those who eat what remains after an offering are freed, while those who cook only for themselves miss the point of cooking. A puja lunch is exactly that: food that was offered first, then shared. The same spirit runs through [annadhanam, feeding others as worship](/blog/annadhanam-feeding-as-worship).

## One small thing to carry into the week

After the aarti, ask everyone to sit for one minute in silence. Nobody speaks. Children included, if they can manage.

At that first puja, twenty-two people fell quiet at once, and I could hear the fridge humming in the kitchen and a car passing outside. It was the most prayerful moment of the afternoon, and it cost nothing. Even with a full house, especially with a full house, make that minute. Then let the noise come back.

If your home is small and you are worried about space, a permanent [small puja corner in an apartment](/blog/small-puja-corner-apartment) can grow into a big afternoon more easily than you think.
