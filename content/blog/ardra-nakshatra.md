A thunderstorm rolled over Edison the week of Margazhi Thiruvathirai. The power flickered, my son counted the seconds between flash and thunder, and I found myself thinking of Rudra, the storm god of the Vedas who weeps, and of the one star he calls his own. Ardra nakshatra, Thiruvathirai in Tamil, is that star: storm first, then rain, then clean air.

## Ardra nakshatra: storm, tears and Rahu

Ardra belongs to Rudra, the fierce early form of Shiva. Its planet, the adhipati that Jyotish assigns to each of the twenty-seven stars, is Rahu, the shadow planet that stirs things up. The star sits wholly in [Mithuna, the sign of the twins](/blog/mithuna-rasi). Its name means moist, and its symbol is a teardrop, sometimes a diamond: something hard and bright that forms under pressure.

One old explanation of Rudra's name is "the one who howls" or "the one who weeps". I like that the storm god is allowed to cry. The rain after the storm is his tears, and it is the rain that makes the fields green.

Tradition describes Thiruvathirai-born people as intense, questioning, sometimes restless, and unusually capable of starting over after upheaval. My aunt in Chennai would add "sharp tongue" to that list, but she says it about most stars. As with all of this, it is a description, not a fate.

The star comes after [Mrigashira, the seeker's star](/blog/mrigashira-nakshatra).

## Why does Shiva dance on Thiruvathirai?

In Tamil tradition, Thiruvathirai is Shiva's own star, the way Karthigai belongs to Murugan. Its great day comes once a year in Margazhi, the cold month of dawn prayers, when the full moon falls on Thiruvathirai. That morning is Arudra Darshanam. At Chidambaram, Nataraja, Shiva as the lord of the dance, is brought out of the sanctum at dawn and carried through the streets so that everyone, not only those who can get inside, can see him dance. The fuller account belongs to [the meaning of Arudra Darshan](/blog/arudra-darshan-meaning); for the temple, see [the Chidambaram Nataraja temple](/blog/chidambaram-nataraja-temple).

In Tamil homes, the morning is sweetened with Thiruvathirai kali, a soft dish of rice, jaggery and coconut. My grandmother in Thanjavur made it every year; we make a small pan now in New Jersey, and the recipe is in [Thiruvathirai kali for Arudra Darshan](/blog/thiruvathirai-kali-recipe).

All through Margazhi, girls in Tamil Nadu used to sing Manikkavasagar's Thiruvempavai at dawn. It opens with "Aadhiyum andhamum illaa arumperum jothiyai", the great light that has neither beginning nor end. That is a good line to keep in your head during a storm.

## The star's temple and its monthly day

When the widely shared Tamil lists pair each star with a temple, Ardra usually gets the Abhaya Varadeeswarar temple at Athirampattinam, near Pattukottai. Abhaya means fearlessness, and varada means the giver of boons: the Lord who gives freedom from fear. For a star named after a storm, that seems exactly right.

The monthly observance is plain. On the Thiruvathirai day each month, when the Moon returns to the star, light a lamp and say Om Namah Shivaya for a few minutes. Those who know it recite the Rudram, the great Vedic hymn to Rudra. Some families add a lamp every Monday, which is Shiva's day in the weekly round. When Thiruvathirai comes round in the month you were born, many families treat it as the real birthday and book a Shiva archana, the recitation of his names with flowers. The star that follows, [Punarvasu, the star of return](/blog/punarvasu-nakshatra), is a gentle next step after the storm.

## One small thing for this week

After the next storm, or the next hard conversation, sit for five minutes and let it pass through. Do not replay it. Do not draft the reply. Just sit. Rudra's tears are the rain that cleans the air, and a little of that is allowed for us too.
