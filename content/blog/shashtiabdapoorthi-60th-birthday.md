At my uncle's shashtiabdapoorthi in Madurai, the moment everyone remembers is not the homam. It is my uncle tying the thali on my aunt again, his hands not quite steady, while their grandchildren giggled behind the priest. Shashtiabdapoorthi is the Hindu ritual for a sixtieth birthday, when the cycle of sixty years completes and a couple, in many families, renews their marriage before the fire. If your parents are approaching sixty and you live abroad, plan early, and plan around what they want.

## What is shashtiabdapoorthi, and why sixty?

The traditional Hindu calendar names its years in a cycle of sixty, from Prabhava to Akshaya. Each year has its own name, and the same name returns only once in sixty years. So a person who turns sixty is standing, for the first time since birth, in a year with the same name as the one they were born in. Shashti means sixty; abda, year; poorthi, completion. The circle has closed.

Families usually hold the ritual in the birth month, on the day the person's birth star, the janma nakshatra, returns. It is the star birthday, the same principle as [the Ayushya homam on the star birthday](/blog/ayushya-homam-star-birthday), only much larger. For couples, the ritual is traditionally done when the husband completes sixty, and the wife sits beside him through it.

## What happens at the ritual

The details vary by family and region, but the shape is similar.

It begins with a Ganapati puja, to remove obstacles, and a sankalpam, the spoken statement of intention naming the couple, their family lineage and their stars. Then kalasa pujas: pots of water, each invoking a deity, are worshipped through the morning. Homams follow, fire rituals. The Ayushya homam prays for long and healthy life. The Mrityunjaya homam is offered to Shiva as the conqueror of death; there is more on it in [the meaning of the Mrityunjaya homam](/blog/mrityunjaya-homam-meaning). Many families add a Navagraha homam for the nine planetary deities.

Then comes the part the grandchildren remember. The couple sit together and are bathed with the water from the kalasams, the kalasa abhishekam. And then mangalya dharanam, the retying of the thali, the sacred marriage thread. Children and grandchildren fall at their feet for blessings. There is a lot of crying, and a very large lunch.

Many families travel to Thirukadaiyur in Tamil Nadu for the ritual. The temple there is traditionally associated with Markandeya, the boy devotee whom Shiva protected from Yama, the god of death. For a ritual about long life, the association is obvious.

## Planning a shashtiabdapoorthi from abroad

This is where those of us in the US, the UK or the Gulf need to be organised.

- **Fix the date early.** Ask the family priest, months ahead, to find the right day by your parent's birth star in the birth month. Halls and temples book out.
- **Share tasks with siblings in India.** My brother in Chennai handled the priest and the hall for my uncle's ritual. The cousins abroad handled invitations and paid for the meal. Nobody should be doing everything from a different time zone.
- **Plan travel around school calendars.** The birth month may fall in the middle of a school term. Decide early whether the family travels, or one parent goes.
- **Join live if you cannot fly.** It is not the same, and it is not nothing. If you are joining from abroad, [joining a puja across time zones](/blog/joining-a-puja-across-time-zones) sorts out the hours.

If the milestones continue, as we hope, there are the seventieth and eightieth rituals too: [Bheemaratha Shanti](/blog/bheemaratha-shanti-70th-birthday) and [Sathabhishekam](/blog/sathabhishekam-80th-birthday).

## One small thing to carry into the week

Before anyone books a hall or a priest, call your parents and ask them one question: what do they want the day to feel like? Some want the whole extended family and a thousand guests. Some want a small homam and their grandchildren close. My aunt, it turned out, only wanted everyone in one photograph. Start there, and plan backwards.
