The week of my father's knee operation, my sister held her phone up in the corridor of a Chennai hospital so that I could see, from my kitchen in New Jersey, the people looking after him. A nurse checking a line. A technician wheeling a machine. A cleaner who smiled at the camera. The surgeon, briefly, still in his cap. I tried to count them and lost track. Much later I learned that there is a star named for exactly that feeling. Shatabhisha nakshatra, Sadayam in Tamil, means "a hundred healers", or "a hundred physicians".

## Shatabhisha nakshatra: Varuna, Rahu and the empty circle

My aunt in Chennai can name the star of every relative, living or gone; in Jyotish the birth star, or nakshatra, is the one of twenty-seven star groups the Moon occupied at your birth. Shatabhisha nakshatra is the twenty-fourth. Its presiding deity is Varuna, lord of the waters and of rita, the cosmic order that keeps the seasons turning. Its adhipati, or ruling planet, is Rahu, the shadow planet. Its symbol is an empty circle: a boundary that holds, and the open space inside it.

Tradition describes Sadayam-born people as independent, inquiring, a little secretive, and often drawn to healing or research. Some of the Sadayam people I know are doctors and some are accountants, and all of them keep their own counsel. I take the description the way I would take a relative's shrewd remark about my character: interesting, sometimes accurate, never final.

## Why does the star of a hundred physicians belong to Varuna?

At first it seems odd. Why should a star of healers belong to the god of the sea and not, say, to the twin physicians of heaven?

In the Rig Veda, Varuna is the god who sees everything, who binds those who break the order of things and releases those who return to it. His hymns are among the most personal in the Veda; the poet asks forgiveness and asks to be loosened from what binds him. Seen that way, healing is not magic. It is order restored: the body, like the world, set back into its rhythm.

The same idea surfaces in the story of the churning of the ocean, when Dhanvantari, the physician of the gods, rose from the waters carrying the pot of amrita. The healer comes out of the sea, out of Varuna's realm. [The story of the churning of the ocean](/blog/samudra-manthan-meaning) tells the whole episode, and [the Dhanvantari mantra and what it asks for](/blog/dhanvantari-mantra-meaning) picks up the healer's side.

Tirumoolar put it plainly in the Tirumantiram: if the body perishes, the life within it goes too, so knowing how to tend the body, he tended it, and in tending the body he tended the spirit. I thought of that verse in the hospital corridor, watching a stranger adjust my father's pillow with more care than I could have managed from the other side of the world.

## Thirupugalur and prayers for the unwell

The Agnipureeswarar temple at Thirupugalur, near Nannilam in Tamil Nadu, is the temple the widely shared Tamil star list pairs with Shatabhisha. Sadayam-born devotees visit when they are in India, and many far away keep its name in mind on their star day.

Each month when Sadayam returns, some families say a short prayer to Dhanvantari, and some light a lamp for Rahu. Both are ways of remembering that health is held by many hands. A simpler observance, and one I have come to prefer, is to visit or call someone who is unwell. Not to fix anything. Just to be one of the hundred. If someone you love is ill right now, the piece on [praying for a sick parent](/blog/praying-for-a-sick-parent) may help.

The Sadayam of your birth month is your star birthday, and a good way to keep it is to thank a healer. Sadayam sits entirely inside [Kumbha rasi, the water-bearer's sign](/blog/kumbha-rasi), which makes sense: a pot of water poured out for others.

## One small thing for this week

My father is home now, older and slower and still chanting on our video calls. I never learned the names of most of the people in that corridor. I wish I had.

One small thing to carry into this week: thank one healer by name. A nurse, a pharmacist, a physiotherapist, a friend who simply listened when you needed it. Sadayam is counted in the hundreds for a reason.
