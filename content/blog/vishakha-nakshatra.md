Vishakha means "forked", a branch that splits in two, and Vishakha nakshatra is ruled by two gods at once. Tamil tradition says Murugan was born on this star, and he grew up to hold two roles at once as well: son of Shiva, and his father's teacher. The star and the story are about the same thing, the fork in the road and the moment you choose.

## Vishakha nakshatra: two gods, Jupiter and a gateway

Most nakshatras, the twenty-seven star groups the Moon moves through each month, have one presiding god and one ruling planet. Vishakha is unusual: it has two presiding deities, Indra and Agni together, the king of the gods and the god of fire. Its ruling planet is Guru, Jupiter, the planet of wisdom and teaching. In Tamil it is Visakam, and it spans Tula, the scales, and Vrischika, the scorpion.

It has several symbols. The forked branch. The triumphal archway decorated with leaves, the kind put up at a wedding or a festival. And the potter's wheel, turning steadily while something takes shape on it. All three are about passage and becoming.

The Visakam-born, in the traditional sketch, are determined and ambitious, able to choose between paths when others hesitate. Then again, plenty of Visakam people dither for a week over a menu, and the star forgives them.

The star follows [Swati, the wind's independent star](/blog/swati-nakshatra).

## What did the boy teach his father at Swamimalai?

The story belongs to the Tamil Skanda tradition, and it is set at Swamimalai, one of Murugan's six abodes. Brahma, the creator, came to visit Shiva's household, and the young Murugan stopped him and asked a simple question: what does Om mean? Brahma, who recites the Vedas all day, could not explain it. The boy imprisoned him for his ignorance and took over creation himself.

Shiva came to sort things out and, half amused, asked his son whether he knew the meaning. Murugan said he did, but that a teaching should be given properly, from teacher to student. So Shiva sat as the student, and the child explained the Pranava, the sacred syllable Om, into his father's ear. From that day Murugan was called Swaminatha, the lord who taught the Lord.

Tamil tradition holds that Murugan was born on Visakam, and his birthday, Vaikasi Visakam, is kept when the full moon falls on this star in the month of Vaikasi. The festival and its processions belong to [Vaikasi Visakam and Murugan](/blog/vaikasi-visakam-murugan).

The story says a son became his father's guru. The story is really about the fork where student and teacher change places. Every teacher, if they are lucky, eventually learns something from the person they taught. For a star ruled by Guru, the planet of teachers, that is exactly the right tale.

## The Panmozhi temple and the Murugan star day

The temple usually paired with Visakam is the Muthukumaraswamy temple at Panmozhi. An archana there, the priest chanting Murugan's names over flowers, or at your nearest Murugan temple, is the usual way to keep the connection.

Because Guru rules the star, Thursday prayers fit a Vishakha life well; [Thursday worship of Guru](/blog/guru-graha-thursday-worship) describes the simple form. On the monthly Visakam day, the janma nakshatra when the Moon returns to your star, many families light a lamp for Murugan. The star birthday, when Visakam returns in the birth month, is often kept the same way.

Swamimalai and Palani are the abodes many Visakam families hold dear. Palani has [a guide of its own](/blog/palani-murugan-temple): it is where the young god stands alone on the hill with only a staff. The next star, [Anuradha, the star of friendship](/blog/anuradha-nakshatra), follows naturally from a star about choosing: once you have chosen, you have to keep showing up.

## Walking through the archway

The archway is Vishakha's symbol for a reason. You pass through an archway; you do not live in it. People put them up for weddings and festivals precisely because they mark a crossing.

On your star day, name one goal you have been standing under too long, admiring the decorations. A course you keep planning to start. A conversation you keep rehearsing. A change you keep describing to friends. Then take the first step through.
