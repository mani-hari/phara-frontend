Budha was born of a scandal. The Puranas say that Chandra, the Moon, carried off Tara, the wife of Brihaspati, the guru of the gods. There was a war in heaven over it. When Tara was finally returned, she was carrying a child, and the boy was so radiant, so obviously brilliant, that both the Moon and Brihaspati claimed him. Tara would not speak until the child himself demanded to know his father. Then she named the Moon. The boy was Budha, the planet Mercury. The traditional budha graha remedies, Wednesday prayers to Vishnu, green gram given, the Vishnu Sahasranamam, make most sense once you know that Mercury begins life as the child both sides wanted.

## Who is Budha, and why does he rule speech?

In Jyotish, Budha stands for intellect, speech, writing, trade, mathematics, wit and clever hands. His name comes from the same root as buddhi, intelligence. He is traditionally associated with Wednesday, the colour green, green gram and the emerald, and his linked deity is Vishnu. He is pictured young, green, gentle, riding a lion or seated on a chariot.

A strong Mercury in a chart is read as quick understanding and an easy way with words; a troubled one as nervousness, confusion, or trouble with study and communication.

Now look again at the story. Budha is born between two households, one of them the Moon, whom the tradition links with the mind, and the other the guru, whom it links with wisdom. The story says both fathers wanted him. The story is really about intelligence that belongs to no one side. Cleverness can serve anyone. That is its gift and its danger.

## Budha graha remedies from tradition

The practices are Wednesday practices.

- **Prayers to Vishnu**, the preserver, at home or at a Perumal temple.
- **The Vishnu Sahasranamam**, the thousand names of Vishnu, chanted in full or in part. There is a gentle introduction for [beginners to the Vishnu Sahasranamam](/blog/vishnu-sahasranamam-for-beginners).
- **Green gram**, whole moong, offered as naivedyam, often as sundal, or given away.
- **Green cloth** worn or given, and green leaves offered.
- **Om Budhaya Namah**, chanted a fixed number of times each Wednesday morning, nine or eighteen or a full round of a mala.

For students, many families also pray to Hayagriva, the horse-headed form of Vishnu, who is said to have recovered the Vedas and is worshipped as the lord of learning. He shares that role with the goddess of learning, as [Saraswati and Hayagriva](/blog/saraswati-and-hayagriva) explains, and families with an exam ahead sometimes arrange the [Hayagreeva homam](/blog/hayagreeva-homam-meaning).

## Thiruvenkadu and the grove of white

Budha's Navagraha temple is at Thiruvenkadu, near Poompuhar in Tamil Nadu. The name means the white forest, and the temple is dedicated to Shiva as Swetharanyeswarar, lord of the white grove. It is known for its Aghora murthi, a fierce form of Shiva that the temple tradition says appeared here to destroy a demon, and for its three temple tanks, named for Surya, Chandra and Agni.

Budha has his own shrine within the temple. Devotees traditionally bathe in or sprinkle themselves with water from the tanks, offer green gram and green cloth at Budha's shrine, and pray there on Wednesdays, especially for children who are about to begin school or sit exams.

Notice that Mercury's temple holds a tank named for his father, the Moon. The story keeps its family together.

## Clever speech, used to make peace

So Budha is the child who belongs to both families. In the Puranas, his cleverness does not deepen the war between the Moon and the guru; his arrival ends it. He is the reason the two sides stop fighting.

That is a good use for anyone's intelligence. This Wednesday, notice where your speech goes. Is it used to win, or to settle? Try one small experiment: in one conversation that has gone sour, use your words to find the thing both sides want.
