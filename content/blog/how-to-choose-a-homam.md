The first time a relative told me to "do a homam", I asked which one, and nobody could quite say. An aunt suggested Navagraha. A cousin said Ganapathi. My father said it depended. It turns out he was right, and the answer to which homam to perform starts not with the fire, but with what you are actually asking for.

## Which homam to perform: start with the need

A homam is a fire ritual, offerings made into a consecrated fire with mantras, each homam addressed to a particular deity for a particular purpose. Here is a plain map, by need, as families commonly use them.

**A new beginning.** A house, a business, a marriage, a new job. The Ganapathi homam is the traditional starting point, because Ganesha is the lord of beginnings. If you have never sat through one, [what happens in a Ganapathi homam](/blog/ganapathi-homam-meaning) walks through the morning.

**Studies and learning.** The Saraswati homam or [the Hayagreeva homam](/blog/hayagreeva-homam-meaning), chosen by family tradition and the student's needs.

**Health and elders.** The Mrityunjaya homam for serious illness and surgery; the Ayushya homam, often done on a birthday for long life; the Dhanvantari homam, addressed to the physician of the gods. [The Mrityunjaya homam](/blog/mrityunjaya-homam-meaning) has its own column, and my family knows it better than I would like.

**A demanding planetary period.** In Jyotish, a difficult dasha or a transit such as Shani's is traditionally met with a [Navagraha homam](/blog/navagraha-homam-meaning), which honours all nine grahas in one fire.

**Strength and protection.** The Chandi homam, which recites the whole Devi Mahatmyam, or the [Sudarshana homam](/blog/sudarshana-homam-meaning) to Vishnu's discus.

**Ancestors.** The Tila homam, with sesame, for those who have died.

That is not every homam, but it covers most of what families ask about.

## What if more than one fits?

This is where I went wrong the first time. I was worried about my job, my father's health and my son's schooling all at once, and I asked whether we should do three homams. Four, if you counted Ganapathi.

Our family astrologer in Madurai, who has known my parents for decades, laughed at me gently on the phone. "One sincere homam is better than five hurried ones," he said. He is not a man given to proverbs, so I listened.

A few practical points follow from that.

- **Most homams begin with Ganapati anyway.** You do not need a separate Ganapathi homam before every other one.
- **Choose the need that is loudest.** If health is the fear that wakes you at night, start there.
- **Resist stacking rituals out of anxiety.** It is very easy, especially from abroad, to try to buy peace of mind by booking more. The tradition does not work like a points system, and nobody is keeping score.

## When should you ask an astrologer?

Some families consult an astrologer for everything; some never do. Both are within the tradition.

A consultation can help when the question is specific. Is this one of the difficult [dasha periods](/blog/dasha-periods-explained), the long planetary chapters of a chart? Which day falls on my birth star? Is there a good muhurtham, an auspicious time, for the homam?

But a homam chosen from the heart, without a chart, is also valid. My grandmother never asked an astrologer which god to pray to. She knew what she was worried about, and she went to the god who looked after that.

## One small thing before you book

Before you book anything, sit down and write one sentence. Only one. What are you praying for? "That my father's surgery goes well." "That I find work before the visa runs out." "That our new house is a happy one."

Read it back. Often the sentence chooses the homam for you. And it is more than a planning step: that sentence is your sankalpam, the spoken statement of intention at the start of every ritual. When the priest asks for it, you will already have it ready, and it will be true.
