A friend from Kerala told me that on Vishu morning her grandmother would cover her eyes with both hands and walk her, half-asleep, to the puja room. Then the hands came away, and the first thing she saw of the new year was a brass vessel heaped with gold flowers, fruit, a mirror and a lit lamp, arranged for her in the dark, with love. That, to me, is the whole Vishu kani meaning: someone chose your first sight for you, and chose well.

## What does Vishu kani really mean?

*Kani* in Malayalam means the first thing seen, or a sight that is auspicious to behold. Vishu is the Kerala new year, the first day of the month of Medam, when the sun enters Mesha. The belief is simple: what you see first on the first morning colours the whole year. So families arrange the most abundant, beautiful, auspicious sight they can, and make sure it is the first thing everyone sees.

Underneath the belief is an idea I find more radical than it looks. We cannot choose what the year will bring. But we can arrange what we let in first. The grandmother's hands over the eyes are a small act of protection: not from the world, but from a careless first look at it. It is the same instinct behind the Tamil tray on [Puthandu, the Tamil New Year kept far from home](/blog/puthandu-tamil-new-year-usa), which falls on the very same morning.

## What goes into the urali?

The kani is arranged the night before in an *urali*, a wide bell-metal vessel. My friend rattled off her family's list as if it were a recipe:

- raw rice, filling the base, sometimes with a little paddy
- a heap of *konna* flowers, the bright yellow blossoms of the Indian laburnum, which bloom exactly in this season
- the *kani vellari*, a golden cucumber
- a halved coconut, jackfruit, ripe mango and bananas
- a few gold coins or a piece of jewellery
- the *val kannadi*, a traditional metal mirror, so that you see yourself inside the abundance
- a folded new cloth, a book of scripture, a few coins
- a lit *nilavilakku*, the tall brass lamp
- and at the centre, an image of Krishna

The yellow is deliberate. Gold, konna flowers, golden cucumber, lamplight: the whole arrangement glows. The mirror is my favourite part. You are not only looking at plenty. You are looking at yourself in the middle of it.

The eldest in the house usually wakes first, lights the lamp, and then leads the others in one by one, eyes closed, to open them before the kani.

## Kaineettam, sadya and the rest of the day

After the kani comes *kaineettam*, elders giving coins or small money gifts to the younger ones. My friend said her grandfather would press a coin into each grandchild's palm and fold their fingers over it. The gesture says: may your hands never be empty in the year ahead. Then come firecrackers, visits to the temple, especially Guruvayur and Sabarimala for those nearby, and the Vishu sadya, a vegetarian feast served on banana leaves.

Vishu falls on the first of Medam, usually in mid-April, the same day as Tamil Puthandu and close to many other spring new years. For the exact day, check the current year's panchangam or a [Hindu festival calendar for the USA](/blog/hindu-festival-calendar-usa). The season is crowded with festivals: [Ram Navami, which you can keep at home at noon](/blog/ram-navami-puja-at-home), and [Hanuman Jayanti and what it means](/blog/hanuman-jayanti-significance) fall around the same weeks. Krishna sits at the centre of the kani, but Rama is never far, and [Rama as maryada purushottama](/blog/rama-maryada-purushottama), the one who keeps his limits, pairs well with a festival about beginning rightly.

## Choose your first sight

Tirumoolar wrote in the Tirumantiram that the heart is a temple. If that is so, then the first thing we let into it each morning matters. For most of us now, the first sight of the day is a phone screen, chosen for us by whatever arrived overnight. This week, choose your first sight yourself. Leave a lamp, a photograph, a window with the curtain open, a flower in a glass, somewhere you will see it before the phone. A small urali of your own. Let the day begin with something you chose.
