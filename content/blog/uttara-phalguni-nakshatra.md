At a friend's wedding in New Jersey, in a rented hall with folding chairs and a very patient priest, there was a moment in the mantras when the priest paused to name Aryaman, the god of contracts and friendship. I had heard that name at every wedding of my life without noticing it. That evening I looked it up, and found that the same god presides over Uttara phalguni nakshatra, Uthiram in Tamil, the star of promises kept.

## Uttara Phalguni nakshatra: Aryaman, the Sun and the bed's back legs

Uthiram's presiding god is Aryaman, one of the Adityas of the Vedas, the god of hospitality, friendship and the marriage bond. In Vedic weddings, the bride circles the fire and offers grain to Aryaman, asking to be released from her father's house and bound to her husband's. Its ruling planet, the adhipati in Jyotish terms, is Surya, the Sun. The star begins in Simha, the lion, and passes into [Kanya, the maiden](/blog/kanya-rasi).

Its symbol is the back legs of a bed. The star before it, [Purva Phalguni, the star of rest and delight](/blog/purva-phalguni-nakshatra), has the front legs. My wife pointed out that this is a very accurate picture of marriage: the first delight comes in front, and the steady support holds up the back for decades.

Tradition describes Uthiram-born people as reliable, generous, loyal, the friend who shows up with a car when yours breaks down. It is a description the tradition offers, not a destiny it demands.

## Why do the gods marry on Panguni Uthiram?

Once a year, in the Tamil month of Panguni, the full moon falls on Uthiram. Panguni Uthiram is the great wedding day of the temple calendar. In temple tradition, Shiva and Parvati married on this day, Murugan and Deivanai married on this day, and Rama and Sita too, depending on whom you ask. Temples across Tamil Nadu hold kalyana utsavams, divine wedding festivals, and families bring their own newlyweds for blessings.

It is also the day Tamil weddings remember Andal. In the Nachiyar Tirumozhi, the girl-saint of Srivilliputhur describes a dream of her own wedding to Vishnu, in the decad of verses called Varanam Ayiram. It opens with a thousand elephants circling the town as the bridegroom arrives. Those verses are still sung at Tamil weddings, and my mother sang a few lines on our video call the week my cousin married. Anyone curious can read about [the seven steps of the saptapadi](/blog/saptapadi-seven-steps-meaning), the vows that sit at the centre of the ceremony.

A wedding is the most public promise most of us make. Uthiram is the star that remembers it is also the most daily one.

## A temple for marriage prayers

The widely shared Tamil star list gives Uthiram the Mangalyeswarar temple at Edayathumangalam, near Lalgudi. Mangalyam is the sacred thread of marriage, and the temple is visited by families praying for marriages, both those waiting for one and those hoping to mend one.

The monthly observance is modest. Because Surya rules the star, many Uthiram-born people light a lamp on Sundays; [how Surya is worshipped](/blog/surya-graha-worship) explains the simple form. Those waiting for a match often keep Swayamvara Parvathi prayers. On the monthly Uthiram day, when the Moon returns to your birth star, light a lamp and quietly renew one promise. And when Uthiram comes back in your birth month, that star birthday is a good day to renew a vow, whether it is a marriage or a friendship.

The next star is [Hasta, the star of skilled hands](/blog/hasta-nakshatra), which follows naturally: first the promise, then the work of keeping it.

## One small thing to carry into this week

Keep one promise you made casually. The call you said you would make. The visit you said "we must" about. The book you borrowed. Nobody is keeping score, which is exactly why it matters. Aryaman is the god of the kept word, and most kept words are small ones.
