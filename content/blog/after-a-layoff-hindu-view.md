When a friend in Ohio was laid off, the first thing he said on the phone was not about money. It was about the visa. The second was that he had not told his parents yet. Coping with a layoff spiritually, for many of us who live here, means carrying both of those at once: the clock that immigration law starts, and the silence we keep with family back home. The tradition does not make either disappear. It does offer a place to stand while you deal with them.

## Coping with a layoff spiritually, without pretending

The Bhagavad Gita, in its second chapter, describes the sthitaprajna, the person of steady wisdom. Arjuna asks Krishna how such a person speaks, sits and walks. Krishna answers that they are not shaken by sorrow, not carried away by pleasure, free of craving, fear and anger. The portrait is fuller in [the sthitaprajna and the steady mind](/blog/sthitaprajna-steady-mind), and it reads differently in the week your badge stops working.

It is easy to misread this as a demand to look calm. It is not. My friend was not calm. He was angry, frightened and embarrassed, and he said so. Steadiness in the Gita's sense is not a calm face on the outside. It is a still point somewhere inside that the storm cannot fully reach. You can be very upset and still have it. You find it by noticing that you are the one watching the fear, not only the fear itself.

The Gita's context matters too. Arjuna, when Krishna teaches him, has just collapsed on the battlefield. The whole teaching is given to a man who has lost his footing. Anyone who reads [what the Gita says about anxiety](/blog/bhagavad-gita-on-anxiety) after a layoff will recognise him.

## What about the visa worry?

For anyone on a work visa, a layoff starts a clock. That clock runs louder for immigrants than for anyone else in the office, and it can make every other thought harder.

Here the order of calls matters.

1. **An immigration attorney first.** Rules and grace periods are specific and they change. Only a qualified attorney can tell you what applies to you. Do not rely on forums or friends' stories.
2. **Prayer second.** Not because prayer matters less, but because a clear legal picture makes the prayer less frantic.
3. **The household third.** Tell your spouse the facts plainly. Then agree, together, not to let the fear run the house. One conversation a day about the visa, at a fixed time. Not every meal.

None of this is spiritual advice pretending to be legal advice. It is the opposite: the tradition asks you to do your duty, and here the duty is to get proper help.

## Small practices for hard weeks

A layoff breaks the shape of the day. The practices that helped my friend were mostly about rebuilding that shape.

- **A morning lamp,** at the same time the commute used to start.
- **A walk,** every day, outside, whatever the weather.
- **A fixed time for job hunting, and a fixed time to stop.** Nine to three, say. After three, the laptop closes.

The Tirukkural, Thiruvalluvar's book of couplets, has a whole chapter on not losing heart in trouble. Its first couplet, kural 621, says:

*Idukkan varungaal naguga adhanai*\
*aduththoorvadhu aqdhoppadhu il.*

When trouble comes, smile; nothing else so well overcomes it. It sounds almost too simple. My friend taped it to his monitor and said it was the only advice anyone gave him that did not require a budget. For the days after the smile runs out, I have written about [what the Tirukkural says about effort](/blog/tirukkural-on-effort).

Some people, after a layoff, decide to build something of their own, and for that first morning there is [the new business opening pooja](/blog/new-business-opening-pooja). When the search begins in earnest, [a daily prayer during the job search](/blog/prayer-for-a-job-hindu) and [a two-minute prayer before an interview](/blog/prayer-before-an-interview) may help.

## One small thing to carry

Call your parents this week and tell them. My friend waited three weeks, and when he finally did, his father's first question was whether he was eating properly. His mother started lighting an extra lamp.

The burden gets lighter when it is shared. Parents usually know when something is wrong anyway, from the tone of the calls. Telling them lets them help, even if all they can do is pray from the other side of the world at their six o'clock.
