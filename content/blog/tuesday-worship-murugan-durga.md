In Chennai, when I was growing up, Tuesday meant Murugan. My aunt, the one who still checks the rahu kalam before booking anything, would light a small lamp in a hollowed lemon peel for Durga every Tuesday afternoon. Here in New Jersey, my North Indian colleagues think of Hanuman on Tuesdays; one of them fasts until evening. If you are wondering which god is worshipped on Tuesday, the answer is that all three are right. Murugan, Durga and Hanuman share the day because Tuesday belongs to one fiery planet, Mars, and all three are gods of courage.

## Which god do Hindu homes pray to on Tuesday, and why?

Tuesday is Mangalavara in Sanskrit, Sevvai in Tamil: the day of Mangala, the planet Mars. In Jyotish, Mars stands for courage, energy, protection and the will to act. So the day goes to the warrior gods.

- **Murugan** is traditionally associated with Sevvai in Tamil homes. He is the young commander of the gods' army, spear in hand, and in the Navagraha tradition he is the deity linked with Mars. The planet's own worship is covered in [Angaraka, Mars, and his worship](/blog/angaraka-mars-worship).
- **Durga** is the Goddess as protector, riding a lion into battle. Tuesday worship of Durga asks for courage and protection.
- **Hanuman** is strength and devotion together. Across much of North India, Tuesday is Hanuman's day, with temples crowded and sindoor offered.

Regional customs differ, and families often keep more than one. My mother prays to Murugan on Tuesday mornings and lights a lamp for Durga later. Nobody in our family found this contradictory.

## Durga in the rahu kalam, Murugan at dusk

The Tamil custom my aunt kept is worth describing, because many people ask about it. Rahu kalam, the daily period considered inauspicious for new beginnings, falls in the mid-afternoon on Tuesdays. Rather than simply avoiding that window, many women in Tamil Nadu use it for Durga. They cut a lemon in half, turn the halves inside out to make small cups, fill them with ghee or oil, add a wick and light them before the Goddess. The idea is that the fiercest protector is the right one to pray to during the most difficult hour. [Rahu kalam explained](/blog/rahu-kalam-explained) sets out the timing clearly.

At dusk, Murugan. Many families recite the Kanda Sashti Kavasam, the Tamil hymn of protection, on Tuesday evenings; the piece on [the Kanda Sashti Kavasam's meaning](/blog/kanda-sashti-kavasam-meaning) goes through it. Others prefer the Skanda Guru Kavasam. Those who can visit a Murugan temple; the six great ones in Tamil Nadu are described in [the six abodes of Murugan](/blog/six-abodes-of-murugan).

In the north, Tuesday evening often means the Hanuman Chalisa, a visit to a Hanuman temple, sindoor and oil offered, and sometimes a fast until sunset.

## What does Tuesday worship look like on a workday?

This is the real question for most of us. My Tuesdays are full of meetings, and by the time I get home the rahu kalam is long past, in any time zone.

Here is what I do, in about five minutes:

1. Wash hands and face after coming home.
2. Light one lamp before the picture of Murugan on our shelf.
3. Offer a red flower if there is one, or a few grains of rice if not.
4. Recite one hymn, or simply the first few lines of the Kavasam, which I know by heart.
5. Offer a small piece of jaggery or a banana, and share it with my son afterwards.

That is all. My wife sometimes adds the lemon lamp for Durga on weekends, when there is time to do it properly. There is an overview of [which god is worshipped on which day](/blog/which-god-on-which-day) if you want to see how Tuesday fits into the week.

## Courage for one specific thing

Arunagirinathar, the poet-saint of Murugan, sang in the Kandar Anubhuti of the spear that cuts through fear. I have never been a brave person. But I have noticed that Tuesday prayers work best for me when I ask for something specific: the courage to have a hard conversation, to make a call I have been avoiding, to say no to something I should not take on.

One small thing to carry into this week: on Tuesday evening, light one lamp for whichever form your family knows best, Murugan, Durga or Hanuman, and ask for courage for one specific thing. Then, on Wednesday, do it.
