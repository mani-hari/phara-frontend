At Newark airport, waiting at the gate for the long flight to Chennai, I noticed that I always say the same short prayer before boarding. I did not choose it. It was simply there, the way my mother's lamp is always there behind her on our video calls. Only later did I learn that Revati nakshatra, the last of the twenty-seven birth stars, belongs to Pushan, the Vedic god of roads and safe journeys. I had been praying to him for years without knowing his name.

## Revati nakshatra: Pushan, Mercury and the fish

Revati nakshatra, which we spell Revathi, closes the circle of twenty-seven star groups the Moon travels through each month; whichever of them the Moon occupied at your birth is your star. Its presiding deity is Pushan, the nourisher and guardian of roads. Its adhipati, or ruling planet, is Budha, Mercury. Its symbols are a fish and a drum; the fish is the one that finds its way through dark water.

Tradition describes Revathi-born people as kind, gentle, protective of the weak and fond of travel. My cousin is a Revathi, and she is the one who drove four hours to sit with an aunt nobody else visited. One example proves nothing, of course. I hold the description the way I hold a family saying: a description, not a destiny.

## Who watches over travellers?

In the Rig Veda, Pushan is the god who knows every path. He finds lost cattle, guides herdsmen, and leads the souls of the dead safely onward. One of his hymns asks him to drive away whatever lurks on the road and to lead the traveller along good paths. It is an old prayer, from people who walked everywhere, and it still sounds exactly right at an airport gate at two in the morning.

Revati sits at the very end of the zodiac, the last star of [Meena, the sign of the fish](/blog/meena-rasi), and the last before the cycle begins again with Ashwini. So it is both an ending and a threshold, the last mile of one journey and the first step of the next. If you want to see the circle close, read on to [Ashwini, the first star](/blog/ashwini-nakshatra).

Vishnu's first avatar belongs here too. In the Matsya story, a small fish asked the king Manu to save it, grew enormous, and then towed Manu's boat through the great flood to safety. The fish that finds its way through dark water is a guide, not just a symbol. Matsya stands first among [the ten avatars of Vishnu](/blog/dashavatara-meaning), and the order of that list tells its own story.

## Karukudi and a prayer before the road

The Kailasanathar temple at Karukudi, near Thottiyam in Tamil Nadu, is the temple the widely shared Tamil star list links with Revati nakshatra. I have not been yet. It is on the list my mother keeps for me.

Because Budha rules the star, Wednesday prayers are a traditional observance, and Vishnu worship sits naturally with Revati because of the fish. Many families light a lamp before a long journey, and again each month when Revathi comes round. The Revathi of your birth month is your star birthday, and tradition considers it a good day to set out on a journey; my aunt in Chennai will tell you the date if the panchangam does not.

If you have ever wondered why the Moon's position matters so much in these traditions, start with [Chandra and Monday worship](/blog/chandra-graha-worship); [Vedic vs Western astrology](/blog/vedic-vs-western-astrology) explains why the two systems give different answers.

## One small thing for this week

I still say the same prayer at the gate. Now I also say it in smaller places: before my son gets on the school bus, before I back the car out of the driveway on an icy morning, before my father goes in for a check-up in Chennai and I can only wait for the call.

One small thing for this week: before you drive, board a train or send your child off to school, pause for one breath and one line of prayer. It does not need to be long. Pushan knows the road.
