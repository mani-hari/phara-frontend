A friend in Houston spent five months applying for jobs. The thing that kept him going was not a mantra that promised an offer. It was a lamp he lit every morning before he opened his inbox. When people ask me about a Hindu prayer for a job, something a family can keep during a long search, I tell them about that lamp first. The prayer did not find him the job. It helped him keep looking.

## Which god do Hindus pray to for a job?

There is no single answer, and regional customs differ, but most families turn to one of four.

**Ganesha,** for new beginnings and the clearing of obstacles. He is worshipped before anything starts, for reasons [why Ganesha comes first](/blog/why-ganesha-comes-first) sets out, and a job search is a long series of starts.

**Lakshmi,** for livelihood and prosperity. Many families light a lamp to her on Fridays, and those who decide to start something of their own often begin with [a new business opening pooja](/blog/new-business-opening-pooja) to Ganesha, Lakshmi and Kubera. The Kubera stories sit beside hers, and [what the Kubera story means](/blog/kubera-story-meaning) gives the older picture of wealth and its keeper.

**Hanuman,** for courage and stamina, especially on Tuesdays and Saturdays. A long search needs endurance more than luck.

**Saraswati,** for clear speech, especially before interviews.

Your family may have its own deity, the kula devata, and that is always a good place to start. The god you already know is the one you will actually pray to at six in the morning.

## A daily prayer to keep while you search

My friend's routine was simple, and I have since suggested it to several people.

1. **A morning lamp.** Before the laptop, before the phone. One lamp, one minute.
2. **Om Gam Ganapataye Namah, eleven times.** Om Gam is Ganesha's seed syllable; the mantra is a salutation to him. Eleven repetitions take less than a minute.
3. **One application sent with full attention.** Not ten applications sent in a daze. One, read carefully, tailored properly, sent with a breath.

That third step is the part people skip, and it is the part I think matters most. The Bhagavad Gita, in chapter two, has the line most of us heard from our grandparents: karmanye vadhikaraste ma phaleshu kadachana, your right is to the work alone, never to its fruits. For a job seeker, this is not a counsel of despair. It is a relief. You are responsible for the application. You are not responsible for the recruiter's mood. I find the line easier to live with after reading [the Gita on work without attachment](/blog/gita-on-work-without-attachment).

## When the search is long

Five months is a long time. For some, especially those of us on visas, it is longer still, with a clock running in the background. A few things helped my friend, and they are as much part of the prayer as the lamp.

- **Rest days.** He stopped applying on Sundays. He went to the temple instead, or simply walked.
- **Walks.** Every afternoon, forty minutes, no phone.
- **Telling one friend the truth.** Not the polished version for the family group chat. The real one.
- **The practical work.** Networking, a good resume, practice interviews. Prayer alongside these, never instead.

If the search follows a layoff, [steadiness after a layoff](/blog/after-a-layoff-hindu-view) talks about the visa worry too. And when an interview finally comes, [a two-minute prayer before an interview](/blog/prayer-before-an-interview) is a good companion.

## One small thing to carry

Before each application this week, pause for one breath. Offer the effort, silently, to whichever god you pray to. Then press send.

The outcome is not yours to carry alone. My friend got his job in the sixth month, at a company he had applied to twice before. He still lights the lamp before he opens his inbox. He says the inbox is less frightening now.
