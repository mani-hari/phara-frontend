A devoted sage once wanted to worship Shiva and ignore Parvati. He got his answer in the form of a god who could no longer be split in two. The Ardhanarishvara meaning is right there in the name: ardha, half; nari, woman; ishvara, lord. The lord who is half woman. Shiva on the right, Parvati on the left, one body, one face divided down the middle, one ear with a man's ring and one with a woman's.

## The Bhringi story

The sage was called Bhringi, and his devotion to Shiva was total. So total, in fact, that it had no room for anyone else. When he came to Kailasa to perform pradakshina, walking around the object of worship, he wanted to circle Shiva alone. Parvati was seated beside her husband. Bhringi asked her to move. She did not.

So Bhringi, determined, took the form of a beetle, or in some tellings a bee, and tried to fly between them, circling only Shiva. Parvati moved closer to Shiva, so there was no gap. Bhringi squeezed through anyway.

In many tellings Parvati, tired of being treated as an obstacle, withdraws from Bhringi everything in him that came from her, which is to say his flesh and blood, the Shakti, the living energy, in his body. The sage collapses into a skeleton, unable to stand. Shiva, taking pity, gives him a third leg so he can stay upright, which is why Bhringi appears in temple sculpture as a gaunt, three-legged figure.

And then, to settle the matter, Shiva draws Parvati into himself, and the two become Ardhanarishvara. Now Bhringi can circle one or the other only by circling both. The story is told in Tamil and Puranic tellings with variations; the version you grew up with may have the beetle but not the skeleton, or the skeleton but not the beetle. The point is the same.

## Ardhanarishvara meaning in the Shiva Purana

The Shiva Purana gives a second, older-sounding origin. Brahma, the creator, had made the first beings, but they did not multiply. Creation was stalling. Brahma prayed to Shiva, and Shiva appeared before him in a form that was half male and half female. From that form the goddess separated herself, and gave Brahma the power that was missing. Only then did creation begin to move.

This is the Ardhanarishvara meaning at its most philosophical. In the language of the tradition, Shiva is consciousness, still and aware, and Shakti is energy, moving and making. Consciousness without energy does nothing. Energy without consciousness has no direction. Shiva and shakti union means that nothing is made, nothing moves, nothing lives, without both.

If the image stays with you, two roads lead on from it: [Shakti as living energy in daily practice](/blog/shakti-in-daily-practice), and [what the goddess stories say about women](/blog/women-in-hinduism-goddess).

## Is it about marriage or about each of us?

It is easy to read Ardhanarishvara as a picture of the ideal couple, and many people do: two partners so close they share a body. That reading is lovely and not wrong. If the image takes you towards your own marriage, try [praying together as a couple](/blog/praying-together-as-a-couple).

But the more searching reading turns it inward. The half man half woman god of Hindu art is also a picture of every person. Each of us carries stillness and movement, awareness and action, firmness and gentleness, the qualities a culture tends to label masculine or feminine. Bhringi's mistake was not preferring Shiva. It was thinking he could love half of something and discard the rest. We make that mistake about ourselves all the time, polishing the half of our nature we approve of and treating the other half as an obstacle to walk around.

The Ardhanareeswarar temple at Tiruchengode, on a hill in Tamil Nadu, is traditionally associated with this form, and it is one of the few places where it is the principal image. Elsewhere Ardhanarishvara appears on temple walls and in bronze, often near [Nataraja, the dancing Shiva](/blog/nataraja-symbolism), who is the same god seen in motion.

## Practise the half you usually hand away

Notice one quality you usually hand to someone else. Perhaps you let your partner be the firm one, or a colleague be the gentle one, or you leave the planning to a parent and keep the feeling for yourself. This week, practise that quality yourself, a little, on purpose. Not to replace the other person. Just to find out that you were never only half.
