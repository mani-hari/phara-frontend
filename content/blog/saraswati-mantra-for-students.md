The Saraswati mantra for students that most families teach is Ya Kundendu, and it is a picture before it is a prayer. A goddess dressed in white, seated on a white lotus, a veena resting in her hands, bright as jasmine and the winter moon. Hold that picture for thirty seconds before you study, then begin, and see what happens to your focus. Nothing else is required.

Many children in the US learn this verse at a temple Sunday class or from a grandparent on a video call, and then it drifts into something said quickly before an exam. It can be more useful than that.

## What does Ya Kundendu mean?

The verse is in Sanskrit and is widely recited across India. Here it is in paraphrase, line by line.

She who is white as the kunda jasmine, the moon and a garland of frost. She who is clothed in pure white. Whose hands are graced by the veena. Who is seated on a white lotus. Who is always honoured by Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva and all the gods. May that Saraswati, the goddess, protect me and remove every trace of dullness from my mind.

Notice the last line. The word is jadya, which means sluggishness, heaviness, the fog that settles when you have been staring at the same page for twenty minutes without reading it. The verse does not ask for marks or success. It asks for a clear mind. That is a very honest thing to ask for before homework.

Notice the colours too. Everything in the picture is white: the flower, the moon, the frost, the clothes, the lotus. White is the colour of a clean page. The image is doing the work of settling the eyes before the mind is asked to do anything.

## A Saraswati mantra for students, used as a study ritual

Here is the simplest way I would use it as a Saraswati vandana before study, whether you are twelve and facing fractions or a graduate student with a thesis chapter due.

Clear the desk of everything except what you need for the first task. Light a small lamp if the household allows it; if you are in a dorm or a shared room, simply sit upright and put both feet on the floor. Take three slow breaths, letting each exhale run a little longer than the inhale. Recite the verse once, aloud or silently, holding the picture of the white goddess while you do. Then begin the first task immediately. Do not check the phone between the verse and the first line of work.

That last instruction is the most important one. The verse is a doorway. If you walk through it and then turn around to look at notifications, it becomes decoration.

For younger children, a Saraswati prayer for kids can be even shorter: fold hands, one breath, the verse or just its last line, then open the book. For more to choose from, see [simple mantras children can learn](/blog/simple-mantras-for-kids). Many families also say [Vakratunda Mahakaya before any beginning](/blog/vakratunda-mahakaya-meaning), and the two sit well together.

## Why does a ritual before study help?

The body learns cues. If the same verse is said at the same desk at roughly the same time, the mind begins to recognise it as the signal that switching has ended and one thing is about to begin. Athletes have pre-game routines. Musicians tune before they play. This is the student's version, and it happens to come with a goddess of learning attached.

Many people find that the first stretch of focus after a short ritual lasts noticeably longer than one that starts cold. You may not. Either way, the prayer sits alongside effort, sleep and good teaching, never in place of them. Saraswati is honoured in the tradition as the giver of knowledge, and the tradition has also always insisted that knowledge is earned by practice. Her own festival, [Vasant Panchami at the start of spring](/blog/vasant-panchami-saraswati), is when many children write their first letters. If exams are close, there is more on [a calm prayer before exams](/blog/prayer-before-exams), and on [how the teacher-student prayer Om Sahana Vavatu](/blog/om-sahana-vavatu-meaning) frames learning as something shared.

## Seven days, one verse, one task

Before homework or a work session, every day for seven days: clear the desk, three breaths, the verse once, then one task. Keep a note of roughly how long the first stretch of focus lasts before you reach for something else. Do not try to improve it. Just watch. By the end of the week, you will know whether the verse has become a cue for your body, and that knowledge is worth more than any promise I could make.
