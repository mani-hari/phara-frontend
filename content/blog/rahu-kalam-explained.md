Rahu kalam is a period of about ninety minutes each day that Jyotish, the Indian astrological tradition, considers unsuitable for starting new things. It is not a time of danger, only a time many families prefer not to begin in. I learned this at eleven, in the back seat of my uncle's Ambassador outside his house in Madurai, dressed for a cousin's wedding. My aunt looked at her watch and said we would not leave until rahu kalam was over. We sat in that hot car for ninety minutes. I thought it was the silliest thing I had ever seen. It took me years to understand what those ninety minutes gave her.

## Rahu kalam meaning and how it is calculated

What is rahu kalam, exactly? Take the hours of daylight, from sunrise to sunset, and divide them into eight equal parts. On each weekday, one of those eight parts is assigned to Rahu, one of the two shadow planets of Jyotish, associated with confusion, obsession and the unexpected. If you want the fuller picture of Rahu and his twin, [Rahu and Ketu explained](/blog/rahu-ketu-dosha-explained) is a calm introduction.

The rahu kalam timing calculation follows a fixed weekday order. Assuming a sunrise around six, it falls roughly:

- Monday: 7:30 to 9:00 in the morning
- Tuesday: 3:00 to 4:30 in the afternoon
- Wednesday: 12:00 to 1:30
- Thursday: 1:30 to 3:00
- Friday: 10:30 to 12:00
- Saturday: 9:00 to 10:30
- Sunday: 4:30 to 6:00 in the evening

These are only approximations. Because the calculation starts from actual sunrise and sunset, the slot shifts with the season and with where you are. Rahu kalam in Chennai and rahu kalam in Seattle on the same day are not the same clock time, and in a northern winter the eighths of daylight are short.

## Should you avoid rahu kalam?

The tradition's advice is narrower than the fear around it. It suggests not beginning important new things during this window: a journey, a wedding, a business, a house purchase, signing a major agreement. It does not forbid living. You can work, cook, eat, study, drive to the office and finish what you already started.

Rahu kalam is also, curiously, considered a good time for prayer, especially to Durga. In many Tamil homes and temples, women light lamps to Durga during rahu kalam on Tuesdays and Fridays. The hour that is poor for beginnings is rich for turning inward.

Two other daily periods follow similar logic. Yamagandam and gulika kalam are also eighths of the day, assigned to Yama and to Gulika, a son of Shani, the slow planet at the centre of [Shani Sade Sati](/blog/shani-sade-sati-explained). Yamagandam is generally avoided for auspicious starts; gulika is said to repeat whatever is begun in it, so it is avoided for sad things and sometimes chosen for happy ones. For choosing a positively good time, rather than avoiding a poor one, see [what is a muhurtham](/blog/what-is-a-muhurtham).

## Living with rahu kalam in a modern schedule

For most people abroad, strict observance is impractical. Meetings are set by calendars in other time zones. Flights leave when they leave. Nobody tells a manager they will join the call after ninety minutes.

So people adapt. Some look up rahu kalam only for the truly large beginnings, a house closing or a wedding. Some leave the house symbolically a few minutes before the window starts, bag by the door, and then return for a coffee. Some simply ignore it and feel no worse. All of these are honest responses. Whether the idea is literally true is a question worth sitting with, and [is astrology real](/blog/is-astrology-real) sits with it fairly.

The Bhagavad Gita says we have a right to our actions, never to their fruits. I think that is what my aunt was doing in the car. She could not control the wedding, the traffic or the rest of her nephew's life. She could control when she began. Those ninety minutes were her way of starting carefully.

## One small thing to carry into the week

Look up rahu kalam for your own city once. Notice where it falls in your week: during the morning commute, the school run, the weekly team call. Then decide for yourself what, if anything, you want to schedule around it. If you use it for nothing else, let it be a pause, ninety minutes when you are allowed to begin nothing at all.
