Thatha, my grandfather, measured every length of cloth twice in his shop in Kumbakonam before he cut it, and folded each piece the same way, corner to corner, before it went into the customer's bag. As a boy I found it maddening. Now I think of him whenever someone mentions Kanya rasi, the sign of care in small things. He was not a Kanya, as far as I know. But he lived like one.

## Kanya rasi: the maiden, the grain and Mercury

Kanya rasi is the sixth of the twelve signs, and when an astrologer asks your rasi he means where the Moon stood at your birth, not the Sun. It holds the last three quarters of Uttara Phalguni, all of Hasta and the first half of Chitra. Its ruling planet is Budha, Mercury, and Budha is also exalted here, at its strongest. At home we always said Kanni. A Western chart would call it Virgo, give or take a sign.

The symbol is a maiden in a boat, holding grain in one hand and fire in the other. I like that picture. The grain is the harvest, the careful result of a whole season's work. The fire is the attention needed to keep it. The boat is the fact that none of it stands still.

## Is attention to detail a kind of devotion?

Tradition describes Kanya natives as analytical, careful, helpful and modest, the people who notice the typo, remember the allergy, check the lock twice. The shadow is worry and over-criticism, of others and above all of oneself. As always, I hold the portrait lightly. It is a description, not a sentence.

The question the sign asks is whether small care can be a form of worship. I think Thiruvalluvar would have said yes. The Tirukkural has a whole chapter, the sixty-first, on madiyinmai, the absence of sloth, about work done without delay. Its most famous couplet is Kural 605:

*Neduneer maravi madi thuyil naankum*\
*keduneeraar kaamak kalan.*

Delay, forgetfulness, sloth and too much sleep: these four are the pleasure-boat of those bound for ruin. It is a stern verse. But notice the boat again. Here it is the wrong boat, the one that drifts. Kanya's boat is the other one, steered with grain and fire. Valluvar's chapter on effort, which I wrote about in [what the Tirukkural says about effort](/blog/tirukkural-on-effort), is the Kanya temperament in ten couplets; Thatha, who measured every length of cloth twice, is in [Thatha's shop and honest trade](/blog/honest-trade-thathas-shop).

What Thatha taught me, without ever saying it, was that care is not the same as worry. Care finishes the task and puts it down. Worry keeps picking it up again. The first is devotion. The second is only fear.

## Thiruvenkadu, Wednesdays and Saraswati

Mercury's temple is Thiruvenkadu near Sirkazhi, one of the Navagraha shrines of the Kaveri country, and Kanya people are often taken there at least once. The [guide to Budha and Wednesday worship](/blog/budha-graha-worship) explains the traditional observances. Wednesday prayers, a lamp and a green offering are the simplest way to begin.

Saraswati, the goddess of learning, sits naturally with Kanya, for the precision and study the sign values. Many families with Kanya children keep a small image of her near the study table, and some arrange [the Saraswati homam for students](/blog/saraswati-homam-for-students), a fire offering to the goddess, before exams or at the start of a school year.

The sign that follows, [Tula, the scales](/blog/tula-rasi), turns Kanya's care into a sense of proportion. And if you want to see how Kanya fits into your whole chart, [reading your birth chart calmly](/blog/reading-your-birth-chart-calmly) is a sensible place to start.

## One small thing for this week

My son folds his clothes the way eleven-year-olds do, which is to say he does not. Last Sunday I showed him Thatha's fold, corner to corner. He did two shirts properly and then lost interest. Two is a start.

One small thing to carry into this week: do one routine task with complete care. Fold the clothes, wash the pot, write the email slowly. Then stop there. Care is not the same as worry.
