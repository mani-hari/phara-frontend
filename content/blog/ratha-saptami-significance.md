Most of us have not watched a sunrise on purpose in months. Ratha Saptami is a day built entirely around doing exactly that. The core of Ratha Saptami significance is simple: it is kept as the birthday of Surya, the sun, and it marks the moment, in tradition, when his chariot turns north and the days begin to lengthen in earnest. You honour it with a dawn bath, a kolam, a pot of sweet pongal and water offered to the rising sun. None of it needs a temple. All of it needs you to be awake and outside at first light.

## What is Ratha Saptami significance?

Ratha Saptami falls on the seventh day of the bright half of Magha, usually late January or February. Ratha means chariot and saptami means seventh. The sun, in the tradition, rides a chariot drawn by seven horses, one for each day of the week, or for the seven colours folded inside white light, depending on who explains it. On this day the chariot is imagined turning toward the north.

It is the natural sequel to Makar Sankranti and Pongal a few weeks earlier. Those celebrate the harvest and the sun's entry into a new sign. This one celebrates the sun himself. In many South Indian homes it is also the last of the winter festival days before the heat begins.

Your body already knows this season. The mornings are getting lighter, a minute or two at a time. Ratha Saptami asks you to notice.

## Why bathe with seven leaves?

The traditional Ratha Saptami bath is taken at dawn, with seven erukku leaves, from the arka plant, placed on the head, the shoulders and the feet, with a little raw rice and turmeric laid on them. You pour water over yourself with the leaves in place, often facing east. Seven leaves for seven horses. Some families say a short verse to Surya while bathing.

A word of practical caution. Erukku, or calotropis, has a milky sap that can irritate the eyes and skin. Handle the leaves gently, do not break the stems near your face, keep them away from children's eyes, and wash well afterwards with plain water. If you cannot find the leaves, which is likely outside India, a bath at dawn with a moment of attention is the practice. The leaf is a reminder; the dawn is the point.

## Ratha Saptami significance at home: kolam, pongal and the Aditya Hrudayam

After the bath, draw a chariot kolam at the door: a simple rice-flour drawing of a chariot with a sun disc, sometimes with seven small lines for the horses. It takes ten minutes and gives the morning a shape.

Then cook sakkarai pongal, the sweet rice and lentil dish with jaggery, traditionally in a pot set where it catches the sun; there is a full method in [sakkarai pongal for pooja](/blog/sakkarai-pongal-for-pooja). Offer it with water poured from cupped hands toward the rising sun. This offering of water is called arghya. Watch the light through the falling water.

Many families chant the Aditya Hrudayam, the hymn to the sun that the sage Agastya teaches Rama on the battlefield in the war chapters of the Ramayana, when Rama is exhausted and unsure. [The meaning of the Aditya Hrudayam](/blog/aditya-hrudayam-meaning) takes the hymn verse by verse, and [surya namaskar](/blog/surya-namaskar-meaning) is its movement in the body.

At Tirumala, the day is a festival of its own: the processional deity is carried around the temple on seven different vahanas, divine mounts, from sunrise to night, beginning with the sun's own chariot.

## The first ten minutes of daylight

Seasonal festivals keep turning the wheel: [Vasant Panchami's call to spring](/blog/vasant-panchami-saraswati) comes just before this one, and a few weeks earlier the winter season had its great dawn festival in [Arudra Darshan](/blog/arudra-darshan-meaning).

Try this for a week from Ratha Saptami. Be outside for the first ten minutes of daylight. Face east. Do not look directly at the sun; look at the sky around it, the trees, the light on the pavement. Breathe slowly, a little longer on the out-breath than the in-breath. That is all.

Many people find that morning light is the one thing that resets a drifting sleep schedule. The tradition would put it differently, as an offering to Surya. You do not need to decide which description is right. Notice your sleep by the seventh day, and notice how the early start feels; [waking in the early hours](/blog/brahma-muhurta-waking-early) is the natural next step if you like it.
