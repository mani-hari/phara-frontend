Hinduism never asked traders to be poor. It asked them to be straight. Artha, wealth and worldly success, sits among the four aims of life, but always inside dharma, right conduct, like water inside a riverbank. That is the short answer to what business ethics in Hinduism looks like: profit is welcome, even blessed, as long as it is earned cleanly and used well. The bank is not there to stop the water. It is there so the water can go somewhere.

## What do Hindu business ethics look like at the counter?

The tradition lists four purusharthas, aims of a human life: dharma, artha, kama and moksha, right conduct, wealth, pleasure and liberation. [The four purusharthas](/blog/four-purusharthas) explains how they fit together. What matters here is the order. Dharma comes first, and artha is pursued inside it.

Valluvar, writing in the Tirukkural, puts this in the language of the shop. In the chapter on impartiality he describes the ideal person as being like a true balance, a weighing scale that stands level and does not tip to either side. Two couplets later, in Kural 120, he turns to traders directly:

*Vaanigam seyvaarkku vaanigam peenip*\
*piravum thamapol seyin.*

The best trade for traders, he says, is to guard the goods of others as if they were their own. Not "be generous" or "give it all away". Just treat the other person's interest with the same care as yours. That is dharma in business in a single couplet. There is more on Valluvar's view of money in [the Thirukkural on wealth](/blog/tirukkural-on-wealth).

## Shubh and labh: auspicious profit

In North Indian shops you will often see two words painted by the door: shubh and labh. Shubh labh means "auspicious" and "profit". They are sometimes described as the two sons of Ganesha, and the pairing is deliberate. Profit on its own is just labh. Profit that is shubh, clean, well-earned and well-used, is what is worth praying for.

At Diwali, many trading families bring out a new account book and bless it in the Lakshmi puja, writing shubh labh on the first page. The old year's accounts are closed; the new year's are opened with a lamp and a prayer. The story behind Lakshmi's welcome, and why she is said to leave where she is taken for granted, is told in [why Lakshmi is restless](/blog/lakshmi-restless-goddess). And the guardian of wealth, as distinct from its owner, has his own story in [the Kubera story](/blog/kubera-story-meaning).

What I notice about these customs is how much they assume. Nobody paints "profit" on a door unless they intend to make some. The tradition was never embarrassed about money. It simply wanted the money to be the kind you could show God.

## Does it still apply in an open-plan office?

I do not run a shop. I sit in an office in New Jersey, with a laptop, a badge and too many meetings. For a long time I assumed the ethics of wealth in Hinduism belonged to my grandfather's cloth counter in Kumbakonam, not to a quarterly report.

Then a few ordinary situations changed my mind. A sales target that could be hit if a few numbers were rounded generously. A vendor who offered a favour that would never appear on paper. A project update where a colleague's work could quietly become mine. None of these are dramatic. Nobody would have noticed.

The frame of artha within dharma helps precisely because it is not dramatic. It does not ask me to give up the target or the salary. It asks one question: is this earned in a way I could say aloud? Valluvar's true balance is not a scale in a bazaar. It is the moment before you press send.

The same point, made without a ledger in sight, runs through [dharma in everyday life](/blog/dharma-meaning-in-everyday-life), and I have written about how my grandfather kept his counter honest in [Thatha's shop](/blog/honest-trade-thathas-shop).

## A yearly look back

When the account book is blessed at Diwali, in a shop or only in your mind, use it as a yearly look back. Not only how much came in, but how it was earned. Which deal are you glad of? Which one do you still think about in the shower? Lakshmi, the old people said, sits more easily in a house where the accounts are clean.

One small thing to carry into this week: before you close one transaction, a sale, an invoice, a promotion case, ask whether you would be comfortable with the other person reading your notes.
