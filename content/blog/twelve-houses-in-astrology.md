One old way of teaching the birth chart pictures a life as a house with twelve rooms. In one room you wake up and look in the mirror. In another the family wealth is kept, locked in a chest. In a third your brothers and sisters argue. In a fourth your mother cooks. Further along there is a room for work, a room for gains, and at the very end a quiet room where you put things down and rest. The 12 houses in Vedic astrology are those rooms, and the picture is worth keeping, because the story is really about a whole life, not a verdict on it.

## What are the 12 houses in Vedic astrology?

In Jyotish, each house is called a bhava, a word that means both house and state of being. A bhava, in astrology, is an area of life, counted from the lagna, the rising sign, which becomes the first house. The rest follow in order around the chart.

1. **Tanu**, the body and the self
2. **Dhana**, wealth, family and speech
3. **Sahaja**, siblings, courage and effort
4. **Sukha**, mother, home and inner happiness
5. **Putra**, children, intellect and creativity
6. **Ripu**, obstacles, illness, rivals and service
7. **Kalatra**, marriage and partnership
8. **Ayur**, longevity, the hidden and sudden change
9. **Dharma**, fortune, teachers and faith
10. **Karma**, work and reputation
11. **Labha**, gains and friendships
12. **Vyaya**, loss, spending and release

Planets sitting in a house are said to colour that room. The sign on each house, and where its ruling planet sits, adds more detail. Everything is counted from the first house, so if that starting point is unclear, begin with [the lagna, the rising sign](/blog/lagna-rising-sign-explained).

People often ask what the 10th house means. It is karma bhava, the room of action: the work you do, the reputation it earns, the public part of your life. It is one of the strongest houses in the chart, but the tradition treats it as a direction of effort, not a job title.

## Kendra, trikona and the difficult rooms

The houses are grouped, and the groups tell a story of their own.

The kendras, the first, fourth, seventh and tenth, are the pillars: self, home, partnership and work. A house stands on its corners, and so does a life.

The trikonas, the first, fifth and ninth, are the blessings: self, intelligence and fortune. The Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra describes favourable combinations, raja yogas, when the rulers of pillars and blessings meet.

Then there are the sixth, eighth and twelfth, the dusthanas, or houses of struggle. People are afraid of these. But look at what they hold. The sixth is obstacles, and also service: the room where you work for others. The eighth is the hidden and the sudden, and also depth: the room where research, secrets and transformation live. The struggle is real. So is what grows from it.

## Why is the twelfth not a bad room?

The twelfth house, vyaya, is the room of loss and spending. Look closer and it is also the room of sleep, of foreign lands, of giving away, and of moksha, liberation. It is where the chart lets go.

Notice the shape of this. The chart begins with the body in the first house and ends with release in the twelfth. The four ashramas, the stages of life in the Dharma Shastras, follow the same arc: student, householder, forest dweller, renunciant. The last stage is not a failure of the first three; it is their completion. The stages have a separate piece of their own, [the householder and the forest dweller](/blog/householder-and-forest-dweller), and the release at the end of them is what [moksha means](/blog/what-is-moksha).

A house whose last room is empty is not a poor house. It is a house with space to breathe.

## Every life has all twelve rooms

Everybody's chart has all twelve houses. Nobody is missing a room for love or a room for work. What differs is where the lamps burn brighter, where the furniture is heavy, which doors stick.

If you look at your own chart, try reading it as a floor plan rather than a report card. Where is the light? Where is the clutter? Which room have you not visited in years? The piece on [reading your birth chart calmly](/blog/reading-your-birth-chart-calmly) is a good companion for this.

The story says the chart is a house with twelve rooms. The story is really about living in all of them.
