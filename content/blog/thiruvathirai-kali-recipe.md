On Thiruvathirai morning my mother in Chennai makes kali before dawn, and when I call her I can hear the ladle knocking on the vessel. In Edison it is still dark and cold, which is exactly the right weather for it. Kali is a sweet, thick porridge of broken rice, moong dal, jaggery and coconut, and this thiruvathirai kali recipe is the one she reads out to me every year over a crackling line. It is offered to Nataraja, Shiva as the cosmic dancer, on Arudra Darshanam.

## Why is kali offered to Nataraja?

It goes back to a poor woodcutter in Chidambaram. Sendanar fed a guest a plain kali of rice flour and jaggery one rainy Thiruvathirai, and the next morning the priests found grains of it on the Lord himself; the guest had been Shiva. [The meaning of Arudra Darshan](/blog/arudra-darshan-meaning) tells the story properly, along with the Thiruppallandu, the short hymn of blessing Sendanar is said to have sung.

There is a Tamil saying: "Thiruvathirai-kku oru vai kali," one mouthful of kali for Thiruvathirai. It means the smallest portion is enough, if it is given the way Sendanar gave it. I think of that saying in the weeks when life is reduced to what you can manage, when the pooja is a lamp and a spoon of something sweet and nothing more. One mouthful counts.

## Thiruvathirai kali recipe

**Ingredients (serves four)**

- 100 g raw rice (½ cup)
- 2 tablespoons moong dal
- 150 g grated jaggery (¾ cup)
- 500 ml water (2 cups)
- 3 tablespoons grated coconut
- ½ teaspoon cardamom powder
- 2 tablespoons ghee
- 10 cashews

**Method**

1. Dry-roast the rice in a pan until it turns light golden, then roast the moong dal separately until fragrant.
2. Cool both and grind them together coarsely, to the texture of fine semolina. Not powder.
3. Melt the jaggery in the water, strain it, and bring it back to a boil.
4. Add the coconut, then pour in the ground rice and dal slowly, stirring so no lumps form.
5. Cook on low heat, covered, stirring often, until the grains are soft and the kali is thick. A pressure cooker saves time here.
6. Stir in cardamom. Fry the cashews in ghee and pour over.

Kali is traditionally served with ezhu kari kootu, a thick, mildly spiced kootu made with seven winter vegetables, often pumpkin, avarai beans, field beans, sweet potato, raw banana, yam and taro. The salt and the sweet go together.

## When is Arudra Darshanam?

Arudra Darshanam falls on the day of the Thiruvathirai star in the Tamil month of Margazhi, near the full moon, usually December or January. It is the night that celebrates Nataraja's cosmic dance, and Chidambaram draws enormous crowds; [the Chidambaram Nataraja temple](/blog/chidambaram-nataraja-temple) describes the place itself. Check the current year's panchangam for the exact day, since the star and the full moon do not always fall together.

In our house the kali is offered at the morning pooja, often after watching part of the Chidambaram darshan on a laptop propped on the counter. My son asked once why Shiva is dancing on someone. That question needed more than a morning, and [what the Nataraja form means](/blog/nataraja-symbolism) is where I sent him later.

Margazhi mornings in our kitchen also turn to [slow paal payasam](/blog/paal-payasam-naivedyam) on other days of the month, and on Saturdays to [ellu urundai, the sesame balls of winter](/blog/ellu-urundai-sesame-balls).

## One small thing to carry into the week

This Margazhi, make a small pot of kali before work. It takes half an hour if you ground the rice the night before. Offer one spoon at the lamp, and eat one spoon yourself before the coffee. And remember Sendanar, who had almost nothing, and gave it anyway.
