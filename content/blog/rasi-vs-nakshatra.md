The Puranas say the twenty-seven nakshatras were sisters, all married to the Moon, who was supposed to spend a night with each in turn and kept lingering with [Rohini, his favourite](/blog/rohini-nakshatra), until their father's curse set him waxing and waning forever. The story sounds like a family quarrel. It is really a calendar, and it is the best way to understand rasi vs nakshatra: two different ways of marking where the Moon is on its monthly round.

## Rasi vs nakshatra: what is the difference?

The sky, in Jyotish, is divided twice.

The first division makes twelve rasis, or signs, of thirty degrees each: Mesha, Rishabha, Mithuna and the rest, the familiar twelve of the zodiac. Your rasi is the sign the Moon occupied when you were born, which is why it is often called the Moon sign.

The second division makes twenty-seven nakshatras, or lunar mansions, of thirteen degrees and twenty minutes each. These are the Moon's nightly lodgings, the twenty-seven wives of the story. Your nakshatra, your birth star, is the one the Moon was lodged in at your birth.

So the difference between moon sign and birth star is a matter of scale. Both come from the same point in the sky. The rasi is the neighbourhood; the nakshatra is the house. If you have not yet worked out yours, [how to find your nakshatra](/blog/how-to-find-your-nakshatra) works it out from birth details.

## How do they fit together?

Here is the arithmetic, and it is tidier than it looks. Twenty-seven stars do not divide evenly into twelve signs, so each nakshatra is split into four quarters called padas. That makes one hundred and eight padas around the whole sky. Each rasi holds exactly nine of them, which is two and a quarter nakshatras.

This means some stars straddle two signs. [Krittika](/blog/krittika-nakshatra) is the classic example: its first quarter falls in Mesha, the remaining three in Rishabha. Punarvasu, Mrigashira and Uttara Phalguni are split in the same way. So which rasi your nakshatra is in depends, for those stars, on the pada. Tell an astrologer "Krittika, second pada" and they know at once you are Rishabha rasi.

The padas also carry naming syllables. Each quarter is linked to a sound, and many families choose a child's name beginning with the syllable of the birth pada. The sky, in other words, supplies the first letter.

Notice, too, that the favourite wife, Rohini, sits entirely within Rishabha, the sign where the Moon is said to be exalted. The story and the astronomy agree: the Moon is happiest there.

## When each is used

Because they work at different scales, tradition uses them for different jobs.

The rasi is used for transits and forecasts. When Saturn or Jupiter changes sign, the weekly and yearly palangal, predictions, are arranged by rasi. A newspaper column for [Makara rasi](/blog/makara-rasi) speaks to everyone whose Moon was in Capricorn, a broad crowd.

The nakshatra is used for anything personal and precise: choosing a name, fixing a muhurtham, the auspicious moment for a ceremony, marking the star birthday each month, and [matching horoscopes for marriage](/blog/horoscope-matching-porutham), where the ten traditional checks start from the two birth stars. At the temple, the priest asks for your star, not your sign.

And neither is the same as your lagna, the rising sign, which depends on the exact birth time and marks the start of the whole chart. That is a third map, explained in [lagna in astrology](/blog/lagna-rising-sign-explained).

## Two maps of one night sky

Go back to the story. The Moon cannot help having favourites, and the sisters cannot help noticing. The curse did not stop the Moon travelling; it only made the travel visible, as waxing and waning. The rasis and nakshatras are the same kind of visibility: a way of saying where the Moon has been and where it is going.

Know both. The rasi tells you which broad season of forecasts you belong to. The nakshatra tells you something more intimate, the lodging the Moon chose on the night you arrived. Hold both lightly. They are two maps of the same sky, and a map is only ever useful to someone who is still walking.
