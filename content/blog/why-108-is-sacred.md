A mala has 108 beads. A stotram of praise often has 108 names. A pilgrimage may cover 108 temples. The number turns up so often in Hindu practice that it starts to feel like a law of nature. Nobody fully agrees why 108 is sacred, though, and that is part of what makes it interesting. Some reasons are astrological, some devotional, some mathematical, and one is simply about the body. Look at them honestly and the number becomes more interesting, not less.

## Why 108 is sacred: the traditional reasons

Here are the explanations you will hear most often.

The first comes from astrology. There are 27 nakshatras, the lunar mansions or birth stars, and each is divided into four padas, or quarters. Twenty-seven times four is 108. So 108 is, in one sense, the complete map of the Moon's path through the sky.

A second comes from the same source. There are 12 rasis, the zodiac signs, and nine grahas, the planetary influences. Twelve times nine is also 108.

A third is devotional. There are 108 Divya Desams, the temples of Vishnu praised in the hymns of the Alvars, the Tamil poet-saints. For many Vaishnavas, visiting as many of them as possible is a lifelong pilgrimage.

A fourth is literary. Ashtottaram, the 108-name hymns, exist for almost every deity: Ganesha, Shiva, Lakshmi, Murugan, Hanuman. Reciting the 108 names is a complete act of praise. For contrast, [the Lingashtakam](/blog/lingashtakam-meaning) praises Shiva in just eight verses.

A fifth is a traditional list of 108 Upanishads, given in the Muktika Upanishad, though scholars count the Upanishads in many different ways.

The significance of 108 in Hinduism, then, is not one reason. It is a web of them, each reinforcing the others. That is often how sacred numbers work.

## What about the astronomy claims?

You will almost certainly come across this one. The distance from the Earth to the Sun is said to be about 108 times the Sun's diameter. And the distance from the Earth to the Moon is said to be about 108 times the Moon's diameter.

Both are approximately true. The figures come out close to 108, though not exactly, and they shift slightly as the orbits change. It is a lovely coincidence. It is also why the Sun and Moon appear almost the same size in our sky.

Be careful here, though. A coincidence is not proof that the ancients built their practices around it, and there is no clear evidence they did. You can enjoy the resonance without needing it to carry more weight than it can hold. Honesty is a form of respect, for the tradition and for yourself.

## Why does 108 work for the body?

Here is the reason that matters most in practice, and it does not depend on any belief.

A round of 108 takes time. For a short mantra, it is somewhere around ten to fifteen minutes. That is long enough for several things to happen. The breath slows down. The mind wanders off, notices, and comes back, not once but several times. The body settles into a rhythm.

A round of 10 would be over before any of that began. A round of 1,000 would exhaust most beginners. 108 sits in a sweet spot, long enough to change your state and short enough to fit into a morning. Whether or not the ancients planned it that way, the body seems to agree with them.

This is also why chanting 108 times is the unit people keep coming back to. If you are starting out, [chanting for beginners](/blog/chanting-for-beginners) has simple mantras that fit a round well. [Likhita japa, writing a mantra by hand](/blog/likhita-japa-writing-mantras), uses the same count on paper. And [trataka, gazing at a lamp](/blog/trataka-lamp-gazing), is a way to use the same attention with the eyes instead of the voice.

## Watch where the mind leaves the mala

One round of 108 each morning. Pick one mantra and one mala, and keep them the same all week.

As you go, notice around which bead the mind first goes quiet. It will be different each day. On some days it will be bead 20. On others, bead 90. On some, it will not happen at all. Keep a rough note. By the end of the week, you will have your own answer to why 108 works, and it will be more convincing than any list.
