When I was a boy in Chennai, my birthday began at the temple and ended with my mother sending a tiffin carrier of sweet pongal to the neighbours. The cake came much later, and it was never the main thing. Giving on your birthday, a habit Hindu families have long kept, rests on a simple idea: a birthday is a day of thanks for another year of life, so you give, rather than only receive. Food, clothes, an offering at the temple, a gift to someone in need, made on your star day or your calendar birthday.

## Why is birthday donation a Hindu custom?

In the older way of thinking, a birthday is not an achievement. You did not do anything to earn another year. It was given. The natural response to a gift is gratitude, and gratitude, in the tradition, is shown by passing something on.

Many South Indian families mark the birthday twice: once on the calendar date and once on the janma nakshatra, the birth star, as it comes round in the lunar month of your birth. The star birthday is the one the temple cares about. An archana is done in your name and star; sometimes a homam for long life, the ayushya homam, is offered. [The ayushya homam and the star birthday](/blog/ayushya-homam-star-birthday) explains the custom properly.

Alongside the temple visit comes the giving. Feeding people, annadanam, is the classic form. Annadanam, feeding people, on a birthday might mean sponsoring a meal at the temple, feeding the priests, or, in my mother's case, a tiffin carrier to every house on the street. New clothes might be given to someone who works in the house. Money might go to a school or an orphanage. The instinct behind all of it is the one I tried to describe in [dana in Hinduism](/blog/dana-in-hinduism): giving as practice, not favour.

## Ways to give on a birthday in America

Living in New Jersey, I have had to translate this. The tiffin carrier to the neighbours does not work quite the same way in a townhouse complex. But the idea travels well.

- Sponsor a meal at your local temple. Many US temples have a list of dates for annadanam sponsorship, and you can pick your star day.
- Do a food bank shift as a family. Two hours on a Saturday morning, sorting cans, and the children see where food goes.
- Make a donation in a parent's name back in India, to the temple they go to, or to a school in their town. Parents love being included in a birthday that is not theirs.
- Sponsor an annadanam for children, through a temple or a trust in India.
- Give away something you own and no longer use: a winter coat, books, toys in good condition.

Many temples in the US also depend on volunteers, and [why US temples have many deities](/blog/why-us-temples-have-many-deities) explains how these community temples grew; they are a natural place to give time as well as money.

## How do you teach a child to give on their birthday?

My son is about eleven, and finding ways for him to give on his birthday has been a small project in our house.

What has worked is letting him choose. We give him three or four options, a food bank, an animal shelter, a school library, a temple meal, and he picks one. The choosing matters more than the size. Keep it small and regular, the same every year, so it becomes part of the birthday rather than a lecture attached to it. One year he chose to put a handful of rice out for the birds on his birthday morning, after watching my mother's morning habit of [feeding crows and birds](/blog/feeding-crows-and-birds) on a video call. A broader piece on [raising Hindu kids in America](/blog/raising-hindu-kids-in-america) has more on passing these habits on.

And it helps to say what giving is not. It is not about being seen, and it is not a bargain for a good year. It is simply thanks. The tradition's word for this is seva, and [what seva means](/blog/seva-meaning) puts it well: service in which you are not the point.

## One small thing to carry

On your next birthday, before the cake, give one thing away. It can be small: a meal, a book, an hour. Ask the family to do it with you, and let the youngest choose. Then cut the cake. It will taste the same, and the day will feel a little larger.
