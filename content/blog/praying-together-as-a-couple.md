Try this for a week: five minutes, two people, one lamp. No phones. You will notice who arrives late, who wants to talk, and who goes quiet. All of that is the practice. When it comes to praying together as a couple, the tradition's favourite picture is not two people kneeling in silence but two people sharing one flame, one flower, one breath of attention. You do not need a priest, a script or matching beliefs. You need five minutes and a willingness to sit side by side.

## Praying as a couple: an old idea

The tradition has a striking picture for this. Ardhanarishvara, Shiva and Parvati sharing one body, half male and half female, one ear with a man's earring and the other with a woman's. It is not a picture of two people agreeing. It is a picture of two very different energies inhabiting the same form. [What Ardhanarishvara means](/blog/ardhanarishvara-meaning) tells the story behind the image.

There is a practical side, too. The wedding itself is a shared puja: the couple sit before the same fire, pour ghee into it together, and walk around it together. In the old view of the grihastha, the householder stage of life, the home is a place of practice in its own right, not a waiting room before the spiritual life begins. Many traditional rites could only be performed by a couple, side by side. A prayer to Shiva and Parvati as a couple is simply a continuation of that first fire.

## How do two people pray together without it feeling awkward?

Awkwardness is real, especially in the first week. One of you will feel self-conscious; the other will want to get it right. A simple structure takes the pressure off.

1. One person lights the lamp. The other offers a flower.
2. Say a short sankalpam together, the spoken statement of intention, with both your names. Something like: "We sit here together today, for peace in our home."
3. Chant one short line together. "Om Namah Shivaya" works; so does "Om Shanti". Three times is enough.
4. Sit in silence for one minute. Just breathe.
5. End with a small aarti, circling the flame once before the deity. There is a step-by-step guide on [how to do aarti at home](/blog/how-to-do-aarti-at-home) if you want to learn the circles.

Take turns. No one leads every day. On Monday one of you lights the lamp; on Tuesday the other. This matters more than it sounds. Daily prayer as husband and wife should not quietly become one person's duty that the other attends.

How to pray together as a couple is less about the words and more about the order. When the steps are clear, nobody has to perform.

## When you believe differently, or one of you is not ready

Many couples do not share the same faith, or the same degree of it. One grew up with a morning lamp; the other did not. One believes in the gods; the other believes in the lamp as a nice idea. That is fine.

The non-praying partner can sit beside the lamp without chanting. Just sitting counts. You can take turns choosing the prayer, a Tamil verse one day, a moment of silence the next, a line of poetry from the other's tradition. The ritual should be an invitation, never an obligation. If it becomes a test of the other person's commitment, stop and try again later. There is a thoughtful piece on [doing puja with a non-Hindu spouse](/blog/puja-with-a-non-hindu-spouse) for interfaith homes.

Newly married couples sometimes find this easiest at the beginning, when the customs of the first year give them a reason; the practices around [Thalai Deepavali, a couple's first Deepavali](/blog/thalai-deepavali-newlyweds) are one such doorway. Couples married many years often return to it on their anniversary, often with a quiet [wedding anniversary pooja](/blog/wedding-anniversary-pooja).

## Try it for seven evenings

Do the five minutes for seven evenings in a row. Same place, roughly the same hour. On the eighth evening, skip the lamp and ask each other one question: did anything change in how we spoke to each other afterward?

Listen to the answer. It may be nothing. It may be that dinner felt calmer, or that one argument ended sooner. Either way, you will have learned something about each other, which was the point.
