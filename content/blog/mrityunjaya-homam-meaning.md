The week before my father's heart surgery, my mother arranged a Mrityunjaya homam at a temple in Madurai, and I flew home in time to sit beside her. She was very clear, before the priest began, that it was not instead of the surgeon. It was for us, she said, so that we would have something to hold. I have thought about that sentence many times since; what the fire itself taught me that morning is in [homam and letting go](/blog/homam-and-letting-go). When people ask me about Mrityunjaya homam benefits, my mother's sentence is the most honest answer I know.

## What happens in a Mrityunjaya homam?

Mrityunjaya means "the one who conquers death", a name for Shiva. The homam, a fire ritual, is built around the Tryambaka mantra from the Rig Veda, better known as the Mahamrityunjaya mantra. [The Mahamrityunjaya mantra's meaning](/blog/mahamrityunjaya-mantra-meaning) goes through it word by word.

The Mrityunjaya homam procedure goes roughly like this.

1. **Sankalpam.** The priest states the intention with the person's name, birth star and gotra, the family lineage. If the person is abroad or in hospital, the family gives these details.
2. **Ganapati first,** as always.
3. **The kalasam.** A pot of water is set up to hold Shiva's presence as Mrityunjaya.
4. **The chanting.** The Tryambaka mantra is chanted a set number of times, often a hundred and eight or a thousand and eight, while ghee, sesame and durva grass go into the fire with each round.
5. **Purnahuti,** the final full offering.
6. **The water.** The kalasam water, now carrying the prayers, is sprinkled on the person, or sent home in a small bottle if they cannot attend.

In our case, my brother carried a small bottle of that water to the hospital. My father, who chants on our video calls, said the mantra quietly with it the night before he went in.

## What are the Mrityunjaya homam benefits, honestly?

The tradition speaks of health and long life. That is the language of the mantra itself, which asks to be freed from death as a ripe cucumber is freed from the vine, gently, in its own time.

What families describe is something slightly different. Steadiness, first. When a person you love is facing surgery, the days before are full of fear with nowhere to go. A homam gives that fear a place and a time. Second, a shared act of care: aunts, cousins and siblings who cannot help in the operating theatre can sit together at the fire, or join a call, and do something. Third, a kind of permission to hope out loud.

What it is not is a medical treatment. It sits alongside the surgeon, the cardiologist and the nurses, never in place of them. My mother, who is as devout as anyone I know, followed every instruction the doctors gave. The homam was for the part of us that the hospital could not reach.

If the person you are praying for is about to go into an operation, [a short prayer before surgery](/blog/prayer-before-surgery-hindu) is brief enough for a waiting room.

## Who arranges it, and when?

Families traditionally arrange a Mrityunjaya homam in a few kinds of moments.

- **Before surgery,** as ours did.
- **During a long illness,** sometimes repeated on the person's birth star each month.
- **On milestone birthdays.** The sixtieth, the Shashtiabdapoorthi, and the eightieth, the Sathabhishekam, often include it. [The sixtieth birthday ceremony](/blog/shashtiabdapoorthi-60th-birthday) describes the whole day.
- **For elders far away.** Children abroad often arrange it in a parent's name, which is the case for many of the families I know here. The practical side is in [praying for a sick parent from abroad](/blog/praying-for-a-sick-parent).

The question of whether to do a homam or a simpler puja comes up often. [Homam versus puja](/blog/homam-vs-puja) explains the difference plainly.

## One small thing to carry this week

If a parent is unwell far away, you do not need to arrange anything grand this week. Find out what time their evening lamp is lit. At that hour, call. Let them hear your voice say Shiva's name, once, or the first line of the mantra if you know it. Then talk about something ordinary, what you had for dinner, what your son said at school.

That is a very small homam. The fire is on their side of the call, and the offering is your voice.
