A lion does not hunt all day. It spends most of its hours resting, watching, conserving itself, and moves with full force only when it matters. Simha rasi, the lion's sign, is often described as the sign of display and leadership, but its strength is not constant performance. If your Moon sits here, that is the first thing to practise: not being seen all the time.

## What does Simha rasi mean in Jyotish?

Simha rasi is the fifth sign. Remember that rasi refers to the Moon at your birth, so a Simha Moon says something about the mind and its moods. It holds all of Magha, all of Purva Phalguni and the first quarter of Uttara Phalguni. Its ruling planet is Surya, the Sun, and it is the only sign the Sun owns. Western astrology calls it Leo, though it measures by the seasons rather than the stars.

The lion stands for royalty, courage and generosity. In temple sculpture, the lion carries the Goddess into battle, and the yali, a lion-like guardian, holds up the pillars of countless mandapams. It is an animal of power, but also of protection.

## Can pride become service?

Tradition's portrait of Simha is confident, warm, generous and proud, with a natural authority that others tend to follow. The shadow is the need to be seen: generosity that waits for applause, leadership that needs an audience. You may recognise some of that, or none of it. Take the portrait as tradition, not a verdict.

Here is the useful part. Look at the ruler. The Sun gives light to everything, every day, and asks for nothing. It does not dim when nobody thanks it. It does not shine brighter for a crowd. That is the tradition's model for Simha: power that serves, warmth that does not keep accounts.

Pride and service are not opposites. Pride turned outward, towards the work and the people, becomes dignity. Pride turned inward, towards the self, becomes vanity. The practice is noticing which way it is facing.

The body has a very direct version of this. Surya namaskar, the sun salutation, is a sequence of twelve postures traditionally done facing the rising sun. Most people rush it as exercise. Try doing it slowly, one breath per movement, and it becomes something else: a bow. Twelve times you lower your head to the ground before the thing that gives you light. Many people find that is the best medicine for a proud mind. Each of the twelve has a name and a mantra, explained in [the meaning of Surya namaskar](/blog/surya-namaskar-meaning).

## Temples and poojas traditionally linked through Surya

Because the Sun rules Simha, the temple most associated with the sign is Suryanar Koil, the Sun temple among the nine Navagraha shrines near Kumbakonam, and the only one of the nine where the Sun is the main deity. [Surya and Sunday worship](/blog/surya-graha-worship) gathers the traditions around it.

The simplest Sunday practice is arghya: water offered to the rising sun from cupped hands or a small vessel, poured slowly while you look towards the light. It takes a minute. For those who want a prayer for all nine planets together, the Navagraha homam, a fire ritual addressed to the nine grahas, is the traditional choice.

And the lion has a devotional companion in Narasimha, the man-lion avatar of Vishnu, who burst from a pillar to protect the child Prahlada. The fierce form exists to protect the vulnerable, which is the best possible use of a lion. [What the Narasimha avatar means](/blog/narasimha-avatar-meaning) tells the whole story.

## Generosity nobody sees

Do one generous thing each day that no one will know about. Not a large thing. Pay for something anonymously, clean up after someone, praise a colleague to their manager without telling them. Watch what happens inside when there is no credit.

On Sunday, wake a little early and offer water to the rising sun. Notice whether you miss the credit you did not get during the week. If the lion in you settles, you have found its real strength. The sign before, [Kataka](/blog/kataka-rasi), belongs to the Moon; the sign after, [Kanya](/blog/kanya-rasi), to Mercury. Between them, the Sun simply gives.
