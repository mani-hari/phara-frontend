Every month the moon thins to almost nothing. On the night before it disappears altogether, the fourteenth night of the waning fortnight, devotees keep Masik Shivaratri, the monthly night for Shiva. It asks very little: a light fast, a lamp, some water and bilva leaves, the trifoliate leaves sacred to Shiva, for a lingam, his aniconic stone form, a name repeated in the dark. The story behind it says the moon was dying. The story is really about coming back.

## What is Masik Shivaratri?

The Hindu lunar month has two halves, the bright fortnight when the moon grows and the dark fortnight when it shrinks. The fourteenth day of the dark half is called chaturdashi. Every month, that night belongs to Shiva, and it is called Masik Shivaratri, masik simply meaning monthly.

Once a year, in the month of Magha or Phalguna depending on which calendar a region follows, that same night becomes Maha Shivaratri, the great night of Shiva, kept with all-night vigils in temples across India. Its stories belong to [the meaning of Maha Shivaratri](/blog/maha-shivaratri-meaning). The other eleven are the monthly shivaratri nights, smaller and quieter, and for many devotees more intimate for that reason. They sit at the dark end of the month, a fortnight from [the full-moon Pournami pooja](/blog/pournami-pooja-at-home) at the bright end.

It sits comfortably beside other Shiva observances. [Pradosham](/blog/pradosham-meaning), the twilight hour on the thirteenth day of each fortnight, falls just a day earlier. Those who keep a [Monday fast for Shiva](/blog/somvar-vrat-monday-fast) often add the monthly Shivaratri as the natural next step.

## Why does Shiva wear the moon?

The Chandra and Shiva story is told in several Puranas, including the Shiva Purana and the Skanda Purana, with small differences.

It begins with a family quarrel. Chandra, the Moon, was married to Daksha's twenty-seven daughters, the nakshatras, and neglected all of them for Rohini, so their father cursed him to waste away. Night by night the moon grew thinner, and as it faded, plants lost their juices, because the moon, in the old imagination, is the keeper of sap and moisture.

Desperate, Chandra went to Shiva. The tradition places this at Prabhasa on the coast of Gujarat, where the Somnath temple now stands; Soma is another name for the moon, and Somnath means the moon's lord. Shiva could not cancel Daksha's curse. What he did was stranger. He placed the dying crescent on his own head. The curse still works: Chandra wastes away for fifteen days. But then, held by Shiva, he grows back for fifteen more. That is why Shiva is called Chandrashekhara, the moon-crowned. It is not the only time he takes something failing or dangerous onto his own body; [the story of Shiva's blue throat](/blog/neelakantha-blue-throat-story) has the same shape.

The story says a god saved the moon. The story is really about what saving looks like. Shiva did not stop the waning. He made waning part of a cycle, and gave it somewhere to rest.

## How is Masik Shivaratri kept at home?

The masik Shivaratri puja vidhi at home is short.

1. **During the day.** Keep a light fast, or a simple vegetarian meal. Those who are unwell or on medication should eat normally.
2. **After dark.** Bathe, clean the shrine and light a lamp.
3. **The offering.** Pour a little water or milk over a small lingam, or place a cup of water before a picture of Shiva. Offer bilva leaves if you can find them, or any white flowers.
4. **The name.** Chant Om Namah Shivaya, as many times as feels right. Each syllable carries a meaning, and [the meaning of Om Namah Shivaya](/blog/om-namah-shivaya-meaning) takes them one at a time.
5. **Stay up a little.** Not all night, just later than usual, with the lamp lit and the house quiet.

That is enough. Nobody is counting the leaves.

## What is thinning in your month?

The monthly night falls when the moon is at its thinnest. Most of us have something thinning too, patience, savings, energy, a friendship we have neglected. The instinct is to fight the waning or to hide it.

The story suggests a third way. Put the thin thing somewhere it can be held. Keep one monthly night this way: a lamp, the name, a little water poured out. Let it hold whatever is running low. Then watch the next fortnight. The moon, after all, has come back every single month since Daksha lost his temper.
