The most sacred thing at the Chidambaram temple is something you cannot see. In the golden hall where Shiva dances as Nataraja, there is a small space beside him, hidden by a curtain. When the priest draws the curtain aside, there is nothing there but a string of golden bilva leaves hanging in the dark. Devotees fold their hands to it all the same. They are bowing to empty space, and at Chidambaram temple that is exactly the point.

## Who watched Shiva dance at Chidambaram temple?

The story told in the Chidambara Mahatmyam, the temple's sacred history, has two unusual sages at its centre. One is Patanjali, half man and half serpent, often said to be a form of Adisesha, the great snake who carries Vishnu. The other is Vyaghrapada, whose name means tiger-foot, because he asked for a tiger's claws so he could climb trees before dawn and pick flowers for Shiva before the bees had touched them.

Both longed to see Shiva dance. They waited and worshipped in the forest of Thillai, a stretch of mangrove by the sea, for a very long time. At last Shiva came and performed the ananda tandava, the dance of bliss, for the two of them. The temple stands where he danced, and the town took the name Chidambaram, often explained as the sky of consciousness.

The story says two sages got a private performance. The story is really about who gets to see. Not kings or gods, but a serpent-man and a tiger-footed man, two beings who did not fit in anywhere. They waited in a swamp, and the dance came to them.

## What is the Chidambara Rahasyam?

Rahasyam means secret, and the Chidambara Rahasyam is the secret of Chidambaram: that curtain beside Nataraja, and the empty space behind it. Chidambaram is counted among the Pancha Bhuta sthalas, the five element shrines of Shiva, and its element is akasha, ether or space. There is no stone lingam for it. There cannot be. The lingam of space is space.

So the temple offers two images side by side. On one side, the most dynamic form in Hindu art: a god whirling, hair flying, fire in one hand. On the other, nothing at all. The story is really about how the dance and the stillness are the same thing seen from two sides. Everything that moves, moves in space. Space does not move, and holds all of it. Shiva is both, and the priest does not ask you to choose.

The wind shrine of the same five has its own riddle, described in [the Sri Kalahasti temple guide](/blog/sri-kalahasti-temple-guide). The Chola kings who held Nataraja as their family deity also built [the Big Temple at Thanjavur](/blog/thanjavur-big-temple), and his dancers are carved all over its walls.

## Reading the Nataraja

Stand in front of Nataraja and read him like a sentence. The small drum in his upper right hand beats the rhythm of creation. The flame in his upper left is dissolution, the end of things. His lower right hand is raised, palm open, saying do not be afraid. His lower left points down to his raised left foot, which is release: this is the way out. His right foot presses on a dwarf, Apasmara, who stands for forgetfulness, the ignorance that makes us lose track of who we are. Around him is a ring of flame, the world itself, burning and renewing.

A full reading of each gesture is in [the symbolism of Nataraja](/blog/nataraja-symbolism). What matters here is that the whole cycle, making, keeping, ending, freeing, happens in one frozen instant of a dance. Nothing is out of sequence. Nothing is left out. Not even the forgetting.

## An empty room

The great festival at Chidambaram is Arudra Darshan in the month of Margazhi, when Nataraja comes out in procession; [the meaning of Arudra Darshan](/blog/arudra-darshan-meaning) tells that story. Many devotees also turn here on [Maha Shivaratri](/blog/maha-shivaratri-meaning), and an abhishekam, a ritual bathing, to Nataraja with a sankalpam in your name is one way in from afar.

And for the seeker who is not sure about any of it, try this. On a quiet evening, sit for a few minutes in an empty room. Not a meditation, just sitting. Notice the space in the room. It holds the chair, the lamp, your breathing, the sound from the street. It does not push anything out. It does not keep anything. The dance goes on inside it. That is the whole secret of Chidambaram, and you did not need a curtain to find it.
