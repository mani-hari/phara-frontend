In a Chandi homam, the story is the offering. The priests recite all seven hundred verses of the Devi Mahatmyam, the great hymn to the Goddess, and with the verses, offerings go into the fire, chapter after chapter, sometimes for most of a day. Nothing else in the Hindu ritual world quite matches it for sheer length of narrative. To understand what takes place, you have to understand the story being told.

## What happens in a Chandi homam?

Chandi is a name of the Goddess in her fierce, protective form. The Devi Mahatmyam, also called the Durga Saptashati or "seven hundred verses", is a section of the Markandeya Purana, and it is her text. If you want to read it yourself, [the Durga Saptashati for beginners](/blog/durga-saptashati-for-beginners) is a good way in.

The Chandi homam procedure, in outline:

1. **Ganapati puja and sankalpam.** Ganesha is honoured, and the intention is stated in the family's names.
2. **The kalasam and the Navarna mantra.** The Goddess is invoked into a pot of water, and her nine-syllable mantra is chanted.
3. **The recitation.** All thirteen chapters of the Devi Mahatmyam are recited. At set verses, offerings of ghee, sesame, flowers and other items go into the fire. This is the long heart of the rite.
4. **Suvasini and kanya puja.** Married women and young girls are honoured as living forms of the Goddess, fed and given gifts. Why a small girl should stand for the Goddess is its own story: [what kanya puja means](/blog/kanya-puja-meaning).
5. **Purnahuti.** The final full offering, traditionally including a red cloth and sweets.

## Three stories, three fires

The Devi Mahatmyam tells three stories, and each can be read as one fire.

**The first,** in the opening chapter, is Madhu and Kaitabha. Two demons spring from Vishnu's ear while he sleeps on the cosmic ocean and threaten Brahma. Brahma prays to the Goddess as Yoganidra, the sleep that holds Vishnu, and she releases him. Vishnu wakes and destroys them.

**The second,** in chapters two to four, is Mahishasura, the buffalo demon, who has driven the gods out of heaven. The gods' anger pours out of them as light, and the light gathers into the Goddess. Each god gives her a weapon. She rides out on her lion and kills him.

**The third,** from the fifth chapter to the thirteenth, is Shumbha and Nishumbha, two brothers who want to possess her. Their generals fall one by one, among them Raktabija, from every drop of whose blood a new demon springs. Kali drinks the blood before it can touch the ground.

The story says the Goddess slays demons. The story is really about three kinds of trouble that live in all of us. Madhu and Kaitabha are inertia, the danger that comes when awareness is asleep. Mahishasura is pride, the brute force that thinks it owns the world. Raktabija is endless craving, the desire that multiplies every time you feed it. They are faced, the text suggests, one chapter at a time.

## Why is the Chandi homam performed in Navratri?

In the twelfth chapter, the Goddess makes a promise. Those who recite or listen to her story with devotion, she says, will be protected from danger, and she will come whenever she is remembered. That promise is the reason the Devi Mahatmyam is chanted at all, and it is the reason for the homam.

Navratri, the nine nights of the Goddess, is the natural setting, since its whole structure retells her battles. Ask [why Navratri lasts nine nights](/blog/why-navratri-lasts-nine-nights) and you are already halfway into the Devi Mahatmyam. Ashtami and Navami, the eighth and ninth days, are the traditional days for the Chandi homam, though many temples also perform it on Fridays or full moons through the year.

For those who choose a homam by need, the Chandi homam sits with the rites of strength and protection. [How to choose a homam](/blog/how-to-choose-a-homam) sets it beside the others, including [the Navagraha homam](/blog/navagraha-homam-meaning) for planetary seasons, and those who have been through a long illness sometimes find its courage fits the season; for them, [a practice that bends with chronic illness](/blog/chronic-illness-and-practice) may matter more than any single rite.

## One chapter on a Friday

You do not need the whole homam, though. The Goddess's promise in the twelfth chapter is to those who remember her story, not only to those who can afford seven hundred verses and a priest.

So on a Friday evening, light a lamp. Read one chapter of the Devi Mahatmyam, in Sanskrit if you can, in translation if you cannot. The first chapter is short. Let the story do its own work. Next Friday, read the next one. In thirteen weeks you will have performed, slowly, the whole of it.
