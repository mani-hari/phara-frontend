A boy of sixteen runs into a Shiva temple and throws his arms around the lingam, the rounded stone form of Shiva. Behind him comes Yama, lord of death, whose noose falls around the boy and the stone together. Shiva bursts out of the lingam and pushes Yama back. The boy is Markandeya, and the Puranas tell his story to explain a name: Mrityunjaya, the one who conquers death. The Mahamrityunjaya mantra meaning grows out of that scene. It is a Vedic prayer to Shiva asking to be released from death the way a ripe fruit is released from its vine, gently and when it is ready.

## Mahamrityunjaya mantra meaning, word by word

The verse is old. It appears in the Rig Veda, in the seventh book, and again in the Yajur Veda, which is why priests treat it as shruti, revealed sound, rather than a later hymn. Here is the Mahamrityunjaya mantra meaning in English, one piece at a time.

- **Om tryambakam**: the three-eyed one. Shiva's third eye sees what the other two miss.
- **Yajamahe**: we worship, we offer.
- **Sugandhim**: the fragrant one. Fragrance is presence you cannot hold in your hand.
- **Pushtivardhanam**: the one who increases nourishment, who makes things grow.
- **Urvarukam iva bandhanan**: like a cucumber from its bond, the stalk that ties it to the vine.
- **Mrityor mukshiya**: may I be freed from death.
- **Maamritat**: but not from immortality, not from the deathless.

Put together, it reads: we worship the three-eyed, fragrant one who nourishes all life; as the ripe cucumber slips from its vine, may he free us from death and not from the deathless. If you are new to this kind of chanting, the piece on [what a mantra actually is](/blog/what-is-a-mantra) explains why the sound matters as much as the translation.

## What does "freed from death like a ripe cucumber" really mean?

This is the strangest line in the verse, and the most important. Why a cucumber? Because a ripe cucumber does not need to be cut. When it is ready, the stalk simply lets go. An unripe one clings, and pulling it tears the vine.

The story of Markandeya says Shiva defeated death. The story is really about something quieter. Markandeya did not become immortal in the ordinary sense; the Puranas say he was granted to remain sixteen forever, which is another way of saying he stopped living in fear of the noose. The mantra does not ask for a body that never ends. It asks to ripen, so that when the letting go comes, it comes without struggle. Maamritat, "not from the deathless", is the part people skip. It says: loosen my grip on what dies, and keep me close to what does not.

## Mahamrityunjaya mantra benefits, as tradition describes them

Families reach for this verse in hospital corridors. It is chanted for someone who is ill, before surgery, for an elder whose health is fading, and on birthdays as a prayer for long life. Tradition calls its benefits health, recovery and courage. What people describe is steadiness: a fixed sound to hold when everything else is uncertain. If you are chanting for someone far away, [praying for a sick parent from abroad](/blog/praying-for-a-sick-parent) walks through what that can look like day to day.

How many times to chant the Mahamrityunjaya mantra? One hundred and eight, on a rudraksha mala, a string of prayer beads made from the seeds of the rudraksha tree, is the usual count. Three, eleven or twenty-one are also traditional. For a more formal offering, families arrange a Mrityunjaya homam, a fire ritual in which priests chant the verse many times and offer ghee into the flames with each repetition. Our guide to [what happens in a homam](/blog/what-is-a-homam) covers the steps.

None of this replaces a doctor. The tradition that gave us this mantra also gave us Dhanvantari, the god who arrives carrying medicine, and [the Dhanvantari homam and healing](/blog/dhanvantari-and-healing) sits comfortably beside a hospital's care. Prayer and treatment walk together.

## A prayer for how to live

Read the verse again and notice what it asks for first. Not escape. Nourishment: pushtivardhanam, the one who makes things grow. Only then does it ask for release, and even then it asks to be ripe for it.

That makes the Mahamrityunjaya a prayer for the healthy as much as the sick. It asks to be fed well by life, to grow fully, and to let go without tearing. You can chant it slowly once, tonight, with no count at all. Say the cucumber line twice. It is the one that stays with people.
