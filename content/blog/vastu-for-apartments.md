Vastu for apartments is mostly about what you can actually change: a clean, well-lit entrance, a quiet corner for prayer, a sensible place for the bed and the stove, and far less clutter than you think you need. You cannot move the front door of a rental, and you do not need to. Try this for a week instead. Notice where the light falls in each room, where things pile up, and where you choose to sit when you are tired. That noticing is where vastu begins.

## Vastu for apartments: what the tradition is actually about

Vastu shastra, the traditional science of space, comes from texts such as the Mayamata and the chapters on building in the Brihat Samhita. It was written for temples and houses on open land, laid out on a grid called the vastu purusha mandala, with a sacred, open centre called the brahmasthana. The rules about directions follow the sun and the wind. The north-east, which receives the first light, was kept for worship and water. The south-east, associated with Agni, the fire, was kept for cooking. The heavy, still south-west was for sleep and storage.

Underneath the rules is something very practical. Light, air, movement and order change how a body feels in a room. You already know this. You walk into a dim, crowded room and your shoulders rise. You walk into a bright, simple room and your breath drops. The old builders simply wrote this down and gave it directions.

## Which vastu tips matter in a rented flat?

Start with the entrance. This is where energy, and you, come in every evening. Keep it clean, lit and free of piles of shoes and parcels. A small lamp or a light left on at dusk makes a surprising difference to how arriving home feels; the practice of [lighting a lamp at dusk](/blog/the-dusk-lamp-ritual) grew out of exactly this threshold.

Next, the puja corner. Tradition prefers the north-east of the home, so that you face east or north while you pray. In an apartment, that might be a shelf in the living room or the top of a low cabinet. If the north-east is the bathroom or the washing machine, choose any clean, quiet spot at a height where the image sits at or above your eye line. There are practical ideas for [a small puja corner in an apartment](/blog/small-puja-corner-apartment), and once it is set up, a [five-minute daily puja](/blog/five-minute-daily-puja) is enough to keep it alive.

For sleep, vastu tips for a rented apartment usually say: head towards the south or east, never with the feet pointing at the door, and nothing heavy hanging over the bed. For the kitchen, the stove in the south-east is ideal, but most rentals come with the stove where the plumber put it. Keep it clean, and cook facing east if you can turn your body. That is enough.

Finally, clear the centre. The brahmasthana was meant to stay open. In a flat, that means the middle of the main room should not be a storage zone. Move the laundry rack and see what happens to the room.

## What can you safely ignore?

Almost everything that makes you anxious. A south-facing door, a toilet in the wrong corner, a bedroom that is too small: these are structural facts, and a tenant cannot fix them. The traditional response was never to panic. It was to keep that area especially bright, clean and uncluttered, and perhaps to place a Ganesha image near the entrance as a sign of welcome.

Be wary of any advice built on fear, particularly advice that ends in buying expensive objects or knocking down walls you do not own. The spirit of vastu is comfort, light and order. A home that is kept with care, where people sleep well and eat together, is already doing what vastu set out to do. If you want a deeper sense of how a small space can hold a full practice, the [sixteen steps of puja](/blog/sixteen-steps-of-puja) show how a tiny altar can carry a complete ritual.

## Try this: one corner, one week

Choose one corner of your home. Clear everything that does not belong there. Wipe it down. Give it light, a small lamp in the evening, perhaps a flower. Then leave it alone and watch how you feel each time you walk past it for seven days. Many people find that one cared-for corner changes the whole room. Once you feel that, you will not need a rulebook to know where the next corner is.
