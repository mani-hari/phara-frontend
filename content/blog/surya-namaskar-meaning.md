Surya namaskar means salutation to the Sun. Most people meet it as a workout, twelve positions done fast for the heart rate. Its meaning changes when you stop counting rounds and start saying a name with each one. The sequence becomes a greeting, and the body becomes the way you say it. Try six slow rounds facing east tomorrow morning, one name with each position, and see whether it feels like exercise or like something else.

## Surya namaskar meaning: a greeting, not a workout

Namaskar means a bow, a salutation, the same gesture you make with folded hands when you greet an elder. Surya is the Sun. So surya namaskar is a bow to the Sun, done with the whole body instead of just the hands.

The sequence has twelve positions that flow into one another with the breath: standing with palms joined, arching back, folding forward, stepping back, lowering, lifting the chest, and returning the same way. Each movement is linked to either an in-breath or an out-breath.

Traditionally it is done at sunrise, facing east, on an empty stomach, before the first meal. Those three conditions are not arbitrary. At dawn the air is cool and the day has not begun to crowd in. Facing east, you are literally facing the one you greet. And on an empty stomach, the body moves freely. The sun salutation's spiritual meaning comes through when you keep all three: it is not a warm-up for the day, it is the day's first act.

## What are the twelve names of Surya?

Here is what turns the sequence from exercise into prayer. Each of the twelve positions has a name of Surya attached to it, said aloud or silently as you move. The names are usually said as short mantras, such as Om Mitraya Namah.

1. Mitra, the friend of all
2. Ravi, the radiant one
3. Surya, the one who moves and inspires activity
4. Bhanu, the one who shines
5. Khaga, the one who moves through the sky
6. Pusha, the nourisher
7. Hiranyagarbha, the golden womb, source of all
8. Marichi, the ray of light
9. Aditya, son of Aditi, the boundless
10. Savitri, the one who awakens and gives life
11. Arka, the one worthy of praise
12. Bhaskara, the one who gives light and wisdom

Each name is a quality: friendship, radiance, nourishment, awakening. Saying them turns the attention outward and upward at the same time. The surya namaskar mantras also slow you down. It is very hard to rush a sequence when you are saying a name with each position. Another short daily mantra that fits well after the rounds is [the Saraswati mantra for students](/blog/saraswati-mantra-for-students).

## How do you practise it gently?

Slow over fast. A slow round with full breaths is worth more than five rushed ones. Let the breath lead the movement, not the other way around. If you run out of breath, you are going too fast.

Knees down when needed. When you lower to the floor, put your knees down first. Nobody is grading the plank.

Skip or adapt if you have back or knee problems, high or low blood pressure, or are pregnant, and ask a doctor before you begin. The bending forward and arching back can put pressure where it is not wanted. Many people do a gentle standing version with only the arms and a light bend.

Then sit for a minute, on the floor or on a chair; [why we sit on the floor to pray](/blog/sitting-on-the-floor-to-pray) covers when the chair is the better choice. A round of japa afterwards works well; [how to use a japa mala](/blog/how-to-use-a-japa-mala) explains the basics. And if the names feel strange to say at first, [chanting for beginners](/blog/chanting-for-beginners) may help you get comfortable with the sound.

## Six rounds at dawn

Six rounds at dawn, facing east, before breakfast. One name per position. A full round of twelve positions uses all twelve names, so six rounds will take you through the list six times. Go slowly.

Keep it up for seven days. Then notice whether your mornings start differently. Some people say the first hour feels steadier. Some say they notice the light more. Some simply find they have greeted the day, instead of being ambushed by it.
