The Puranas tell of the moon god, Chandra, who married the twenty-seven daughters of Daksha and loved only one of them, Rohini. Their father cursed him to waste away. Shiva softened the curse, so the moon now shrinks and grows again, forever. It sounds like a tale of punishment. Underneath, it explains why a Hindu festival can never be pinned to one square on a wall calendar. The Hindu festival calendar USA families follow works just as it does in India: festivals follow the moon, their dates move every year, and you find the right day by using a panchangam set to your own city.

## How the Hindu festival calendar works in the USA

Start with the moon's rhythm. A lunar month runs from one new moon to the next, about twenty-nine and a half days, and is split into two fortnights: the bright half, when the moon waxes, and the dark half, when it wanes. Each fortnight holds fifteen tithis, lunar days, and each tithi is the time the moon takes to pull another twelve degrees ahead of the sun.

Most festivals are fixed to a tithi in a particular month. Ganesh Chaturthi is the fourth day of the bright half of Bhadrapada; Diwali is the new moon of Kartika, or the end of Ashvin, depending on how your region counts the month. Some festivals are fixed to a nakshatra instead, one of the twenty-seven lunar mansions, which are, not by accident, Daksha's daughters: Karthigai Deepam falls when the moon is in the star Krittika, Thaipusam when it is in Pusam.

Twelve lunar months come up about eleven days short of a solar year. So every few years an extra month, adhika masa, is slipped in, and the festivals are pulled back into their seasons. Meanwhile the South keeps solar months too: the Tamil and Malayalam calendars count months by the sun's entry into each zodiac sign, which is why Pongal, a solar festival, barely moves at all.

## Why do dates differ between India and the USA?

A tithi begins and ends at one astronomical moment, the same instant everywhere on earth. But festivals are usually fixed by which tithi prevails at a particular local time: sunrise for many, the evening for Diwali's Lakshmi puja, midnight for Janmashtami, Krishna's birth.

New York is nine and a half or ten and a half hours behind India, and California three hours further. So the tithi that prevails at sunrise in Chennai may have ended before sunrise in Chicago. The same festival can honestly fall on different calendar days in the two countries. The same thing happens with Diwali, as I have explained in [why the Diwali date moves in the USA](/blog/when-is-diwali-in-the-usa).

This is not a modern problem. The classical astronomers always calculated for a place. A festival date is an answer to the question: where are you standing?

## Reading a panchangam for your city

A panchangam, the traditional almanac, gets its name from five limbs: tithi, the lunar day; vara, the weekday; nakshatra, the star; yoga, a sun-moon combination; and karana, half a tithi. You do not need to master all five. For festivals, look for two things: the tithi or star the festival requires, and whether it prevails at the right time of day in your city.

Choose a panchangam that lets you set a location, and set it to where you live, not to Delhi or Chennai. It will also show daily periods such as [rahu kalam, the inauspicious interval](/blog/rahu-kalam-explained), and help you judge [what a muhurtham, or auspicious time, looks like](/blog/what-is-a-muhurtham) for your family.

When should you follow your temple instead? Almost always, if the temple is where you will gather. American temples publish calendars worked out for their own region, often by priests trained in a particular tradition. If your family in India celebrates a day earlier, light a lamp with them on the call and again with your community. Nobody is keeping score.

Once the dates are clear, the harder task begins, explaining them. There are simple approaches for [Diwali at the office](/blog/diwali-at-the-office), for [explaining Diwali to kids](/blog/explaining-diwali-to-kids), and for [festival rituals children can do](/blog/festival-rituals-for-kids).

## A calendar as remembering

Keeping a lunar calendar abroad is a small, daily kind of stubbornness. The office runs on Gregorian months, the school on semesters, the tax office on its own year. And somewhere under all of that, you are quietly checking which tithi it is.

That is not inefficiency. It is remembering. Chandra's curse never ended; it simply became the rhythm by which a whole civilisation keeps time. Every new moon, every full moon, you are reminded that some things wane and return. Look up tonight, and see which half of the month you are in.
