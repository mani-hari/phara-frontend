The tradition imagines a life as a book with chapters, each written by a different planet. Seven years are written by Ketu, twenty by Venus, six by the Sun, ten by the Moon. The chapters come in a fixed order, and nobody gets to skip one. That, in a sentence, is vimshottari dasha explained: a cycle of one hundred and twenty years divided among the nine grahas, the planets of Jyotish, with your birth star deciding which chapter you open on. The story sounds like fate. It is really about seasons, and nobody stays in one season forever.

## Vimshottari dasha explained: how does the cycle work?

Vimshottari means one hundred and twenty, the full span of the cycle, which the tradition treated as the ideal length of a human life. The system is set out in the Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra, the great textbook of Jyotish, and it remains the most widely used dasha system in India.

The nine planets rule their periods in this order, always:

1. Ketu, seven years
2. Venus, twenty years
3. Sun, six years
4. Moon, ten years
5. Mars, seven years
6. Rahu, eighteen years
7. Jupiter, sixteen years
8. Saturn, nineteen years
9. Mercury, seventeen years

Add them up and you get one hundred and twenty. Each of these major periods is a mahadasha. What is a mahadasha, in plain words? A long chapter whose mood, in the tradition, is coloured by its planet: Venus for comfort and relationships, Saturn for effort and patience, Jupiter for learning and growth, Ketu for letting go.

## Where does your first chapter start?

Here the book metaphor becomes precise. Each of the twenty-seven nakshatras is ruled by one of the nine planets, in the same order, three times round the sky. Ashwini is ruled by Ketu, Bharani by Venus, Krittika by the Sun, and so on. The ruler of your birth star writes your first chapter. If you are not sure of your star, [finding your nakshatra](/blog/how-to-find-your-nakshatra) takes a few minutes with your birth details.

But you do not start at the beginning of that chapter. If the Moon had already travelled halfway through your birth star when you were born, half of the first dasha is considered already spent. Someone born with the Moon near the end of a Venus star might have only a few years of Venus left before the Sun's six years begin. This is why two people with the same star can be in quite different periods at the same age.

Notice what the system is saying under its breath. You arrive mid-sentence. The book was already open.

## Chapters within chapters

Each mahadasha is divided into nine sub-periods, called antardashas or bhuktis. An antardasha is simply a smaller chapter inside the big one, and the nine planets take turns again in the same order, beginning with the lord of the mahadasha itself, each getting a share in proportion to its full period. So a person in Jupiter mahadasha starts with Jupiter-Jupiter, then Jupiter-Saturn, then Jupiter-Mercury, and so on. Astrologers read the pair together, and the transits of slow planets on top of that; [gochara, planetary transits](/blog/gochara-transits-explained) explains that layer.

This is where fear usually creeps in. People hear "Saturn period" or "Rahu period" and brace for disaster, especially when a Saturn dasha overlaps with [Shani's sade sati](/blog/shani-sade-sati-explained). But a Saturn period is not a sentence. In the tradition, Saturn teaches through work, delay and endurance; many people remember their Saturn years as the ones in which they became capable. The same is true of Ketu, the headless planet whose story is told in [Ketu graha worship](/blog/ketu-graha-worship): his chapter is often about losing what was not needed.

And the old astrologers never read a dasha alone. They read it against the whole chart, and against a person's own effort, which the tradition calls purushartha. The chapter sets a theme. You still write the sentences.

## Ask what the chapter is teaching

Seasons change whether we fear them or not. The monsoon does not ask permission, and it is not a punishment for the summer.

If you know your current dasha, try asking a different question of it. Not "what will this take from me?" but "what is this chapter teaching?" A Venus period may be asking you to enjoy what you have. A Mercury period may be asking you to learn a new skill. A Saturn period may be asking for patience you did not know you had.
