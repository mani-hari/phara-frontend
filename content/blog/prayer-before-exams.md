The night before an exam, the problem is rarely what you know. It is where your attention keeps running: to the chapter you skipped, to your friend who seems calmer, to what your parents will say. A short prayer before exams gives that attention somewhere to land. Five minutes, a lamp, three slow breaths and a Saraswati or Hayagriva mantra, done the same way each day, can settle a scattered mind before you study or walk into the hall. It will not write the paper for you. It can help you sit down to it.

## A five-minute prayer before exams

Here is a simple sequence. Do it at your desk or in front of the family altar.

1. **Wash your face and hands.** Cold water, if you can. The body registers a change of state.
2. **Light a lamp.** A small ghee or oil lamp, or a tea-light. Place it where you can see it without turning your head.
3. **Three slow breaths.** In through the nose, a little longer out. Let the shoulders drop on each exhale.
4. **Chant.** Choose one: the Saraswati mantra for students, "Om Aim Saraswatyai Namah", or the Hayagriva mantra for exams, which begins "Jnanandamayam devam", saluting the form of Vishnu associated with clear memory. Chant eleven or twenty-one times, slowly. If you are unsure how to begin chanting, this guide to [chanting for beginners](/blog/chanting-for-beginners) helps.
5. **Place your pen and books before the lamp** for a moment, then pick them up and begin.

Saraswati is the goddess of learning and speech; Hayagriva is the horse-headed Vishnu who, in the Puranas, rescued the stolen Vedas. If you want the stories behind them, read about [Saraswati and Hayagriva, the deities of learning](/blog/saraswati-and-hayagriva).

## Does praying before an exam actually help?

Honestly, it depends on what you expect. If you expect the prayer to change the questions, no ritual can promise that. If you expect it to change how you meet the questions, many students find that it does.

Here is why. The mind learns by association. An athlete who does the same warm-up before every match is telling the body, "now we play." A prayer before exam for concentration works in a similar way. When you light the same lamp, take the same breaths and chant the same sound every time you sit down to study, the mind starts to recognise the sequence as a cue. After a week or so, many people notice they settle faster. Nerves still come, but they come to a steadier place.

What prayer cannot replace is the studying itself. Tradition never claimed it could. Saraswati holds a book in her hand, not a shortcut.

## The body before the brain

The mind you bring to an exam lives inside a body. If the body is exhausted, dehydrated and full of sugar and caffeine, no mantra will make up for it.

- **Sleep.** Memory consolidates during sleep. An all-nighter trades tomorrow's recall for tonight's panic.
- **Water.** Keep a glass on the desk and drink from it each time you finish a section.
- **Light meals.** A heavy lunch before an afternoon exam makes the eyelids drop.
- **The phone in another room.** Not face down. Another room. Every buzz breaks the thread.

The tradition understood this well. Students were encouraged to study in the early morning, in the hours before dawn, when the house is quiet and the mind has not yet filled up with the day. If you want to try it, [waking early in brahma muhurta](/blog/brahma-muhurta-waking-early) explains how to ease into it without wrecking your sleep.

## Try this for the whole week

Do not wait for the morning of the exam. Start seven days before, and do the same five-minute ritual every time you sit down to study. Same lamp, same breaths, same mantra, same order.

Notice what changes. How long does it take you to start working after you sit down? How often do you reach for the phone? How do you feel walking into the hall?
