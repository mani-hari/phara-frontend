Ada pradhaman is the payasam that closes an Onam sadya, the great vegetarian feast served on a banana leaf, and it is richer than anything before it: rice flakes, dark jaggery and coconut milk added in three strengths. It is also easier than it looks. This ada pradhaman recipe works with canned coconut milk and store-bought ada, and the only real rule is patience with the last pour of coconut milk.

## Ada pradhaman recipe, step by step

Ada here means small flakes of rice dough, steamed and dried; Kerala stores sell them ready-made.

**Ingredients (serves six)**

- 100 g rice ada (about ¾ cup)
- 200 g dark jaggery (1 cup), melted in 120 ml water (½ cup) and strained
- 3 tablespoons ghee
- 500 ml thin coconut milk (2 cups)
- 250 ml medium coconut milk (1 cup)
- 120 ml thick coconut milk (½ cup)
- ½ teaspoon cardamom powder and a pinch of dry ginger powder
- 12 cashews, a tablespoon of raisins and 2 tablespoons thin coconut slivers

**Method**

1. Drop the ada into plenty of boiling water and cook until soft, about fifteen minutes. Drain and rinse in cold water so the flakes do not stick.
2. In a heavy pan, warm the jaggery syrup with a tablespoon of ghee. Add the cooked ada and stir over medium heat until the mixture turns thick and glossy.
3. Pour in the thin coconut milk and simmer, stirring, for about ten minutes until it thickens.
4. Add the medium coconut milk and simmer a few minutes more.
5. Turn the heat to its lowest. Stir in the thick coconut milk and cardamom and dry ginger, and switch off the heat just before it boils.
6. Fry the coconut slivers, cashews and raisins in the remaining ghee and pour everything over the payasam.

## How do you make it with US ingredients?

Most of what you need is on the shelf of a South Indian store, and the rest is in any supermarket.

- **Ada.** Look for packets labelled rice ada or palada in the Kerala section. Pink or white both work.
- **Coconut milk.** One 400 ml can gives you all three strengths. Pour off 120 ml of the thickest part from the top for the final addition. Dilute a little of the rest with an equal amount of water for the medium milk, and dilute the remainder further for the thin.
- **Jaggery.** Dark jaggery gives the traditional deep brown colour. Panela from the Latin aisle is a close substitute.
- **Instant Pot.** Cook the ada on pressure for about five minutes with plenty of water, then drain and rinse.

The one mistake to avoid is boiling the thick milk. It splits and turns grainy. Stand at the stove for the last two minutes and watch it closely.

## Why is ada pradhaman offered at Onam?

Onam falls in the Malayalam month of Chingam, on the day the Thiruvonam star rises, usually August or September. It welcomes King Mahabali, who, the story says, returns once a year to see his people, and it honours Vamana, the dwarf avatar of Vishnu worshipped as Thrikkakara Appan. Why a festival welcomes back the king a god pushed underground is the question at the centre of [Mahabali and the meaning of Onam](/blog/onam-mahabali-meaning); the dwarf's side of it is in [the story of Vamana's three steps](/blog/vamana-three-steps-story).

The payasam is offered first at the home shrine along with the sadya, then served last on the leaf. The order of the leaf, and why a leaf at all, is laid out in [eating from a banana leaf](/blog/eating-on-a-banana-leaf).

## Offer the first ladle

If you only make one dish this Onam, make the payasam. Try giving the whole process your attention: the flakes softening, the jaggery turning glossy, the three milks going in one after another. Offer the first ladle at the lamp before anyone tastes.

Then invite someone who has never had it, a neighbour, a colleague, a friend from another state. Payasam is meant to be shared, and [prasad and gratitude](/blog/prasad-and-gratitude) says more about why the sharing is part of the offering. If you enjoy the rhythm of festival sweets, [ellu urundai, the sesame balls of winter](/blog/ellu-urundai-sesame-balls), is a quicker project for the cold months.
