Kashi means the luminous one. Varanasi, the city on the Ganga that the tradition calls Kashi, has been treated as sacred for so long that the Kashi Vishwanath temple feels less like a building and more like a centre the whole city turns around. Shiva is worshipped here as Vishwanath, lord of the universe, and the temple is counted among his twelve jyotirlingas. But to understand why people have come here for thousands of years, you have to look past the temple to the river, the ghats and the fires.

## Why is the Kashi Vishwanath temple a jyotirlinga?

A jyotirlinga is a lingam of light. The idea comes from a story in the Shiva Purana. Brahma and Vishnu were arguing about which of them was greater, when a column of fire appeared between them, rising above the sky and plunging below the earth. They agreed that whoever found its end would win. Vishnu became a boar and dug down. Brahma became a swan and flew up. Neither found the end. Shiva emerged from the column, and the argument was over.

The twelve jyotirlingas are the places where tradition says that endless light is present in a particular spot, and Kashi is among the most revered of them. The same column of fire lies behind [the meaning of Maha Shivaratri](/blog/maha-shivaratri-meaning) and the great flame of [Karthigai Deepam](/blog/karthigai-deepam-significance).

Notice what the story is doing. The two most powerful gods try to measure something and fail. The lingam is not a picture of Shiva. It is a marker for what cannot be measured.

## Why does Varanasi matter so much?

The Kashi Khanda, a section of the Skanda Purana devoted to the city, calls it the place Shiva never leaves. Other sacred places are visited by the gods. Kashi, the tradition says, is where Shiva lives. From that follows the belief that dying in Kashi brings moksha, release from the cycle of birth and death, with Shiva himself whispering the saving mantra into the ear of the dying. [What moksha means](/blog/what-is-moksha) is its own subject.

This is why the ghats, the stone steps down to the Ganga, hold both bathing and cremation. At Manikarnika ghat the funeral fires burn day and night. People bathe a short walk away. Children play cricket on the steps. Nobody pretends death is elsewhere.

You do not need to share the belief to feel what it does. A city where the fires are always visible is a city that has made impermanence ordinary. Sit on those steps for an hour and something in you stops bargaining. That is a kind of meditation the city performs on you whether you intend it or not. Families abroad who carry a parent's ashes to the Ganga meet it at close range; [immersing ashes from abroad](/blog/immersing-ashes-from-abroad) covers the practical side of that journey.

## What is the Kashi-Rameswaram link?

For centuries, the full pilgrimage tied Kashi to Rameswaram, at the southern tip of India. The pilgrim began with a bath in the sea at Rameswaram and collected sand there. They carried it north to Kashi and offered it to the Ganga. Then they took Ganga water home, south again, to pour over the lingam at Rameswaram. The south's sand given to the north's river, the north's river given to the south's Shiva.

It is one practice stretched across a subcontinent, and the story at the southern end, of a lingam made of sand by Sita, is told in [the Rameswaram temple and its sand lingam](/blog/rameswaram-ramanathaswamy-temple). Families who cannot make the journey often ask for rites at Rameswaram instead; [the Tila Homam at Rameswaram](/blog/tila-homam-at-rameswaram-meaning) explains one of them.

## Water poured slowly at dusk

Wherever you live, at dusk, pour a little water over a small lingam if you have one, or over the soil of a plant if you do not. Pour slowly. Watch the water go.

Then sit for a minute with one idea: nothing, not even this evening, stays. Not the light, not the day's worries, not the water in the saucer. It sounds bleak written down. Many people find it is the opposite. When you stop asking the evening to last, you can actually be in it. That is what Kashi has been teaching by its river for a very long time.
