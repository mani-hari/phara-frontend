Karadaiyan Nombu is kept at a precise moment, the instant one Tamil month turns into the next, and the offering is simple: a steamed adai and a lump of butter that has not melted. Nombu means a vow or observance, and this karadaiyan nombu adai recipe gives you both kinds of adai, sweet and salt, made from roasted rice flour and karamani, the small cowpeas. Everything about the day asks you to be present, and on time.

## Karadaiyan nombu adai recipe

Many families make their own flour: raw rice soaked for an hour, spread on a cloth to dry, ground fine and then roasted lightly until it is dry and fragrant. Store-bought rice flour works if you roast it well.

**For the sweet adai**

- 150 g roasted rice flour (1 cup)
- 2 tablespoons karamani (dried red or white cowpeas), soaked for a few hours and cooked until just soft
- 150 g grated jaggery (¾ cup)
- 375 ml water (1½ cups)
- 2 tablespoons small coconut bits
- ¼ teaspoon cardamom powder

**For the salt adai**

- 150 g roasted rice flour (1 cup) and 2 tablespoons cooked karamani
- 375 ml water (1½ cups) with salt to taste
- 1 tablespoon oil, 1 teaspoon mustard seeds, 1 green chilli, 1 teaspoon grated ginger, curry leaves, a pinch of asafoetida, 2 tablespoons coconut bits

**Method**

1. For the sweet adai, melt the jaggery in the water, strain, and bring it back to a boil. Add coconut, cardamom and karamani.
2. Lower the heat and add the rice flour slowly, stirring, until it forms a thick dough that comes away from the pan. Cover and let it cool slightly.
3. For the salt adai, heat oil, add mustard seeds, then chilli, ginger, curry leaves, asafoetida and coconut. Pour in the salted water, add karamani, bring to a boil, and stir in the flour the same way.
4. With greased palms, shape each dough into small flat discs and press a hole in the centre with a finger.
5. Steam on a greased plate or banana leaf for ten to twelve minutes, until the adai look glossy and are no longer sticky.

## What is Karadaiyan Nombu remembering?

The nombu remembers Savitri, whose story is told in the Mahabharata. When Yama, the lord of death, came for her husband Satyavan, Savitri followed him. She did not plead or bargain. She walked beside him and talked, with such clarity and steadiness that Yama granted her boons, and she used them, carefully, until Satyavan's life was returned.

What the tradition honours here is not a miracle so much as a quality: steadiness under pressure, the refusal to be rushed or frightened out of what matters. Savitri stands in a long line of women the tradition holds up, and [how Hinduism honours women and the Goddess](/blog/women-in-hinduism-goddess) places her among the others. Many families who pray for a steady marriage also turn to the goddess of [the Kanchi Kamakshi temple](/blog/kanchi-kamakshi-temple).

On the day, married women tie a yellow thread, often with a small piece of turmeric, and offer adai with unmelted butter. The Tamil prayer that goes with it asks, roughly, that in return for this adai and this butter, the bond with one's companion may never break. It is the same wish a bride carries in [the thali, the sacred wedding necklace](/blog/thali-mangalsutra-meaning).

## When is it kept?

Karadaiyan Nombu is kept at the moment the Tamil month of Maasi ends and Panguni begins. This is a solar transition, and it usually falls around mid-March. The exact minute is given in the panchangam and changes each time; learning [how to read a panchangam](/blog/how-to-read-a-panchangam) lets you find it yourself.

The adai and butter are placed before the lamp, the thread is tied at the given minute, and only then is the adai eaten. For a small abhishekam on the same day, [panchamrutham at home](/blog/panchamrutham-at-home) takes only two minutes.

## Present at the right minute

Check the transition time a few days ahead, and convert it to your local time zone if you are abroad; it may land in the middle of your working day or the middle of the night. Prepare the flour early, and cook the karamani the day before.

Then, when the minute comes, stop whatever you are doing. Tie the thread, offer the adai, sit for a breath or two. Even if the offering is short, let it happen at the right time. Being exactly on time for something, once, can be its own kind of attention.
