Varalakshmi Vratham falls on a Friday, which means for most people in the US it falls on a workday. That is not a problem. A varalakshmi vratham pooja at home splits cleanly into two halves: the heart of it before you open the laptop, and the rest after sunset. The festival is kept on the Friday just before the full moon of Shravana, usually in July or August, and it asks for one thing above all: a decorated pot that stands in for Lakshmi, the goddess of wellbeing, and your full attention while you sit with it. No priest is needed. Check the current year's panchangam for the exact Friday.

## Varalakshmi vratham pooja at home: setting up the kalasam

The kalasam, a pot that holds water or grain and becomes the seat of the goddess for the day, is the centre of everything. Set it up the night before. You will be grateful for it at six in the morning.

Take a silver or brass pot. Fill it with water, or with raw rice, a few coins, a piece of turmeric and some betel leaves. Arrange five mango leaves around the mouth so they fan outward, and place a coconut smeared with turmeric on top. Many families tie a blouse piece, a small square of silk, around the neck of the pot, then set a Lakshmi face, silver or printed, against the coconut. Add whatever jewellery you like, flowers, and a garland. The whole thing sits on a plate or a banana leaf spread with rice.

Notice what this does. Decorating the kalasam is slow, fiddly work for the hands, and it is the best preparation there is. By the time the face is in place, your mind has already arrived.

## What is the Charumathi story?

The vratham has a story, and in the tradition Shiva tells it to Parvati. In the town of Kundina lived a woman named Charumathi, known for the care she gave her husband and his parents. One night Lakshmi appeared to her in a dream and asked to be worshipped on the Friday before the Shravana full moon. Charumathi told the other women of the town, they kept the vratham together, and the story says their homes filled with gold and grain.

It is easy to hear this as a transaction: worship on Friday, receive wealth. Look again. Lakshmi goes to someone who is already paying attention, already caring for the people around her. The vratham is gratitude for what is present, not an order placed for more. Vara means boon, and the boon, in the telling, found a house that was awake.

## A workday version that still counts

Here is a shape that fits between the alarm and the first meeting.

1. **Morning, twenty minutes.** Bathe, light the lamp before the kalasam, and say your sankalpam, the spoken statement of intention before a pooja. It can be one honest sentence in your own language.
2. **The thread.** Tie the nombu charadu, a yellow thread dipped in turmeric, sometimes with a flower knotted in, on your right wrist. Someone else can tie it, or you can do it yourself.
3. **A short chant.** Read the Lakshmi Ashtottaram, the hundred and eight names, or simply repeat one name slowly for a few minutes.
4. **Naivedyam.** Offer fruit and one sweet. If you have time the evening before, the recipes in [Varalakshmi Vratham naivedyam](/blog/varalakshmi-vratham-naivedyam) are made for exactly this day.

In the evening, light the lamp again, do the aarti, and give thamboolam, a small offering of betel leaves, fruit, turmeric and kumkum, to friends who drop by. In the US this often becomes the real gathering: a few families on the living room floor after work. The same approach works for other Lakshmi days; [Lakshmi puja at home abroad](/blog/lakshmi-puja-at-home-abroad) covers the Deepavali version, and [what the kalasam means](/blog/kalasam-meaning) explains the pot at the centre of both.

## Try this: one undivided window

The monsoon festivals come close together, [Naga Panchami](/blog/naga-panchami-meaning), Varalakshmi, [Avani Avittam](/blog/avani-avittam-meaning) and then [Krishna Janmashtami at home](/blog/krishna-janmashtami-at-home), and it is tempting to rush each one to get through the list. Resist that.

Whatever the weekday, give your full attention to one ten-minute window of the puja. Phone in another room. Sit before the kalasam, look at the face, breathe, and let the list go quiet. If the rest of the day is rushed, that window is still whole. Lakshmi, the tradition says, stays where she is noticed. So does almost everything else.
