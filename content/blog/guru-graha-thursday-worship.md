Every time Jupiter moves into a new sign, my aunt in Chennai reads out the Guru Peyarchi palangal, the predictions for each rasi, over the phone to anyone who will listen. I listen politely. But the Thursday habit I actually value is much quieter: a yellow flower placed before a small picture of Dakshinamurthy, and a thought, just one, for a teacher. The traditional guru graha remedies are like that. A lamp, a prayer, a handful of chana, a kindness to someone who taught you. They are among the gentlest practices in the tradition, which seems right for the planet of wisdom.

## Who is Guru among the grahas?

In Jyotish, Guru, the planet Jupiter, is the great benefic. He stands for wisdom, teachers, children, dharma, faith and good fortune. He is traditionally associated with Thursday, the colour yellow, Bengal gram, which most of us call chana or kondakadalai, and the yellow sapphire. His other name is Brihaspati, the guru of the gods, the teacher who advised Indra and the devas.

Here is something that confuses many people, and I was confused about it for years. On Thursdays, many Tamil families pray to Dakshinamurthy, the form of Shiva who sits facing south under a banyan tree and teaches in silence. Brihaspati, the planet, and Dakshinamurthy, the god, are distinct. One is a graha; the other is Shiva himself. But both are teachers, and over time families began offering Thursday prayers to Dakshinamurthy as the teacher of all teachers. Practice varies. Some families pray to Brihaspati directly, some to Dakshinamurthy, some to Sai Baba or Guru Raghavendra. I wrote about those traditions in [Thursday worship of Sai Baba and the Guru](/blog/thursday-sai-baba-guru).

## Guru graha remedies, week by week

The practices are simple enough to keep on a workday.

- **A ghee lamp on Thursday evening.** Light it at the same time each week.
- **Yellow flowers**, such as marigolds or chrysanthemums, offered before the deity.
- **Chana offered or given.** Many families make sundal from Bengal gram on Thursdays.
- **The Brihaspati mantra.** The short form is Om Brihaspataye Namah, chanted eleven or one hundred and eight times.
- **Honouring a teacher.** Feeding, thanking or simply visiting someone who taught you. This is the remedy the tradition itself points to most clearly.

When Jupiter changes sign, families often add special Thursday prayers; my own family's version is described in [the Guru Peyarchi piece](/blog/guru-peyarchi-jupiter-transit).

Jupiter's great temple in Tamil Nadu is the Apatsahayeswarar temple at Alangudi, near Kumbakonam. Apatsahayeswarar means the lord who helps in danger; the story goes that Shiva drank the poison at the churning of the ocean here, and that is why he is worshipped as the protector. Dakshinamurthy is worshipped as Guru in a prominent shrine, and Thursday queues can be long. Friends who have visited tell me the yellow of the flowers on a Thursday morning is something you do not forget.

## Who were your gurus?

The Guru Gita has the verse most of us learned as children: *Gurur Brahma, Gurur Vishnu, Gurur Devo Maheshwarah.* The teacher is Brahma, the teacher is Vishnu, the teacher is Shiva.

It is a big claim, and for most of my life I took it as poetry. Then I started making a list. My maths teacher in Chennai, who stayed after class for three weeks until I understood fractions. The family astrologer in Madurai, who never once frightened my parents, even when he could have. A senior colleague in my first job here, who explained the same process to me four times without sighing. None of them were saints. All of them changed the direction of my life, a little.

The verse is not asking us to worship every teacher as a god. I think it is asking something more ordinary: to notice that wisdom came to us through people, and to be grateful on a regular day of the week. Guru Purnima, the full moon of teachers, is the festival for this, and [Guru Purnima's meaning](/blog/guru-purnima-meaning) tells why it falls where it does. But once a year is a long gap. Thursday comes every week.

## One Thursday message

One small thing to carry into this week: this Thursday, send one message to a teacher who changed your life. It does not need to be long. "I was thinking of you, and of the fractions" is enough.

Then light the lamp. If you want to follow the week's planets further, Friday comes next, in [Shukra, Venus, and Friday worship](/blog/shukra-graha-worship). Wisdom on Thursday, beauty on Friday. That seems like a good order.
