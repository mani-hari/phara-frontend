A Friday Lakshmi pooja does not need a priest or an hour. Ten minutes, one lamp, a small kolam at the door and a sweet will do. What makes it work is not length but repetition. Do the same ten minutes at the same hour every Friday and something interesting happens: the house starts to expect it. The counter gets cleared earlier. Someone remembers to buy flowers. The evening shifts gear on its own.

This is especially useful if you live far from the temple, work late, or grew up with a grandmother who did all of this and never wrote it down.

## Why is Friday Lakshmi's day?

Friday is Shukravara, the day of Shukra, the planet Venus. In Jyotish, Venus is traditionally associated with beauty, comfort, grace and plenty, and in many homes Friday worship goes to Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity, who carries exactly those qualities. The planetary side, including the story of Venus as guru of the asuras, belongs to [Shukra graha worship](/blog/shukra-graha-worship).

Because Lakshmi is associated with cleanliness and light, many households clean the house before Friday evening, wash the threshold and draw a fresh kolam, and light the lamp a little earlier than usual. The old reasoning is that Lakshmi enters a home that is ready to receive her. You do not need to believe that literally to notice what it does: the house is prepared for the weekend, and so are you.

## A Friday Lakshmi pooja in ten minutes

Here is a sequence that fits between work and dinner.

1. **Minute one.** Wipe the shrine shelf and the lamp. Change the water in any flower vase.
2. **Minutes two and three.** Draw a small kolam at the front door or in front of the shrine. Rice flour or chalk is fine. Keep it simple; a few dots and loops. The drawing has its own logic, set out in [the meaning of kolam and rangoli](/blog/kolam-and-rangoli-meaning).
3. **Minute four.** Light the lamp, with ghee if you can. Sit down.
4. **Minute five.** Offer flowers, a pinch of kumkum and turmeric before Lakshmi's picture.
5. **Minutes six to eight.** Recite the Mahalakshmi Ashtakam, eight verses praising the Goddess, or the 108 names of Lakshmi, or simply Om Mahalakshmyai Namah eleven times. The [Mahalakshmi Ashtakam's meaning](/blog/mahalakshmi-ashtakam-meaning) is worth reading once, so the words are not just sounds.
6. **Minute nine.** Offer a sweet: jaggery, fruit, or payasam if you made some. [Paal payasam as naivedyam](/blog/paal-payasam-naivedyam) is a traditional Friday offering, and many homes make [sundal on Fridays](/blog/sundal-for-navratri-and-fridays).
7. **Minute ten.** A short aarti: wave the lamp or a piece of camphor three times clockwise. Then sit for three breaths.

That is a complete Friday pooja.

## How do you keep it going every week?

Most people start with enthusiasm and stop by the fourth week. A few things help.

- **Keep a Friday tray ready.** Lamp, wicks, a small bottle of ghee, matches, kumkum, a bell. When everything is in one place, starting takes seconds.
- **Set a reminder for dusk.** A phone alarm labelled "lamp" is not unspiritual. It is practical.
- **Give the kolam to the children.** Many kids who will not sit through a pooja will happily draw on the floor. It makes them part of it.
- **If you miss a Friday, simply light the lamp the next week.** There is no penalty. Nobody is keeping score. The ritual is a way of noticing, and you can start noticing again at any point.

Try not to add steps too quickly. The ten minutes work because they are ten minutes.

## Four Fridays

Try this for four Fridays: the same ten minutes at the same hour. Do not change the routine, do not make it longer, do not skip the kolam.

By the fourth week, pay attention to Friday evening itself. How does the house feel when you walk in? How does your body feel when the lamp is lit? Many people find that the week now has a clear edge to it, a moment when work ends and home begins. That edge is worth more than any single pooja, and it is what the tradition was building all along.
