I had four browser tabs, two chat windows and a meeting open when my father called from Tamil Nadu to chant a sloka with me. It was a short one, the kind he has chanted every morning for fifty years. I could not hold the line for two minutes. My eyes kept drifting to a notification in the corner of the screen, and he noticed, and he did not say anything, which was worse. That evening I thought of Mithuna rasi, the sign of the twins and the divided mind. That afternoon, I was one.

## Why does Mithuna rasi carry two figures?

Mithuna rasi is the third of the twelve signs. As the family astrologer reminds everyone, your rasi is your Moon's sign, not the Sun sign the magazines print. It holds the second half of Mrigashira, all of Ardra, and the first three quarters of Punarvasu. Its ruling planet is Budha, Mercury, the planet of speech, learning, trade and quick thinking. Those magazines would call it Gemini.

Its symbol is not simply twins. In the Indian tradition it is a couple, a man holding a mace and a woman holding a veena: strength and music, action and art, two halves of one life. I like that better than twins. Twins are copies. A couple is two different things trying to move together.

## The restless mind tradition describes

Tradition describes Mithuna natives as quick, curious and witty, lovers of books, talk and ideas. The shadow is scattered attention: ten things begun, none finished, a mind always in the next room. My family astrologer in Madurai once described a Mithuna cousin of mine as "a very intelligent person who never sits down." He meant it kindly.

The best description of that mind is not in an astrology book at all. It is in the sixth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, when Arjuna, a man with more discipline than most of us will ever have, turns to Krishna and complains. "Chanchalam hi manah Krishna, pramathi balavad dridham": the mind is restless, Krishna, turbulent, strong and stubborn; to control it seems as hard as controlling the wind.

I find it comforting that Krishna does not argue. He agrees. The mind is restless, he says, and hard to hold. "Abhyasena tu Kaunteya, vairagyena cha grihyate": but it can be held by practice and by letting go. Not by force. By repetition, and by loosening the grip on everything else. If that verse speaks to you, [the Gita's steady mind](/blog/sthitaprajna-steady-mind) stays with it longer.

## Temples and poojas traditionally linked through Budha

Budha's temple is Thiruvenkadu, near Sirkazhi, where the planet of speech has its own shrine within a large Shiva temple. Wednesdays are Budha's day, and a lamp and a short prayer on Wednesday evening are the simplest observance.

Mithuna loves learning, so the deities of learning sit naturally with the sign. Hayagreeva, the horse-headed form of Vishnu who restored the Vedas, and Saraswati, the goddess of speech and knowledge, are both traditional choices, and [the Hayagreeva homam and what it means](/blog/hayagreeva-homam-meaning) explains why he became the scholar's god. Students with the Moon in Mithuna often keep a small Saraswati image near their desk.

## One small thing for this week

My father called again the next week. I closed the laptop before I answered. We chanted the whole sloka, and afterwards he told me about his knee and I told him about my son's soccer match, and neither of us was in a hurry.

One small thing to carry into this week: close every tab but one for the first twenty minutes of your day. Give the twins one task to share, and see if they can walk together for a while.
