Shanti is said three times because there are three places peace can break: inside you, between you and others, and in the world you cannot control. That is the traditional Om shanti shanti shanti meaning, and it is more practical than it sounds. Each shanti is aimed somewhere different. Once you know where, the chant stops being a polite ending and becomes something you can actually use at night.

## Om shanti shanti shanti meaning, in three parts

Shanti means peace, the settled quiet that comes when disturbance ends. The tradition names three kinds of suffering, called the tapa-traya, the three fires or afflictions, and gives one shanti to each.

**Adhyatmika.** Suffering that arises from within: the body's pain, illness and fatigue, and the mind's anxiety, grief and restlessness. The first shanti is addressed here.

**Adhibhautika.** Suffering that comes from other beings: people, animals, insects, the colleague who undermines you, the neighbour's dog at three in the morning. The second shanti is for this.

**Adhidaivika.** Suffering that comes from forces beyond any person's control: weather, earthquake, flood, the larger accidents of fate. In older language, these were the domain of the devas. The third shanti goes out to them.

Some teachers add that the three are said with slightly different force: the first loud enough for the world, the second softer, the third softest, turning inward. You will hear it chanted that way in some traditions and evenly in others. Either is fine.

Notice what the list covers. Body and mind, other people, and the uncontrollable. There is almost nothing a human being suffers that does not fall into one of these three.

## Where does the chant come from?

The triple shanti closes the shanti mantras, the peace invocations that open and close the Upanishads. Students traditionally recited them with their teacher before and after study.

The best known is Om Saha Navavatu, found in the Taittiriya Upanishad and also recited with the Katha Upanishad. It asks that teacher and student be protected together, nourished together, work together with vigour, that their study be bright, and that they not quarrel. Then: Om shanti shanti shanti. [The meaning of Om Sahana Vavatu](/blog/om-sahana-vavatu-meaning) takes that prayer line by line.

Another is Om Purnamadah, the invocation of the Ishavasya Upanishad: that is whole, this is whole; from the whole the whole arises; take the whole from the whole and the whole remains. It too ends with three shantis.

So the chant was, from the beginning, a way of clearing the ground. You said it before study so that nothing from within, from others or from the world would disturb the work. You said it after study so that the work would rest in peace. Why sound, rather than silent thought, does this work is a question for [what a mantra is](/blog/what-is-a-mantra).

## Three shantis before sleep

Here is a way to use the three shantis at night, before sleep. Sit on the edge of the bed, or lie down. Take a slow breath.

**The first shanti, to your own body.** Say it softly, and let your attention go to wherever the body is holding pain or tension: a sore back, a tight jaw, a restless stomach. You are not fixing it. You are offering it peace.

**The second shanti, to someone you are at odds with.** A colleague, a sibling, an old friend you have not called. Say their name silently, then the shanti. You do not have to forgive them tonight. You are only declining to carry the fight into sleep.

**The third shanti, to the thing you cannot fix.** A parent's illness far away. The news. A diagnosis. A decision that is not yours to make. Say the shanti and let it go out to that.

That is all. Three breaths, three words. Try it every night for seven nights, and notice what softens. Many people find the second shanti the hardest and, by the end of the week, the most useful.

The chant is especially steadying in hard seasons: around illness, surgery or loss. ## Where it starts

The chant does not ask the world to be peaceful first. It does not wait for the colleague to apologise or the weather to clear. It starts where you are sitting, with the body you have tonight, and moves outward only from there.

That order matters. Peace inside first, then between people, then towards the world. Say it tonight in that order, and see how far it reaches.
