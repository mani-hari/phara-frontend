Thalai Deepavali, the first Deepavali after the wedding, is meant to be spent at the bride's parents' home. The couple arrive in new clothes, the son-in-law is fussed over, and the whole family wakes before dawn together. If you are newly married in the US, that home may be a fourteen-hour flight away. You can still keep the heart of it: new clothes, a lamp lit together before sunrise, and the parents' blessings, even over a screen.

## What is Thalai Deepavali?

It is a Tamil custom. Thalai means head, or first; this is the couple's first Deepavali as a married pair, and it is hosted by the bride's family. In South India, Deepavali is kept on the pre-dawn of Naraka Chaturdashi, near the new moon of the Tamil month of Aippasi, usually in October or November; check the current year's panchangam for the exact day.

The day follows a familiar shape. Everyone rises in the dark for the oil bath, sesame oil massaged into the hair and washed off with warm water and shikakai, a soapnut powder for the hair; the story behind it is in [the Naraka Chaturdashi oil bath](/blog/naraka-chaturdashi-oil-bath). Then new clothes, a lamp before the family deity, sweets, and the first crackers, lit by the couple together. For a newly married woman, this is the first Deepavali she spends as a daughter who has left home and come back. For the groom, it is the first time the bride's family treats him as one of their own.

What sits underneath all this is attention. Waking in the dark, feeling warm oil on the scalp, wearing something that has never been worn: the body knows this is a threshold, even if the mind is still sleepy.

## What do the bride's parents give, and what does it mean?

Gifts for the son-in-law are a well-known part of the day. Traditionally the groom receives a silk veshti, the white wrap worn at the waist, and an angavastram, the matching shoulder cloth, and the bride a silk sari. Some families add a gold ring or chain for the son-in-law; others give silver vessels, a lamp or a set of plates for the new home. Sweets and savouries go home in large boxes.

It is easy to read this as transaction, and some families do slide that way. The older meaning is welcome. The gifts say: you are part of this house now; you have a place at this table. Customs differ widely between families and regions, and modest gifts carry exactly the same welcome as lavish ones. Many young couples today gently ask their parents to keep it simple, and that is a kind of respect too.

Seen another way, the first Deepavali echoes the wedding's threads and promises. [What the thali and mangalsutra mean](/blog/thali-mangalsutra-meaning) shows how much of a marriage is about two families holding a new person between them.

## Thalai Deepavali far from home: can you keep it abroad?

Yes. If you can fly home, do; parents treasure it. If you cannot, and many first-year couples in the US cannot, here is how to keep Thalai Deepavali in the US without pretending it is the same.

Wear the new clothes the parents sent, or buy something new together. Wake before dawn on the day, even if it is a weekday and you have a meeting at nine. Take the oil bath, or at least oil your hair. Light one lamp together. Time a video call to India's morning, which will be your evening before or your very early morning, and bow to the parents on the screen so they can bless you. A few ideas in [staying close to grandparents on video calls](/blog/grandparents-on-video-calls) work just as well for parents. Make one sweet from the bride's mother's recipe, even if it comes out wrong. It will taste like home anyway.

For the larger festival, the practical guide to [celebrating Diwali in the USA](/blog/celebrate-diwali-in-the-usa) covers the rest: lights, workplaces, friends.

A first Diwali after marriage can feel a little lonely abroad. That is honest. It is also yours, the start of whatever tradition the two of you will build.

## Try this on the day

On your first Deepavali together, light one lamp before dawn with both hands on it, yours and your spouse's. Sit for one minute in front of the flame. No phones. Whatever else the day holds, work, calls, crackers or none, that is the part you begin together.

If you want to make it a habit, there is a simple practice in [praying together as a couple](/blog/praying-together-as-a-couple) that picks up where the lamp leaves off.
