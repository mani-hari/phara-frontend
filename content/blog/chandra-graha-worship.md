The Purusha Sukta of the Rig Veda says the Moon was born from the mind of the cosmic being: chandrama manaso jatah. Anyone who has lain awake on a bright full-moon night already suspects the two are related. That link, Moon and mind, is the key to the traditional chandra graha remedies. Monday worship of Shiva, water or milk offered, white rice or cloth given away, a quiet evening: all of them are ways of settling the mind by paying attention to the Moon.

## Who is Chandra in Jyotish?

In Jyotish, Vedic astrology, Chandra, the Moon, represents the manas, the mind, along with emotions, memory, nourishment and the mother. The Moon's position at birth gives you your rasi and your birth star, which is why Indian astrology gives it more weight than Western astrology does; [rasi and nakshatra, side by side](/blog/rasi-vs-nakshatra) shows how the two are worked out.

Chandra is traditionally associated with Monday, the colour white, rice, milk and the pearl. Many lists link him with Parvati; others with Shiva, since lists vary by region and family. The image most devotees carry is Shiva as Chandrashekhara, with the crescent moon resting in his matted hair. The story goes that Shiva gave the waning Moon a place on his head when it was cursed to fade away, so the Moon returns every month instead of disappearing forever.

## Chandra graha remedies from tradition

The practices are gentle, which suits the planet.

- **Monday worship of Shiva.** A lamp in the evening, a little milk or water poured over a lingam or offered before a picture, and Om Namah Shivaya or Om Chandraya Namah chanted eleven times. Many people keep a light Monday fast; there is a practical guide to [the somvar vrat, the Monday fast](/blog/somvar-vrat-monday-fast).
- **White things given.** Rice, milk or white cloth given to someone in need.
- **Full moon evenings.** Many families keep a small pooja on pournami, the full moon; [a pournami pooja at home](/blog/pournami-pooja-at-home) describes one.
- **The pearl.** Some people wear a pearl, but tradition says to do this only on the advice of an astrologer who has read your chart.

Chandra's Navagraha temple is at Thingalur, a village near Thiruvaiyaru in Thanjavur district; the name itself comes from thingal, Tamil for moon. The Periya Puranam, the Tamil lives of the Shaiva saints, tells how the saint Appar came here to the home of his devotee Appoothi Adigal, whose son had died of a snakebite while fetching a banana leaf to serve the guest. Appar sang a hymn, and the boy woke. Devotees visit on Mondays and full moon days.

## Why does the moon affect the mind?

Whatever you believe about planets, look at what a Moon practice asks you to notice: the tides of sleep, appetite and mood. Most people have a pattern of worrying at night. Thoughts that seem manageable at noon become enormous at midnight. The mind is not more truthful after dark; it is only more tired, and less distracted.

The tradition responds to this with evening practices, water and quiet. That is not an accident.

Try this on Mondays for a month:

1. No screens after moonrise, or after dinner if you cannot see the moon.
2. Light a small lamp and sit with it for two minutes before bed.
3. Keep a glass of water at your bedside and drink a little before you lie down.
4. Lying on your back, take ten slow breaths, counting only the out-breaths.

That is the whole remedy. Notice how you sleep on Monday nights compared with the rest of the week.

## Letting the mind wax and wane

The Moon is never the same two nights running. It grows, fills, thins and disappears, and then comes back. No one calls the new moon a failure.

The mind is allowed to do the same. Some weeks you will be full and bright; some weeks you will feel like a thin crescent. The tradition did not ask the Moon to stay full. It gave it a place on Shiva's head and let it keep moving.

The Sun has its own day and its own practice: [Surya graha worship](/blog/surya-graha-worship). The Sun wakes the body. The Moon asks it to rest. Both are part of the day.
