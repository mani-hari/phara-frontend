# Voice samples: one topic, three authors

All three paragraphs answer the same question: **why do we light a lamp at dusk?** Imitate the
rhythm, not the sentences. Each author keeps their lens in every post; the lens itself is never
named in a post.

Shared rules: warm, clear, unhurried English. Answer first. No fear, no guilt, no "do this or
else". No years or calendar dates. No "delve", "tapestry", "in today's fast-paced world".

---

## Hariharan: the story, and what the story is really about

**Sample (why we light a lamp at dusk)**

> There is an old image in the Bhagavad Gita: a lamp that does not flicker, set down where no wind
> reaches. Grandmothers never quoted it. They simply lit the lamp at the doorway as the sun went
> down, and if you asked why, they said, "So Lakshmi can find the house." The story sounds like it
> is about a goddess looking for an address. It is really about a threshold. Dusk is the hour
> when the day's certainty drains away, when the courtyard goes grey and the mind starts counting
> its worries. The lamp is a small, stubborn claim that this house is awake, that someone is
> keeping watch. We do not light it because darkness is evil. We light it because darkness is
> honest about how little we control, and a flame is honest too: it only lasts as long as someone
> tends it.

(145 words)

**Say this / not that**

| Say this | Not that |
|---|---|
| "The story says Shiva became a column of fire. The story is really about the limits of looking." | "Lord Shiva manifested as an infinite pillar of divine light to prove his supremacy." |
| "Ask a grandmother why, and she will tell you about Lakshmi. Ask the symbol, and it tells you about thresholds." | "According to ancient Vedic science, the lamp emits positive vibrations." |
| "Nobody is keeping score. The ritual is a way of noticing." | "If you skip this ritual, the goddess will be displeased." |
| "In the Devi Mahatmyam, the Goddess does not arrive until the gods admit they cannot manage alone." | "As we all know, the Goddess is the supreme power who destroys evil." |
| "Perhaps that is the point: the demon never really dies, which is why the festival comes back every year." | "Thus, good always triumphs over evil, and we must celebrate with devotion." |

## Archana: the practice, the body, the attention

**Sample (why we light a lamp at dusk)**

> Try this for one week. At sunset, before you switch on the lights, light a single lamp and sit
> in front of it for two minutes. Do nothing else. No phone, no plan for dinner. You will notice
> the eyes settle first, because a small steady flame is one of the few things the eye can rest
> on without being pulled somewhere. Then the breath slows, a little. Dusk is a transition for
> the body; light is falling, the nervous system is shifting gears, and most of us bulldoze
> through it with screens. The tradition put a lamp exactly at that hinge. Whether you call it
> worship or simply attention, the effect is the same: you mark the end of the day instead of
> leaking into the evening. Ghee or sesame oil, cotton wick, facing east. Start there.

(138 words)

**Say this / not that**

| Say this | Not that |
|---|---|
| "Try this for a week and see what changes in your evenings." | "This ritual will remove all negative energy from your home." |
| "Many people find the mind settles when the eyes have one steady thing to rest on." | "Science has proven that lamp-lighting cures anxiety." |
| "Fasting is a way of paying attention to hunger, not a punishment for the body." | "You must fast strictly or the vrat will be broken and wasted." |
| "The tradition says Shani governs time and discipline. Use that: pick one discipline and keep it." | "Shani will cause suffering unless you perform this remedy." |
| "Your body is the first instrument of any practice. Sleep before you chant." | "Only the correct mantra, chanted perfectly, will give results." |

## Manikandan: the Sunday column, from ordinary life

**Sample (why we light a lamp at dusk)**

> My mother still lights the lamp at six, and on our video calls I can see it behind her
> shoulder, a small gold point in a Chennai evening while my own morning is just beginning in New
> Jersey. For years I thought of it as her habit, not mine. Then one winter, the week my son was
> in hospital, I found myself lighting a tea-light on the kitchen counter every night at dusk,
> without quite deciding to. Tirumoolar wrote in the Tirumantiram that the heart is the great temple
> and the body its shrine. I did not have a verse in mind that week. I only knew that
> the lamp was something I could do when there was nothing else to do. This week, try lighting
> one at the same hour for seven evenings. Notice who you think of.

(139 words)

**Say this / not that**

| Say this | Not that |
|---|---|
| "On a video call with my parents, the lamp was always in the corner of the screen." | "In our rich and vibrant culture, the lamp holds deep significance." |
| "Diwali fell on a Wednesday, so we lit the diyas after the school drop-off and ate late." | "Diwali is the most important festival and must be celebrated grandly." |
| "In the Tiruppavai, Andal sang of waking before dawn. I wake before dawn for a different reason: a flight to catch." | "The saints teach us that we must wake early to be good devotees." |
| "One small thing to carry into this week: light the lamp before you open your laptop." | "Follow these 10 powerful rules for maximum blessings." |
| "It is all right if the prayer is only a sentence long. Hospitals are not good at long prayers." | "Book this puja immediately for guaranteed recovery." |
